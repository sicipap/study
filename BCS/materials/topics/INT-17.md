# Myanmar & Rohingya

> **Why it matters:** The Rohingya crisis is Bangladesh's biggest foreign-policy issue and is asked in almost every exam: the 1982 Citizenship Law, the August 2017 exodus, Kutupalong and Bhasan Char, The Gambia's ICJ case, the ICC, the 2021 coup and the Arakan Army.

## Myanmar basics (মিয়ানমার)
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Capital | 🔥 **Naypyidaw** (Nay Pyi Taw), since **2005–06**; former capital **Yangon (Rangoon)** |
| Currency | **Kyat** |
| Parliament | **Pyidaungsu Hluttaw** |
| Independence | **4 January 1948** (from Britain); independence hero **General Aung San** |
| Name change | **Burma → Myanmar**, **1989** |
| Border with Bangladesh | 🔥 **271 km**; the **Naf River** forms part of it; Bangladeshi districts on the border: **Cox's Bazar, Bandarban, Rangamati** |
| Rohingya homeland | 🔥 **Rakhine State** (formerly **Arakan**); capital **Sittwe (Akyab)** |

- **Aung San Suu Kyi** (daughter of Aung San): **Nobel Peace Prize 1991**; led the **NLD**, which won the **2015** and **2020** elections.
- Myanmar joined **ASEAN** in **1997**; it is also a **BIMSTEC** member.

## Who are the Rohingya? (রোহিঙ্গা) 🔥
- A mainly **Muslim** ethnic minority of **northern Rakhine State**, with centuries of history there.
- 🔥 Myanmar's **Citizenship Law of 1982** does not list the Rohingya among the **135 recognised "national races"**, leaving them **stateless (রাষ্ট্রহীন)**.
- The UN has called them one of the **most persecuted minorities in the world**.

## Waves of displacement into Bangladesh 🔥
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1978** | About **200,000** fled (Operation "Nagamin"); most were later repatriated |
| **1991–92** | About **250,000** fled; most repatriated |
| October 2016 | Military crackdown after attacks on border posts |
| 🔥 **25 August 2017** | After **ARSA** attacks on police posts, the army began "**clearance operations**"; 🔥 **over 700,000** Rohingya fled to Bangladesh within months. The UN human-rights chief called it "**a textbook example of ethnic cleansing**" |

- Bangladesh now hosts about **1.1–1.2 million** Rohingya (as of 2025), mostly in **Ukhia and Teknaf** upazilas of **Cox's Bazar**.
- 🔥 **Kutupalong** is described as the **world's largest refugee camp**.
- 🔥 **Bhasan Char** (Noakhali, in the Bay of Bengal): Rohingya relocation began in **December 2020**; about **30,000+** have moved there.
- Bangladesh calls them "**Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMN)**"; Bangladesh is **not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention**.
- **Joint Response Plan** (with UNHCR, IOM) funds the camps; funding shortfalls have cut food rations.

## Diplomacy and repatriation
- 🔥 **Kofi Annan-led Advisory Commission on Rakhine State** (report **August 2017**): recommended citizenship reform and freedom of movement.
- 🔥 **Bangladesh–Myanmar repatriation arrangement: 23 November 2017**. Attempts to start repatriation (**November 2018, August 2019**) failed because no Rohingya agreed to return without **safety, citizenship and rights**.
- **UN Fact-Finding Mission (2018)** called for senior generals, including **Min Aung Hlaing**, to be investigated for **genocide**.
- **UN high-level conference on the Rohingya**, New York, **30 September 2025**.
- Bangladesh's position: **safe, voluntary, dignified and sustainable repatriation**.

## International justice 🔥
| Forum | Fact |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **ICJ** | **The Gambia v. Myanmar**, filed **11 November 2019** under the **Genocide Convention**, backed by the **OIC**. **Aung San Suu Kyi** defended Myanmar at The Hague (December 2019). 🔥 **Provisional measures ordered on 23 January 2020**. Myanmar's objections rejected (**July 2022**); the case is now on the merits |
| 🔥 **ICC** | Myanmar is not an ICC member, but **Bangladesh is**, so the ICC took jurisdiction over **deportation** into Bangladesh (2019). In **November 2024** the ICC Prosecutor applied for an **arrest warrant for Senior General Min Aung Hlaing** |
| **Argentina** | Under **universal jurisdiction**, an Argentine court issued arrest warrants for Myanmar leaders (2025) |

## Myanmar since the 2021 coup 🔥
- 🔥 **1 February 2021:** military coup led by **Senior General Min Aung Hlaing**; Aung San Suu Kyi detained; military body called the **State Administration Council**.
- Opposition: **National Unity Government (NUG)**, **People's Defence Forces**, and ethnic armed groups.
- **Operation 1027 (October 2023):** offensive by the "**Three Brotherhood Alliance**" (including the **Arakan Army**).
- 🔥 The **Arakan Army (AA)** took most of **Rakhine State**, including **Maungdaw (December 2024)**, and now controls Myanmar's **whole border with Bangladesh**. Fighting pushed more Rohingya (tens of thousands) into Bangladesh in 2024–25.
- **Earthquake of 28 March 2025** (magnitude 7.7, near **Mandalay**) killed thousands.
- The junta held phased elections from **December 2025**, widely rejected as not free.

## Quick revision
- Rohingya homeland: **Rakhine (Arakan)**; stateless under the **1982 Citizenship Law**.
- Mass exodus from **25 August 2017**; 700,000+ fled; total in Bangladesh ~**1.1–1.2 million**.
- **Kutupalong**: world's largest refugee camp; **Bhasan Char** relocation from **Dec 2020**.
- Repatriation deal: **23 November 2017** (never implemented).
- ICJ case by **The Gambia** (Nov 2019); provisional measures **23 Jan 2020**.
- ICC warrant sought for **Min Aung Hlaing** (Nov 2024).
- Coup: **1 February 2021**; Arakan Army controls the **271 km** border.
