# Shakespeare: comedies, histories, sonnets

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask which play a character belongs to (Shylock, Portia, Prospero, Falstaff, Puck), the classification of plays, famous comic lines, and facts about the 154 sonnets (Fair Youth, Dark Lady, Sonnet 18).

## How Shakespeare's plays are grouped
The **First Folio (1623)** divided the plays into **Comedies, Histories and Tragedies**. Critics later added **Romances (late plays)** and **Problem plays**.

## Comedies 🔥
| Play | Approx. date | Key characters / facts |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Comedy of Errors*** | c. 1592–94 | Two sets of identical twins; **shortest play** |
| ***The Taming of the Shrew*** | c. 1590–92 | **Petruchio** tames **Katherina** |
| ***A Midsummer Night's Dream*** 🔥 | c. **1595–96** | Fairies **Oberon, Titania, Puck**; **Bottom** (gets an ass's head); lovers in a wood near **Athens**. "**The course of true love never did run smooth.**" "Lord, what fools these mortals be!" (Puck) |
| ***The Merchant of Venice*** 🔥 | c. **1596–97** | **Shylock** (Jewish moneylender) demands a **pound of flesh** from **Antonio**; **Portia** disguised as a lawyer; **Bassanio**, **Jessica**. "**The quality of mercy is not strained**" (Portia). "All that glisters is not gold." |
| ***Much Ado About Nothing*** | c. 1598–99 | **Beatrice and Benedick**; **Dogberry** |
| ***As You Like It*** 🔥 | c. **1599** | **Rosalind** (disguised as Ganymede), **Orlando**, **Jaques**, the Forest of **Arden**. "**All the world's a stage, / And all the men and women merely players**" — the **seven ages of man** speech (Jaques) |
| ***Twelfth Night*** | c. **1601** | **Viola** (disguised as Cesario), **Orsino**, **Olivia**, **Malvolio**. "**If music be the food of love, play on**" (opening line). "Some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon 'em." |
| ***The Merry Wives of Windsor*** | c. 1597–1601 | **Falstaff** in love-plots |

- **Mature / joyous comedies:** *A Midsummer Night's Dream*, *The Merchant of Venice*, *Much Ado*, *As You Like It*, *Twelfth Night*.
- **Problem plays / dark comedies:** ***Measure for Measure***, ***All's Well That Ends Well***, ***Troilus and Cressida***.

## Romances (last plays)
| Play | Fact |
|---|---|
| ***The Tempest*** 🔥 (c. **1610–11**) | Usually considered his **last complete play** written alone. **Prospero** (magician, Duke of Milan), daughter **Miranda**, spirit **Ariel**, monster **Caliban**, **Ferdinand**. "**O brave new world, / That has such people in't!**" (Miranda). "We are such stuff / As dreams are made on." |
| ***The Winter's Tale*** | **Leontes**, **Hermione**, **Perdita**; stage direction "**Exit, pursued by a bear**" |
| ***Cymbeline***, ***Pericles*** | Romances of loss and reunion |

## History plays 🔥
Ten plays on English kings, mainly from **Holinshed's *Chronicles***.

| Play | Key fact |
|---|---|
| ***Henry VI*** (Parts 1–3) | His early plays; Wars of the Roses |
| ***Richard III*** | Villain-king; "**Now is the winter of our discontent**"; "**A horse! a horse! my kingdom for a horse!**" |
| ***Richard II*** | "This royal throne of kings, this sceptred isle… **this England**" (John of Gaunt) |
| ***Henry IV*** (Parts 1–2) | **Sir John Falstaff** (the fat comic knight), **Prince Hal**, **Hotspur**. "**Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown**" |
| ***Henry V*** | Battle of **Agincourt**; "**Once more unto the breach, dear friends**"; "We few, we happy few, we band of brothers" |
| ***King John***, ***Henry VIII*** | *Henry VIII* was written with **John Fletcher**; a cannon fired during it burned down **the Globe (1613)** |

- **Falstaff** is Shakespeare's most famous comic character (in *Henry IV* and *The Merry Wives of Windsor*).

## Sonnets 🔥
- **154 sonnets**, published in **1609** (by Thomas Thorpe), dedicated to the mysterious "**Mr. W. H.**".
- **Sonnets 1–126:** to the **Fair Youth** (a young man); **127–152:** to the **Dark Lady**; a **Rival Poet** also appears.
- **Shakespearean (English) sonnet:** **three quatrains + a couplet**, rhyme **abab cdcd efef gg**, in iambic pentameter.
- Famous sonnets:
  - **Sonnet 18:** "**Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?**"
  - **Sonnet 116:** "**Let me not to the marriage of true minds / Admit impediments.**"
  - **Sonnet 130:** "My mistress' eyes are nothing like the sun."
  - **Sonnet 29:** "When, in disgrace with fortune and men's eyes."
- **Long poems:** ***Venus and Adonis*** (1593), ***The Rape of Lucrece*** (1594), both dedicated to the **Earl of Southampton**.

## Quick revision
- 🔥 **Shylock, Portia, Antonio** — *The Merchant of Venice*.
- 🔥 **"All the world's a stage"** — *As You Like It* (Jaques).
- 🔥 **Prospero, Miranda, Ariel, Caliban** — *The Tempest*.
- **Puck, Oberon, Bottom** — *A Midsummer Night's Dream*.
- **Falstaff** — *Henry IV* and *The Merry Wives of Windsor*.
- "If music be the food of love" — *Twelfth Night*.
- **154 sonnets**, 1609; Sonnet 18 = "Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?"
- Shakespearean sonnet rhyme: **abab cdcd efef gg**.
