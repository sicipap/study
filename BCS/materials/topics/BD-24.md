# Administration: divisions, districts; CHT Accord 1997

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the number of divisions and districts, the newest divisions (Rangpur, Mymensingh), the largest and smallest district, border districts, the administrative hierarchy (DC, UNO), and the CHT Peace Accord (2 December 1997).

## Administrative hierarchy 🔥
| Level | Head |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / Division (Secretariat)** | Minister; **Secretary** (senior-most civil servant: **Cabinet Secretary**) |
| **বিভাগ (Division)** | **Divisional Commissioner (বিভাগীয় কমিশনার)** |
| **জেলা (District)** | 🔥 **Deputy Commissioner (DC, জেলা প্রশাসক)** — also District Magistrate and Collector |
| **উপজেলা (Upazila)** | 🔥 **Upazila Nirbahi Officer (UNO)** |
| **ইউনিয়ন (Union)** | Union Parishad (elected; see BD-23) |

- The civil service is the **Bangladesh Civil Service (BCS)**, recruited by the **PSC**; there are **26 cadres** (after BCS Economic merged with Administration in 2018).

## Divisions (বিভাগ) 🔥🔥
- 🔥 **8 divisions**, **64 districts**.

| Division | Created | Districts |
|---|---|---|
| **Dhaka** | Old (British period) | **13** (most) |
| **Chattogram** | Old | **11** |
| **Rajshahi** | Old | 8 |
| **Khulna** | 1960 | 10 |
| **Barishal** | **1993** | 6 |
| **Sylhet** | **1995** | 4 |
| 🔥 **Rangpur** | **2010** | 8 |
| 🔥 **Mymensingh** | **2015** — the **newest** division | 4 |
| **Total** | | **64** |

- The present **64 districts** were created in **1984** by upgrading the old sub-divisions (মহকুমা).
- Largest division by area: **Chattogram**; smallest: **Mymensingh**. Most districts: **Dhaka (13)**; fewest: **Sylhet and Mymensingh (4 each)**.

- **Enclave exchange:** under the **1974 Land Boundary Agreement** and its 2011 protocol, **111 Indian enclaves** came to Bangladesh and **51 Bangladeshi enclaves** went to India at midnight on **31 July 2015**.

## Chattogram Hill Tracts (CHT) background
- **Three hill districts**: **Rangamati, Khagrachhari, Bandarban**.
- Home to many ethnic communities; the **Chakma** are the largest group, followed by the Marma and Tripura.
- The **Kaptai dam** (1960s) flooded large areas and displaced many hill people.
- **Parbatya Chattagram Jana Samhati Samiti (PCJSS)** was formed in 1972; its armed wing **শান্তিবাহিনী** fought for autonomy for about two decades.

## CHT Peace Accord 1997 (পার্বত্য চট্টগ্রাম শান্তিচুক্তি) 🔥🔥
- 🔥 **Signed: 2 December 1997**, in Dhaka, during Sheikh Hasina's first government.
- Parties:
  - **Government side:** the **National Committee on CHT**, led by its convener **আবুল হাসনাত আবদুল্লাহ** (Chief Whip).
  - **Hill people's side:** **PCJSS**, led by **জ্যোতিরিন্দ্র বোধিপ্রিয় লারমা (সন্তু লারমা)**.
- No foreign third party was involved (it was a domestic agreement).
- Structure: **4 parts (খণ্ড)** with **72 articles/clauses** in total.
- 🔥 **Shanti Bahini surrendered arms on 10 February 1998** at **Khagrachhari** stadium.
- For this accord, **Sheikh Hasina** received **UNESCO's Félix Houphouët-Boigny Peace Prize (1998)**.

### Main outcomes of the accord
| Outcome | Year |
|---|---|
| **Ministry of Chattogram Hill Tracts Affairs** created | **1998** |
| **CHT Regional Council (আঞ্চলিক পরিষদ)** — Act 1998; chairman **Santu Larma** | 1998 (functioning from 1999) |
| Strengthening of the three **Hill District Councils** | 1998 |
| **Land Dispute Resolution Commission** | 1999 (Act 2001) |
| Withdrawal of temporary army camps; rehabilitation of refugees returning from India | Phased |

- The **UPDF** (United People's Democratic Front) was formed by a group of hill people who **opposed** the accord.

## Quick revision
- **8 divisions, 64 districts**; districts created in **1984**.
- Newest divisions: **Rangpur (2010)**, **Mymensingh (2015)**.
- Barishal **1993**, Sylhet **1995**.
- Most districts: **Dhaka (13)**.
- Largest district: **Rangamati**; smallest: **Narayanganj**.
- Myanmar border districts: **Rangamati, Bandarban, Cox's Bazar**.
- District head: **DC**; upazila head: **UNO**.
- CHT Accord: **2 December 1997**; Abul Hasnat Abdullah and **Santu Larma**.
- Arms surrender: **10 February 1998**, Khagrachhari.
- Enclave exchange: **31 July 2015** (111 to Bangladesh, 51 to India).
