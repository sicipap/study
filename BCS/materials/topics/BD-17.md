# Constitution: fundamental rights (Art. 26–47)

> **Why it matters:** "Which article guarantees …?" is one of the most frequent constitution questions. Learn the article → right table (27–44), Article 33 (24 hours), Article 44/102 (enforcement and writs), and the difference between Part II principles and Part III rights.

## Basics 🔥
- **Part III** of the Constitution: **Articles 26–47A**.
- The rights themselves are in 🔥 **Articles 27–44 — 18 fundamental rights** (the usual exam answer).
- Unlike the Part II principles, fundamental rights are **enforceable in court**: a person can go to the **High Court Division** under **Article 102** (right given by **Article 44**).
- 🔥 **Article 26:** any law **inconsistent with fundamental rights is void** to that extent; Parliament cannot make such a law.

## Key details asked 🔥
### Article 33 — arrest and detention
- An arrested person must be **told the grounds** of arrest and has the right to **consult and be defended by a lawyer** of his choice.
- 🔥 He must be produced before the **nearest magistrate within 24 hours** (excluding travel time), and cannot be kept longer without the magistrate's order.
- These safeguards do not apply to an **enemy alien** or a person held under **preventive detention**. Preventive detention beyond **six months** needs the report of an **Advisory Board**.

### Article 35 — trial and punishment
- No **ex post facto** punishment (no punishment under a law made after the act).
- No **double jeopardy** (not tried or punished twice for the same offence).
- Right to a **speedy and public trial** by an independent court.
- No person can be **compelled to be a witness against himself**.
- No **torture** or cruel, inhuman or degrading punishment.

### Article 39 — speech and press
- Freedom of thought and conscience is **guaranteed** (absolute).
- Freedom of speech and of the press is subject to **reasonable restrictions** by law: state security, friendly relations with foreign states, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence.

### Citizens only vs. all persons
- Most rights are for **citizens** (e.g. Art. 27, 36–40).
- Some protect **every person** in Bangladesh, including foreigners: e.g. **Art. 32** (life and liberty), **33** (arrest), **34** (forced labour), **35** (trial).

## Writs under Article 102 🔥
| Writ | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Habeas corpus** (বন্দী প্রদর্শন) | "Produce the body" — against unlawful detention |
| **Mandamus** (পরমাদেশ) | Order to a public authority to do its legal duty |
| **Prohibition** (নিষেধাজ্ঞা) | Stops a lower court/tribunal from exceeding jurisdiction |
| **Certiorari** (উৎপ্রেষণ) | Quashes an unlawful order or proceeding |
| **Quo warranto** (কারণ দর্শাও) | "By what authority?" — challenges an unlawful holder of public office |

## Rights during an emergency
- When an emergency is proclaimed (**Article 141A**):
  - 🔥 **Article 141B:** the freedoms in **Articles 36–40 and 42** are suspended for the emergency period.
  - **Article 141C:** the President may suspend the **right to move any court** to enforce the fundamental rights named in his order.

## Quick revision
- Part III: **Art. 26–47A**; fundamental rights **27–44 (18 rights)**.
- **Art. 26**: laws inconsistent with fundamental rights are void.
- **Art. 27** equality before law; **32** life and liberty; **39** speech and press.
- **Art. 33**: produce before a magistrate within **24 hours**.
- **Art. 35**: no double jeopardy, no self-incrimination, no torture.
- **Art. 41** religion; **42** property; **43** privacy of home and correspondence.
- **Art. 44** + **102**: enforcement through the **High Court Division** (writs).
- Foreign titles need the **President's** approval (**Art. 30**).
- Emergency: **Art. 141B** suspends Art. 36–40 and 42.

(a) Article 36 (b) Article 37 (c) Article 39 (d) Article 43

**3.** Within how many hours must an arrested person be produced before a magistrate?
(a) 12 hours (b) 24 hours (c) 48 hours (d) 72 hours

(a) Article 26 (b) Article 32 (c) Article 47A (d) Article 44

(a) Article 12 (b) Article 41 (c) Article 2A (d) Article 38

(a) Habeas corpus (b) Mandamus (c) Quo warranto (d) Certiorari

(a) Article 7 (b) Article 8 (c) Article 142 (d) Article 26
