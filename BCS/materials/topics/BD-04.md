# Mughal era, Baro Bhuiyans, Subedars, Nawabs

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask when Dhaka became capital (1610), who led the Baro Bhuiyans (Isa Khan), Shaista Khan's cheap rice and Lalbagh Fort, and who moved the capital to Murshidabad (Murshid Quli Khan).

## The Mughals reach Bengal
- **বাবর** founded the Mughal Empire after the **First Battle of Panipat (1526)**.
- **আকবর**'s army defeated **Daud Khan Karrani** at the **Battle of Rajmahal (1576)**. But local chiefs kept resisting, so full Mughal control came only under **জাহাঙ্গীর**.
- Akbar is credited with starting the **Bengali calendar (বঙ্গাব্দ)**, based on his **ফসলি সন**, with the help of **আমির ফতেহউল্লাহ সিরাজী**.

## Baro Bhuiyans (বারো ভূঁইয়া) 🔥
- Powerful landlords/chiefs of Bengal who resisted Mughal rule in the late 16th – early 17th century. "Baro" (twelve) means "many"; the number was not exactly twelve.
- 🔥 Leader: **ঈসা খাঁ**. Capital: **সোনারগাঁও**; he had a stronghold at **কাত্রাবো**. He fought **Man Singh**, Akbar's general, and took the title **"মসনদ-ই-আলা"**.
- After Isa Khan's death (1599), his son **মুসা খাঁ** led the resistance. He was finally defeated by Subedar **ইসলাম খাঁ** (1611).

| Bhuiyan | Area |
|---|---|
| **ঈসা খাঁ, মুসা খাঁ** | Sonargaon (Narayanganj) |
| **চাঁদ রায় ও কেদার রায়** | Bikrampur (Munshiganj) |
| **প্রতাপাদিত্য** | Jashore (Jessore) |
| **ফজল গাজী** | Bhawal (Gazipur) |
| **খাজা উসমান** | Bokainagar / Sylhet region (last Afghan chief) |

## Dhaka becomes the capital 🔥
- Subedar **ইসলাম খাঁ** moved the capital of **Suba Bangla** from **রাজমহল** to **ঢাকা** in **1610** (some sources: 1608).
- He named Dhaka **"জাহাঙ্গীরনগর"** after Emperor Jahangir.

## Important Subedars (সুবাদার)
| Subedar | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **ইসলাম খাঁ** | Defeated Musa Khan; capital to **Dhaka (1610)**; "Jahangirnagar" |
| **শাহ সুজা** (son of Shah Jahan) | **বড় কাটরা (1644)** built in his time; moved the capital back to **Rajmahal** for a time |
| **মীর জুমলা** | Campaigns in **Kamrup and Assam**; **Mir Jumla's cannon (মীর জুমলার কামান)**; built roads and bridges around Dhaka |
| 🔥 **শায়েস্তা খাঁ** | Twice Subedar (1664–1678, 1679–1688). **Rice at 8 maunds for 1 taka**. Drove the **Magh and Portuguese pirates** out of **Chattogram (1666)** and named it **"ইসলামাবাদ"**. Built **ছোট কাটরা**; completed buildings including **পরী বিবির মাজার** |
| **আজিম-উস-শান** (grandson of Aurangzeb) | Subedar at the end of the 17th century; **Murshid Quli Khan** served under him as Diwan |

### Lalbagh Fort (লালবাগ কেল্লা) 🔥
- Started in **1678** by Prince **মুহম্মদ আজম** (son of Aurangzeb). Its old name was **আওরঙ্গবাদ কেল্লা**.
- **Shaista Khan** continued it but left it **incomplete** after the death of his daughter **পরী বিবি**, whose tomb is inside the fort.

## Nawabi period (নবাবী আমল)
- 🔥 **মুর্শিদকুলী খান**: first Nawab of Bengal (practically independent while accepting the Mughal emperor). He moved the capital from **Dhaka to Murshidabad (মকসুদাবাদ)** in **1717** (the Diwani office moved earlier, around 1704). He introduced the **"মাল জামিনী"** revenue system.
- **সুজাউদ্দিন খান** and **সরফরাজ খান** followed.
- **আলীবর্দী খান** (1740–1756): seized power by defeating Sarfaraz Khan at the **Battle of Giria (1740)**. Bengal suffered **Maratha raids (বর্গির হাঙ্গামা)** in his time; the Maratha commander was **ভাস্কর পণ্ডিত**.
- **সিরাজউদ্দৌলা** (1756–1757): grandson of Alivardi; the **last independent Nawab of Bengal** (see BD-05).

## Quick revision
- Leader of the Baro Bhuiyans: **Isa Khan**; title **Masnad-i-Ala**; capital **Sonargaon**.
- Musa Khan was defeated by **Islam Khan**.
- Dhaka became capital in **1610** under **Islam Khan**; named **Jahangirnagar**.
- **Bara Katra**: Shah Shuja's time; **Chhoto Katra**: Shaista Khan.
- Rice 8 maunds per taka: **Shaista Khan**; Chattogram renamed **Islamabad**.
- Lalbagh Fort begun by **Prince Muhammad Azam** (1678); Pari Bibi's tomb inside.
- First Nawab: **Murshid Quli Khan**; capital to **Murshidabad** (1717).
- Maratha raids (bargi): **Alivardi Khan's** time.
- Last independent Nawab: **Siraj-ud-Daulah**.
