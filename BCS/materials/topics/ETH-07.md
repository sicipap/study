# Law, liberty & equality

> **Why it matters:** Law (আইন), liberty (স্বাধীনতা) and equality (সাম্য) are basic political concepts. Exams ask for definitions by famous writers, the sources and kinds of law, the kinds of liberty and equality, their relationship, and the related articles of the Constitution of Bangladesh.

## Law (আইন) 🔥
| Definition | By |
|---|---|
| "Law is the **command of the sovereign**." | **John Austin** (analytical / imperative school) |
| "A law is a general rule of **external human action** enforced by a sovereign political authority." | **T. E. Holland** |
| Law is established thought and habit that has gained formal recognition as uniform rules backed by the authority and power of government. | **Woodrow Wilson** |

### Sources of law (Holland's six) 🔥
**Custom** (the **oldest** source), **religion**, **judicial decisions**, **scientific commentaries**, **equity**, and **legislation** (the **most important modern** source).

### Kinds of law
- **National (municipal)** and **international** law.
- **Public law** (state–citizen: constitutional, administrative, criminal) and **private law** (citizen–citizen: contract, property).
- **Constitutional law** and **ordinary law**.

## Liberty (স্বাধীনতা) 🔥
- From the Latin **"liber"**, meaning free.
- **Negative liberty:** absence of restraint. **Positive liberty:** real opportunity to develop oneself.
| Definition | By |
|---|---|
| "Liberty is the eager maintenance of that atmosphere in which men have the opportunity to be their best selves." | **Harold Laski** (*A Grammar of Politics*) |
| "Liberty is the opposite of over-government." | **J. R. Seeley** |
| The only purpose for which power can rightly be used over a person against his will is to **prevent harm to others** (harm principle). | **J. S. Mill**, *On Liberty* (1859) |
| "Where there is no law, there is no freedom." | **John Locke** |

**Kinds of liberty:** natural, **civil (personal/social)**, **political** (vote, stand for election, criticise government), **economic** (freedom from want, right to work), **national** (independence), religious, moral.

**Safeguards of liberty:** democracy, a written constitution, **fundamental rights**, **rule of law**, **separation of powers**, **independent judiciary**, free press, decentralisation, and public vigilance. "**Eternal vigilance is the price of liberty**" is commonly attributed to Thomas Jefferson (its exact origin is disputed).

## Equality (সাম্য) 🔥
- "Equality means first of all the **absence of special privilege**." — **Harold Laski**. It also means **adequate opportunities** for all.
- **Kinds:** natural, **social**, **civil / legal** (equality before law), **political** (one person, one vote), **economic**, and equality of opportunity.

## Relationship between law, liberty and equality
| View | Supporters | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| Law is the **condition** of liberty | Locke, idealists, Laski | Without law, the strong would crush the weak |
| Law **limits** liberty | Individualists, anarchists | Every law reduces freedom |
| Liberty and equality are **opposed** | **Lord Acton**, de Tocqueville | "The passion for equality made vain the hope for freedom" (Acton) |
| Liberty and equality are **complementary** | **Laski, R. H. Tawney** (*Equality*, 1931), Rousseau | Liberty without equality benefits only the privileged |

The slogan of the **French Revolution (1789)**: **"Liberty, Equality, Fraternity"**.

## In the Constitution of Bangladesh 🔥
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **19** | **Equality of opportunity** (Fundamental Principle of State Policy) |
| **26–47A (Part III)** | **Fundamental rights** |
| **27** | Equality before law and equal protection of law |
| **28** | **No discrimination** on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth; women have equal rights in all spheres of state and public life |
| **29** | Equality of opportunity in **public employment** |
| **32** | Protection of **right to life and personal liberty** |
| **36 / 37 / 38** | Freedom of movement / assembly / association |
| **39** | Freedom of **thought and conscience, and of speech** (and of the press) |
| **41** | Freedom of religion |

> 🔄 Update: article numbers refer to the Constitution as it stood in 2024; check for amendments made in the later reform process.

## Quick revision
- Austin: law = command of the sovereign.
- Custom is the oldest source of law; legislation is the most important modern source.
- Liberty ← Latin "liber"; Laski: liberty = "atmosphere … to be their best selves".
- Laski: equality = absence of special privilege.
- Mill, *On Liberty* (1859): harm principle.
- Locke: "Where there is no law, there is no freedom."
- Article 27 equality before law; 28 non-discrimination; 29 public employment; 32 life and liberty; 39 speech.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** "Law is the command of the sovereign" was said by:
(a) John Austin (b) T. E. Holland (c) Harold Laski (d) J. S. Mill

**2.** Which is the oldest source of law?
(a) Legislation (b) Judicial decisions (c) Custom (d) Scientific commentaries

**3.** "Equality means first of all the absence of special privilege" was said by:
(a) Lord Acton (b) J. R. Seeley (c) Aristotle (d) Harold Laski

**4.** The word "liberty" comes from the Latin word:
(a) liber (b) lex (c) libra (d) lux

**5.** "Where there is no law, there is no freedom" was said by:
(a) Hobbes (b) John Locke (c) Rousseau (d) Austin

**6.** Which article of the Constitution of Bangladesh protects the right to life and personal liberty?
(a) Article 27 (b) Article 39 (c) Article 32 (d) Article 29

**7.** The slogan "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity" comes from:
(a) the American Revolution (b) the Russian Revolution (c) the Glorious Revolution (d) the French Revolution

**8.** *On Liberty* (1859) was written by:
(a) Jeremy Bentham (b) J. S. Mill (c) Harold Laski (d) John Locke

**9.** Which article of the Constitution of Bangladesh guarantees equal opportunity in public employment?
(a) Article 29 (b) Article 36 (c) Article 41 (d) Article 22

| 2 | c | Custom is the oldest source; legislation is the most important modern one. |
| 3 | d | Laski, A Grammar of Politics. |
| 4 | a | "Liber" means free. |
| 5 | b | Locke, Second Treatise of Government. |
| 6 | c | Article 32: protection of right to life and personal liberty. |
