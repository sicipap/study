# Victorian novel: Dickens, Brontës, Hardy, George Eliot

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask for the author of a novel or character (Oliver Twist, Pip, Heathcliff, Tess, Maggie Tulliver), pen names (Boz, Currer/Ellis/Acton Bell, George Eliot), Hardy's Wessex, and famous first lines.

## The Victorian novel
- The **novel** was the dominant form of the Victorian age; many were **serialised** in magazines.
- Themes: poverty, industrial cities, class, education, women's position, faith and doubt.
- Other novelists: **W. M. Thackeray** — ***Vanity Fair*** (1847–48), heroine **Becky Sharp**, subtitle "A Novel without a Hero"; **Anthony Trollope** — *Barsetshire* novels; **Elizabeth Gaskell** — *Mary Barton*, *Cranford*, *North and South*; **Wilkie Collins** — ***The Moonstone*** (1868), often called the first full-length English detective novel; **Lewis Carroll** — ***Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*** (1865); **R. L. Stevenson** — *Treasure Island*, *Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde* (1886).

## Charles Dickens (1812–1870) 🔥
- The most popular Victorian novelist; pen name **"Boz"** (*Sketches by Boz*, 1836). Worked in a **blacking factory** as a child when his father was in a debtors' prison.
- Famous for **social criticism**, humour, memorable **caricatures**, orphan heroes.

| Novel | Year | Characters / facts |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Pickwick Papers*** | **1836–37** | First novel; **Mr Pickwick**, **Sam Weller** |
| ***Oliver Twist*** 🔥 | **1837–39** | Orphan **Oliver** ("**Please, sir, I want some more**"); **Fagin**, **the Artful Dodger**, **Bill Sikes**, **Nancy**, **Mr Bumble** |
| ***Nicholas Nickleby*** | 1838–39 | **Dotheboys Hall**, Wackford Squeers |
| ***The Old Curiosity Shop*** | 1840–41 | **Little Nell**, Quilp |
| ***A Christmas Carol*** 🔥 | **1843** | Miser **Ebenezer Scrooge** ("Bah! Humbug!"), **Tiny Tim**, Bob Cratchit |
| ***David Copperfield*** 🔥 | **1849–50** | **Most autobiographical**; Dickens's "favourite child"; **Mr Micawber**, **Uriah Heep**, **Betsey Trotwood**, Steerforth, Agnes |
| ***Bleak House*** | 1852–53 | Court case **Jarndyce and Jarndyce** |
| ***Hard Times*** | **1854** | **Mr Gradgrind** ("facts"), **Coketown**; attack on utilitarianism |
| ***Little Dorrit*** | 1855–57 | Marshalsea debtors' prison |
| ***A Tale of Two Cities*** 🔥 | **1859** | London and Paris in the **French Revolution**; **Sydney Carton**, **Charles Darnay**, **Madame Defarge**. First line: "**It was the best of times, it was the worst of times**" |
| ***Great Expectations*** 🔥 | **1860–61** | **Pip**, **Miss Havisham**, **Estella**, **Joe Gargery**, convict **Magwitch** |
| ***Our Mutual Friend*** | 1864–65 | Last completed novel |
| ***The Mystery of Edwin Drood*** | 1870 | **Unfinished** at his death |

## The Brontë sisters 🔥
Daughters of a clergyman at **Haworth**, Yorkshire. They first published under male pen names.

| Sister | Pen name | Novels |
|---|---|---|
| **Charlotte Brontë** (1816–1855) | **Currer Bell** | ***Jane Eyre*** (**1847**) 🔥 — orphan governess **Jane** and **Mr Rochester** of **Thornfield Hall**; his mad wife **Bertha Mason** in the attic; "**Reader, I married him.**" Also *Shirley*, ***Villette***, *The Professor* |
| **Emily Brontë** (1818–1848) | **Ellis Bell** | ***Wuthering Heights*** (**1847**) 🔥 — her only novel; **Heathcliff** and **Catherine Earnshaw**; houses **Wuthering Heights** and **Thrushcross Grange**; narrators **Lockwood** and **Nelly Dean**; "**I am Heathcliff**" |
| **Anne Brontë** (1820–1849) | **Acton Bell** | ***Agnes Grey*** (1847), ***The Tenant of Wildfell Hall*** (1848) |

## George Eliot (Mary Ann Evans, 1819–1880) 🔥
- **"George Eliot" is the pen name of Mary Ann (Marian) Evans** — a woman using a male name. Partner: **G. H. Lewes**.
- Known for **psychological realism** and moral seriousness; many novels are set in the English **Midlands**.

| Novel | Year | Characters / facts |
|---|---|---|
| ***Adam Bede*** | **1859** | First full novel; Adam, Hetty Sorrel, Dinah Morris |
| ***The Mill on the Floss*** 🔥 | **1860** | **Maggie Tulliver** and her brother **Tom**; they drown together in a flood |
| ***Silas Marner*** 🔥 | **1861** | Weaver of **Raveloe** whose gold is stolen; he adopts the child **Eppie** |
| ***Romola*** | 1863 | Set in Renaissance **Florence** |
| ***Middlemarch*** 🔥 | **1871–72** | Subtitle "**A Study of Provincial Life**"; **Dorothea Brooke**, **Casaubon**, **Lydgate**; often called one of the greatest English novels |
| ***Daniel Deronda*** | 1876 | Last novel |

## Thomas Hardy (1840–1928) 🔥
- Novelist and poet; trained as an **architect**. Set his novels in **"Wessex"**, a fictional version of **south-west England (Dorset)**.
- Themes: **fate, chance, cosmic irony**, pessimism; the "**President of the Immortals**".
- After the hostile reception of *Jude*, he gave up novels and wrote only **poetry** (*Wessex Poems*, *The Dynasts*, "The Darkling Thrush").

| ***The Return of the Native*** | **1878** | **Egdon Heath**; Clym Yeobright, Eustacia Vye |
| ***The Mayor of Casterbridge*** 🔥 | **1886** | **Michael Henchard** sells his wife and daughter at a fair when drunk |

| ***Jude the Obscure*** | **1895** | **Jude Fawley**, **Sue Bridehead**; his last novel |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 **Boz** = Dickens; ***David Copperfield*** is his most autobiographical novel.
- 🔥 "**It was the best of times, it was the worst of times**" — ***A Tale of Two Cities***.
- **Pip, Miss Havisham** — *Great Expectations*; **Scrooge** — *A Christmas Carol*.
- 🔥 ***Jane Eyre*** = Charlotte (**Currer Bell**); ***Wuthering Heights*** = Emily (**Ellis Bell**), **Heathcliff**.
- 🔥 **George Eliot = Mary Ann Evans**; ***Middlemarch***, ***Silas Marner***, ***The Mill on the Floss***.
- 🔥 Hardy's **Wessex**; ***Tess*** subtitle "**A Pure Woman**".
- ***Vanity Fair*** — **Thackeray**, "A Novel without a Hero".
