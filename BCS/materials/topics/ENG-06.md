# Elizabethan: Spenser, Marlowe

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask about *The Faerie Queene* (Gloriana, Redcrosse Knight, Spenserian stanza), Spenser's epithet "Poet's poet", Marlowe's *Doctor Faustus*, "Marlowe's mighty line", and the University Wits.

## The Elizabethan Age (1558–1603)
- Reign of **Queen Elizabeth I**; defeat of the **Spanish Armada (1588)** gave national pride.
- Called the **Golden Age of English literature** (especially drama).
- **Wyatt and Surrey** had brought the **sonnet** into English; their poems appeared in ***Tottel's Miscellany* (1557)**. **Surrey** first used **blank verse** in English (translation of Virgil's *Aeneid*).
- **First English comedy:** ***Ralph Roister Doister*** (Nicholas Udall). **First English tragedy:** ***Gorboduc*** (1561, Sackville and Norton), also the first English play in blank verse.

## Edmund Spenser (c. 1552–1599) 🔥
### Epithets
- **"The Poet's Poet"** — given by **Charles Lamb** 🔥
- **"Prince of poets"** in his own time (the phrase used on his tomb)
- He called Chaucer "**Well of English undefiled**" and saw himself as Chaucer's follower. Buried in **Westminster Abbey** near Chaucer.

### Works
| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Shepheardes Calender*** | **1579** | 12 pastoral eclogues, one for each month; his first major work |
| ***The Faerie Queene*** 🔥 | **1590** (Books I–III), **1596** (IV–VI) | Allegorical epic; planned 12 books, finished **6** (plus the *Mutability Cantos*) |
| ***Amoretti*** | **1595** | Sonnet sequence (88/89 sonnets) to his wife Elizabeth Boyle |
| ***Epithalamion*** | **1595** | Wedding song for his own marriage |
| ***Prothalamion*** | **1596** | Refrain: **"Sweet Thames, run softly, till I end my song"** |
| *Astrophel* | 1595 | Elegy on Sir Philip Sidney |
| *A View of the Present State of Ireland* | prose | |

### The Faerie Queene 🔥
- The **Faerie Queene = Gloriana**, who represents **Queen Elizabeth I**. The poem is dedicated to her.
- Hero of the whole poem: **Prince Arthur** (Magnificence).
- Each book shows one virtue through a knight:

| Book | Virtue | Knight |
|---|---|---|
| I | **Holiness** | **Redcrosse Knight** (St George), helped by **Una** (truth); enemies **Duessa**, **Archimago** |
| II | Temperance | Sir Guyon |
| III | Chastity | Britomart (a lady knight) |
| IV | Friendship | Cambell and Triamond |
| V | Justice | Artegall |
| VI | Courtesy | Calidore |

- Written in the **Spenserian stanza** 🔥: **9 lines**, 8 lines of iambic pentameter + a final **alexandrine** (iambic hexameter), rhyme **ababbcbcc**. Later used by Byron (*Childe Harold*), Keats (*The Eve of St Agnes*) and Shelley (*Adonais*).
- **Spenserian sonnet** rhyme: abab bcbc cdcd ee (linked quatrains).

## Christopher Marlowe (1564–1593) 🔥
### Epithets and facts
- The greatest of the **University Wits**; born in **Canterbury**, the same year as Shakespeare.
- **"Father of English tragedy"** (common exam answer).
- He made **blank verse** powerful on stage: **Ben Jonson** praised **"Marlowe's mighty line"**.
- Killed in a tavern fight at **Deptford** in **1593**, aged **29**.

### Works
| Work | Approx. year | Main character / fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***Tamburlaine the Great*** (Parts 1 and 2) | c. **1587** | A Scythian shepherd who becomes a world conqueror |
| ***Doctor Faustus*** 🔥 | c. **1592** (printed 1604) | Faustus sells his soul to **Lucifer** through **Mephistopheles** for **24 years** of power |
| ***The Jew of Malta*** | c. **1589** | **Barabas**, a greedy, revengeful Jew |
| ***Edward II*** | c. **1592** | History play; the weak king and his favourite **Gaveston** |
| *Dido, Queen of Carthage* | | with Nashe |
| ***Hero and Leander*** | poem | Completed by George Chapman |
| ***"The Passionate Shepherd to His Love"*** | poem | **"Come live with me and be my love"** |

### Famous lines from *Doctor Faustus* 🔥
- **"Was this the face that launched a thousand ships, / And burnt the topless towers of Ilium?"** (about **Helen of Troy**)
- "Sweet Helen, make me immortal with a kiss."
- "Why this is hell, nor am I out of it." (Mephistopheles)
- Marlowe's heroes show the Renaissance "**overreacher**": unlimited desire for power (Tamburlaine), wealth (Barabas) and knowledge (Faustus).

## The University Wits
Young playwrights educated at **Oxford or Cambridge** who prepared the way for Shakespeare.

| Wit | Famous work |
|---|---|
| **Christopher Marlowe** | *Doctor Faustus* |
| **Thomas Kyd** | ***The Spanish Tragedy*** — model of the **revenge tragedy** |
| **Robert Greene** | *Friar Bacon and Friar Bungay*; called Shakespeare an "**upstart crow**" |
| **John Lyly** | ***Euphues*** (gave us "euphuism", an ornate style) |
| **George Peele** | *The Old Wives' Tale* |
| **Thomas Lodge**, **Thomas Nashe** | *Rosalynde* (source of *As You Like It*); *The Unfortunate Traveller* |

## Other Elizabethans
| Writer | Work / fact |
|---|---|
| **Sir Philip Sidney** | *Arcadia*; *Astrophel and Stella* (sonnets); ***An Apology for Poetry*** (*The Defence of Poesy*) |
| **Francis Bacon** | ***Essays* (1597)** — **"Father of English essay"**; "**Some books are to be tasted, others to be swallowed, and some few to be chewed and digested**" (*Of Studies*) |
| **Sir Walter Raleigh** | *The History of the World* |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 Spenser = **"Poet's poet"** (Charles Lamb).
- 🔥 ***The Faerie Queene***: Gloriana = **Queen Elizabeth**; Book I = **Holiness, Redcrosse Knight**.
- 🔥 **Spenserian stanza**: 9 lines, **ababbcbcc**, last line an **alexandrine**.
- Marlowe: **Doctor Faustus**, **"Marlowe's mighty line"** (Ben Jonson).
- **"Was this the face that launched a thousand ships"** — *Doctor Faustus*, about Helen.
- Kyd's ***The Spanish Tragedy*** = revenge tragedy model.
- **Bacon** = father of the English essay.

**8.** For how many years does Faustus sell his soul?
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 7 (d) 30
