# Circle: chord, tangent, arc, angles

> **Why it matters:** Circle theorems (বৃত্ত) are regular BCS and bank questions: the longest chord, angle in a semicircle, angle at the centre vs the circumference, cyclic quadrilaterals, chord distance and tangent length.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Radius (ব্যাসার্ধ)** | centre to any point on the circle |
| **Diameter (ব্যাস)** | chord through the centre = 2 × radius; 🔥 the **longest chord** |
| **Chord (জ্যা)** | segment joining two points on the circle |
| **Arc (চাপ)** | part of the circumference; minor and major arcs |
| **Sector (বৃত্তকলা)** | region between two radii and an arc |
| **Segment (বৃত্তাংশ)** | region between a chord and an arc |
| **Tangent (স্পর্শক)** | line touching the circle at exactly **one** point |
| **Secant (ছেদক)** | line cutting the circle at two points |
| **Concentric circles (এককেন্দ্রিক)** | same centre, different radii |

## Chord theorems 🔥
- The **perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord**. So (half chord)² + (distance)² = r².
- **Equal chords are equidistant** from the centre; a chord nearer the centre is longer.
- Exactly **one circle** passes through three non-collinear points.

## Angle theorems 🔥
- **Angle at the centre = 2 × angle at the circumference** on the same arc.
- **Angles in the same segment are equal.**
- 🔥 **Angle in a semicircle = 90°.**
- **Cyclic quadrilateral (বৃত্তস্থ চতুর্ভুজ):** opposite angles add to **180°**; an exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle.

## Tangent theorems 🔥
- A tangent is **perpendicular to the radius** at the point of contact.
- From an external point, exactly **two** tangents can be drawn, and they are **equal in length**.
- Tangent length from a point at distance d from the centre = **√(d² − r²)**.
- If two tangents from P touch at A and B, then ∠APB + ∠AOB = 180°.

## Formula and shortcut table
| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Chord length at distance d from centre | 2√(r² − d²) |
| Distance of chord of length c from centre | √(r² − (c/2)²) |
| Tangent length | √(d² − r²) |
| Angle at circumference | half the central angle |
| Cyclic quadrilateral | opposite angles sum to 180° |

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** A chord is 3 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. Find its length.
Steps: Half chord = √(25 − 9) = 4. Chord = 8.
**Answer: 8 cm**

**Example 2.** An arc subtends 110° at the centre. What angle does it subtend at the circumference?
Steps: 110°/2 = 55°.
**Answer: 55°**

**Example 3.** One angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is 75°. Find the opposite angle.
Steps: 180° − 75° = 105°.
**Answer: 105°**

**Example 4.** A point is 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the length of the tangent from it.
Steps: √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12.
**Answer: 12 cm**

**Example 5.** A chord of length 24 cm is in a circle of radius 13 cm. How far is it from the centre?
Steps: √(169 − 144) = √25 = 5.
**Answer: 5 cm**

## Common traps
- Using the full chord instead of **half** the chord in r² = (c/2)² + d².
- Angle at the circumference is **half** the central angle, not double.
- Only **opposite** angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°; adjacent ones need not.
- The tangent meets the **radius** at 90°, not the chord.

## Quick revision
- Diameter is the longest chord.
- Perpendicular from centre bisects a chord.
- Central angle = 2 × inscribed angle; angle in semicircle = 90°.
- Cyclic quadrilateral: opposite angles = 180°.
- Tangent ⟂ radius; two equal tangents from an outside point; length √(d² − r²).

## Practice MCQ
**1.** The longest chord of a circle is the:
(a) radius (b) diameter (c) tangent (d) arc

**2.** The angle in a semicircle is:
(a) 45° (b) 180° (c) 90° (d) 60°

**3.** A chord of length 16 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 10 cm. Its distance from the centre is:
(a) 8 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 12.8 cm (d) 6 cm

**4.** An arc subtends 140° at the centre. The angle it subtends at any point on the remaining part of the circle is:
(a) 70° (b) 140° (c) 280° (d) 40°

**5.** One angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is 110°. The opposite angle is:
(a) 110° (b) 90° (c) 250° (d) 70°

**6.** The angle between a tangent and the radius at the point of contact is:
(a) 90° (b) 45° (c) 180° (d) 60°

**7.** How many tangents can be drawn to a circle from a point outside it?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) infinitely many

**8.** A point is 17 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 8 cm. The length of the tangent from the point is:
(a) 9 cm (b) 25 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 12 cm

**9.** How many circles can pass through three non-collinear points?
(a) none (b) two (c) infinitely many (d) one

**10.** Two tangents PA and PB are drawn to a circle with centre O. If ∠APB = 60°, then ∠AOB = ?
(a) 120° (b) 60° (c) 90° (d) 30°

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | The diameter passes through the centre and is the longest chord |
| 2 | c | Angle in a semicircle = 90° |
| 3 | d | √(100 − 64) = 6 |
| 4 | a | Half of the central angle: 70° |
| 5 | d | 180° − 110° = 70° |
| 6 | a | Tangent ⟂ radius |
| 7 | b | Exactly two tangents from an external point |
| 8 | c | √(289 − 64) = √225 = 15 |
| 9 | d | Exactly one circle through three non-collinear points |
| 10 | a | ∠APB + ∠AOB = 180° → 120° |
