# Periods of English literature

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask which period a writer belongs to, the name and dates of an age, and who the age is named after ("Age of Chaucer", "Age of Pope", "Age of Johnson").

## The periods at a glance 🔥
Dates are the ones used in most exam guides; historians round them slightly differently.

| Period | Approx. dates | Key writers / works |
|---|---|---|
| **Old English (Anglo-Saxon) Period** | **450–1066** | ***Beowulf***, Caedmon, Cynewulf, Bede, King Alfred |
| **Middle English Period** | **1066–1500** | **Chaucer**, Langland (*Piers Plowman*), Gower, *Sir Gawain and the Green Knight*, Malory |
| — **Age of Chaucer** | **1340–1400** | *The Canterbury Tales* |
| **Renaissance Period** | **1500–1660** | Includes the Elizabethan, Jacobean and Caroline ages |
| — **Elizabethan Age** | **1558–1603** | **Shakespeare, Spenser, Marlowe**, Sidney, Bacon |
| — **Jacobean Age** | **1603–1625** | **Ben Jonson, Donne**, Webster; **King James Bible (1611)** |
| — **Caroline Age** | **1625–1649** | Cavalier poets (Herrick, Lovelace, Suckling), metaphysical poets |
| — **Commonwealth / Puritan Age** | **1649–1660** | **Milton** (prose), Marvell, Hobbes |
| **Restoration Period** | **1660–1700** | **Dryden** (Age of Dryden), Bunyan, Congreve, Milton's *Paradise Lost* (1667) |
| **Neo-classical / Augustan Age** | **1700–1745** | **Pope, Swift, Addison, Steele, Defoe** (Age of Pope) |
| **Age of Johnson / Age of Sensibility** | **1745–1798** | **Dr. Johnson, Goldsmith, Richardson, Fielding**, Gray, Burke |
| **Romantic Period** | **1798–1832** | **Wordsworth, Coleridge, Byron, Shelley, Keats**, Scott, Jane Austen, Lamb |
| **Victorian Period** | **1832–1901** | **Tennyson, Browning, Arnold, Dickens, the Brontës, George Eliot, Hardy** |
| **Modern Period** | **1901–1939** (to c. 1945) | **Yeats, T. S. Eliot, Joyce, Woolf, Lawrence, Conrad, Shaw** |
| **Post-modern / Contemporary** | **1945–present** | Beckett, Osborne, Larkin, Ted Hughes, Golding |

## Key turning points 🔥
- **1066**: Norman Conquest → end of Old English, start of Middle English (French influence).
- **1476**: **William Caxton** set up the **first printing press in England** (Westminster). He printed *The Canterbury Tales* and Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur* (1485).
- **1558**: Queen Elizabeth I comes to the throne.
- **1642**: theatres closed by the Puritans; reopened in **1660** at the Restoration of Charles II.
- **1798**: ***Lyrical Ballads*** by **Wordsworth and Coleridge** → start of the Romantic Movement.
- **1832**: First **Reform Act**, and death of Sir Walter Scott → end of the Romantic age.
- **1837**: Queen Victoria crowned; **1901**: she dies.

## Other names you must know
- **Augustan Age**: the early 18th century, named after Roman emperor Augustus, whose age produced Virgil, Horace and Ovid. Writers copied classical models, so it is also called **Neo-classical**.
- **Age of Reason / Age of Prose and Reason**: 18th century (reason over emotion).
- **Golden Age of English literature / drama**: **Elizabethan Age**.
- **Age of Transition**: the later 18th century (Gray, Collins, Cowper, Blake, Burns), which prepared the way for Romanticism. Poets of this group are called **pre-Romantics**.
- **Renaissance** = "rebirth" (of classical learning). It began in **Italy** (14th century) and reached England in the 16th century.

## Main features by period
| Period | Features |
|---|---|
| Old English | Alliterative verse, heroic epic, Christian and pagan elements |
| Middle English | Romance, allegory, French influence, Chaucer's realism |
| Elizabethan | Drama at its peak, sonnet, blank verse, patriotism |
| Restoration | Comedy of manners, satire, heroic couplet, reason |
| Augustan | Satire, periodical essay (*The Tatler*, *The Spectator*), rise of the novel |
| Romantic | Nature, imagination, emotion, the common man, the supernatural |
| Victorian | Social problems, science vs faith, realism, the novel dominant |
| Modern | Stream of consciousness, free verse, alienation, experiment |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 Old English: **450–1066**; Middle English: **1066–1500**.
- 🔥 Elizabethan Age: **1558–1603**; Romantic Age: **1798–1832**; Victorian Age: **1832–1901**.
- Romanticism started with ***Lyrical Ballads* (1798)**.
- **Caxton** brought printing to England in **1476**.
- **Age of Pope** = Augustan / Neo-classical age.
- Restoration = **1660**, when Charles II returned; the age of **Dryden**.
- **Beowulf** is the chief Old English work.
