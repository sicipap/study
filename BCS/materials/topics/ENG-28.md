# Modern: Joyce, Woolf, Lawrence, Conrad, Orwell

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask "Who wrote X?", "X is a character of which novel?", and the technique a writer is known for, such as stream of consciousness or political allegory. The modern period is roughly **1900–1945**.

## Features of the modern period
- Break with Victorian certainty after **World War I**; loss of faith, alienation, fragmentation.
- 🔥 **Stream of consciousness**: showing the flow of a character's thoughts. The term was first used by psychologist **William James**. Main novelists: **James Joyce, Virginia Woolf, Dorothy Richardson**.
- **Interior monologue**, experiments with time, myth, symbol.
- Other modern names: **T. S. Eliot** (*The Waste Land*, 1922), **W. B. Yeats**, **E. M. Forster** (*A Passage to India*, 1924), **Aldous Huxley** (*Brave New World*, 1932).

## James Joyce (1882–1941), Irish 🔥
| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| ***Dubliners*** | 1914 | 15 short stories; "paralysis" of Dublin life; last story "The Dead" |
| ***A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man*** | 1916 | Autobiographical; hero **Stephen Dedalus** |
| ***Ulysses*** 🔥 | **1922** | Modelled on Homer's *Odyssey*; set in Dublin on **16 June 1904** ("**Bloomsday**"); **Leopold Bloom**, **Molly Bloom**, Stephen Dedalus; ends with Molly's unpunctuated monologue |
| ***Finnegans Wake*** | 1939 | Most experimental; dream language |
| *Exiles* (play), *Chamber Music* (poems) | 1918, 1907 | Minor works |

- Joyce used **epiphany**: a sudden moment of spiritual insight.
- *Ulysses* is often called the greatest novel of the 20th century; it was banned for obscenity in the USA and UK for years.

## Virginia Woolf (1882–1941), English 🔥
- Leading member of the **Bloomsbury Group**; with her husband Leonard Woolf she ran the **Hogarth Press**.
- She drowned herself in the **River Ouse** in 1941.

| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| *The Voyage Out* | 1915 | First novel |
| *Jacob's Room* | 1922 | |
| ***Mrs Dalloway*** 🔥 | **1925** | One day in London; **Clarissa Dalloway** and shell-shocked **Septimus Warren Smith** |
| ***To the Lighthouse*** 🔥 | **1927** | **Mrs Ramsay**, painter **Lily Briscoe**; three parts: "The Window", "Time Passes", "The Lighthouse" |
| *Orlando* | 1928 | Hero lives for centuries and changes sex |
| ***A Room of One's Own*** | **1929** | Feminist essay: "A woman must have money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction." Invents **Judith Shakespeare** |
| *The Waves* | 1931 | Six voices in soliloquies |
| *Between the Acts* | 1941 | Last novel |

- Essay "Modern Fiction" attacks the "materialists" (Wells, Bennett, Galsworthy).

## D. H. Lawrence (1885–1930), English
- Son of a Nottinghamshire coal miner; wrote about sex, instinct and industrial society.

| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| *The White Peacock* | 1911 | First novel |
| ***Sons and Lovers*** 🔥 | **1913** | Autobiographical; **Paul Morel**, mother **Gertrude Morel**, **Miriam**, **Clara**; mother–son bond (Oedipal theme) |
| ***The Rainbow*** | 1915 | Three generations of the **Brangwen** family; banned for obscenity |
| ***Women in Love*** | 1920 | Sisters **Ursula** and **Gudrun Brangwen** |
| ***Lady Chatterley's Lover*** | **1928** | **Constance (Connie) Chatterley** and gamekeeper **Oliver Mellors**; full text published in the UK only after the **1960** Penguin obscenity trial |
| Poems | | "**Snake**", "**Piano**", "**Bavarian Gentians**" |

## Joseph Conrad (1857–1924), Polish-born British 🔥
- Real name **Józef Teodor Konrad Korzeniowski**. He was a sailor and learned English as an adult, yet became a master of English prose.
- Narrator **Charles Marlow** appears in *Heart of Darkness*, *Lord Jim*, *Youth* and *Chance*.

| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| *Almayer's Folly* | 1895 | First novel |
| *The Nigger of the 'Narcissus'* | 1897 | Preface: "to make you see" |
| ***Heart of Darkness*** 🔥 | **1899** (book 1902) | Marlow's voyage up the **Congo** river to find **Kurtz**, an ivory trader; Kurtz's last words: "**The horror! The horror!**" |
| ***Lord Jim*** | 1900 | Jim abandons the ship *Patna*; lifelong search for redemption |
| ***Nostromo*** | 1904 | Imaginary South American republic Costaguana; silver mine |
| *The Secret Agent* | 1907 | Anarchists in London; **Verloc** |
| *Under Western Eyes* | 1911 | Russian revolutionaries |
| *Victory* | 1915 | |

- **Chinua Achebe** called Conrad "a thoroughgoing racist" in his lecture "**An Image of Africa**" (1975).

## George Orwell (1903–1950), English 🔥🔥
- Real name **Eric Arthur Blair**; born at **Motihari, Bihar, India**. Served in the Indian Imperial Police in **Burma**.

| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| *Down and Out in Paris and London* | 1933 | First book; poverty |
| *Burmese Days* | 1934 | Colonial Burma |
| *The Road to Wigan Pier* | 1937 | Working-class life in northern England |
| *Homage to Catalonia* | 1938 | Spanish Civil War |
| ***Animal Farm*** 🔥 | **1945** | Subtitle "**A Fairy Story**"; allegory of the **Russian Revolution** and Stalinism |
| ***Nineteen Eighty-Four*** 🔥 | **1949** | Dystopia; state **Oceania**; ruler **Big Brother** |
| Essays | | "**Shooting an Elephant**", "Politics and the English Language", "Why I Write" |

**Animal Farm:** **Old Major** (idea of rebellion; Marx/Lenin), **Napoleon** (Stalin), **Snowball** (Trotsky), **Squealer** (propaganda), **Boxer** (loyal cart-horse; motto "I will work harder"), **Benjamin** (cynical donkey), **Mollie**, **Mr Jones** (the farmer).
- "**All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.**"
- "Four legs good, two legs bad."

**Nineteen Eighty-Four:** **Winston Smith**, **Julia**, **O'Brien**, **Emmanuel Goldstein**; **Thought Police**, **Newspeak**, **doublethink**, **Room 101**, Ministry of Truth.
- "**Big Brother is watching you.**"
- Party slogans: "**War is peace. Freedom is slavery. Ignorance is strength.**"
- The adjective **"Orwellian"** means a totalitarian, surveillance-driven state.

## Quick revision
- Stream of consciousness: **Joyce, Woolf**; term from **William James**.
- *Ulysses* (1922) = **Leopold Bloom**, one day, **16 June 1904**.
- *Mrs Dalloway* (1925), *To the Lighthouse* (1927), *A Room of One's Own* (1929): **Woolf**.
- *Sons and Lovers* (1913), **Paul Morel**: **Lawrence**.
- *Heart of Darkness*: **Marlow, Kurtz, Congo**; "The horror! The horror!"
- Conrad's real name: **Józef Teodor Konrad Korzeniowski**.
- Orwell = **Eric Arthur Blair**, born in **India** (Motihari).
- *Animal Farm* (1945) = allegory of the **Russian Revolution**; *1984* (1949) = **Big Brother**.

**7.** *A Room of One's Own* is a feminist essay by:
(a) George Eliot (b) Jane Austen (c) Charlotte Brontë (d) Virginia Woolf

**8.** Which of these is NOT a novel by Joseph Conrad?
(a) Nostromo (b) Women in Love (c) Lord Jim (d) The Secret Agent

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |

| 1 | b | Leopold Bloom's day in Dublin, 16 June 1904 |
| 2 | d | Joyce and Woolf are the chief stream-of-consciousness novelists |
| 3 | a | Lawrence's autobiographical novel of 1913 |
| 4 | c | Kurtz in *Heart of Darkness* |
| 5 | c | Orwell = Eric Arthur Blair |
| 6 | a | Napoleon = Stalin, Snowball = Trotsky |
| 7 | d | Woolf, 1929 |
| 8 | b | *Women in Love* is by D. H. Lawrence |
