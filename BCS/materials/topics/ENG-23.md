# Voice change

> **Why it matters:** Voice-change questions give an active sentence and ask for the correct passive (or the reverse). Tricky types: questions, imperatives, "let", two objects, "known to", "It is said that", and verbs with prepositions.

## Basics
- **Active voice:** the subject **does** the action — "Rahim **wrote** a letter."
- **Passive voice:** the subject **receives** the action — "A letter **was written** by Rahim."
- Formula: **Object → subject + be (in the right tense) + past participle (V3) + by + agent**.
- Only **transitive verbs** (with an object) can be changed into the passive. "He sleeps" has no passive.

## Pronoun changes
| Active | I | we | you | he | she | they | it | who |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Passive (by …)** | me | us | you | him | her | them | it | **whom** |

## Tense table 🔥
| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple | He **writes** a letter. | A letter **is written** by him. |
| Present continuous | He **is writing** a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| Present perfect | He **has written** a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| Past simple | He **wrote** a letter. | A letter **was written** by him. |
| Past continuous | He **was writing** a letter. | A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Past perfect | He **had written** a letter. | A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Future simple | He **will write** a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |
| Future perfect | He **will have written** a letter. | A letter **will have been written** by him. |
| Modal | He **can / must / should write** a letter. | A letter **can / must / should be written** by him. |
| going to | He **is going to write** a letter. | A letter **is going to be written** by him. |

- **Perfect continuous tenses and the future continuous** are normally **not changed** into the passive.

## Special cases 🔥
### 1. Interrogative sentences
| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Did** he **write** the letter? | **Was** the letter **written** by him? |
| **Do** you **know** him? | **Is** he **known to** you? |
| **Who** wrote this? | **By whom was** this **written**? |
| **What** are you doing? | **What is being done** by you? |
| **Why** did he beat the dog? | **Why was** the dog **beaten** by him? |

### 2. Imperative sentences — use **Let**
| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| **Shut** the door. | **Let the door be shut.** |
| **Do not** insult the poor. | **Let not** the poor **be insulted**. / Let the poor not be insulted. |
| **Help** me. | **Let me be helped.** |
| **Please help** me. / Kindly lend me your pen. | **You are requested to** help me. |
| **Obey** your parents. (advice) | **You are advised to** obey your parents. |
| **Leave** the room. (order) | **You are ordered to** leave the room. |
| **Let** him do the work. | **Let the work be done by him.** |

### 3. Two objects
"The teacher **gave me** a book." →
- "**I was given** a book by the teacher." (personal object as subject — preferred)
- "**A book was given (to) me** by the teacher."

### 4. Verbs that don't take "by" 🔥
| Verb | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **know** | **to** | "He is **known to** me." (not *by me*) |
| **please / satisfy / disappoint** | **with** | "I am **pleased with** your work." |
| **surprise / astonish** | **at** | "I was **surprised at** his behaviour." |
| **interest** | **in** | "He is **interested in** music." |
| **fill** | **with** | "The glass **is filled with** water." |
| **cover** | **with** | "The field is **covered with** snow." |
| **marry** | **to** | "She **was married to** a doctor." |
| **contain** | **in** | "Sugar **is contained in** the box." |
| **vex / annoy** | **with / at** | "I am **annoyed with** him." |

### 5. Verb + preposition (phrasal)
Keep the preposition: "They **laughed at** the beggar." → "The beggar **was laughed at** by them." "We must **look after** the old." → "The old **must be looked after**."

### 6. "It is said / believed / known that"
"People **say** that he is honest." →
- "**It is said that** he is honest." or
- "He **is said to be** honest."
"People believe that he was a spy." → "He **is believed to have been** a spy."

### 7. Omit "by + agent" when it is unimportant or unknown
"Someone **has stolen** my pen." → "My pen **has been stolen**." "They **speak English** all over the world." → "English **is spoken** all over the world."

### 8. Passive → active (reverse)
"The work **is being done** by them." → "They **are doing** the work." "By whom was the glass broken?" → "**Who broke** the glass?"

## Common mistakes
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| He is known by me. | He is known **to** me. |
| Let the door is shut. | Let the door **be** shut. |
| By who was it done? | By **whom** was it done? |
| The beggar was laughed by them. | The beggar was laughed **at** by them. |
| A letter is wrote by him. | A letter is **written** by him. |
| I am pleased by your work. | I am pleased **with** your work. |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 Passive = **be + V3**; continuous = **being + V3**; perfect = **been + V3**.
- 🔥 Imperative → **Let + object + be + V3**; requests → "**You are requested to**".
- 🔥 **known to, pleased with, surprised at, interested in, filled with, married to**.
- **Who → By whom**.
- Prepositions of phrasal verbs remain (**laughed at**, **looked after**).
- Intransitive verbs have **no passive**.
- "People say …" → "**It is said that …**" / "He **is said to be** …".

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Passive of "He is writing a letter" is —
(a) A letter is written by him. (b) A letter was being written by him. (c) A letter is being written by him. (d) A letter has been written by him.

**2.** Passive of "Shut the door" is —
(a) Let the door be shut. (b) The door is shut. (c) Let the door shut. (d) The door should shut.

**3.** "I know him." The passive is —
(a) He is known by me. (b) He is known to me. (c) He was known to me. (d) He is knowing to me.

**4.** Passive of "Who broke the glass?" is —
(a) Who was the glass broken by? (b) By who was the glass broken? (c) The glass was broken by whom? (d) By whom was the glass broken?

**5.** Passive of "They laughed at the old man" is —
(a) The old man was laughed by them. (b) The old man was laughed at by them. (c) The old man laughed at them. (d) The old man is laughed at by them.

**6.** Passive of "Please lend me your book" is —
(a) You are requested to lend me your book. (b) Let your book be lent me. (c) Your book is lent to me. (d) You are ordered to lend me your book.

**7.** "His conduct surprised me." The passive is —
(a) I was surprised to his conduct. (b) I am surprised at his conduct. (c) I was surprised at his conduct. (d) I surprised at his conduct.

**8.** Which sentence cannot be changed into the passive voice?
(a) The baby is sleeping. (b) He opened the door. (c) She sings a song. (d) They built a house.

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | Present continuous: is being + V3 |
| 2 | a | Imperative: Let + object + be + V3 |
| 3 | b | know takes "to" in the passive |
| 4 | d | Who becomes By whom |
| 5 | b | Keep the preposition "at" |
| 6 | a | Requests use "You are requested to" |
| 7 | c | Past tense, and surprise takes "at" |
| 8 | a | "sleep" is intransitive, no object |
