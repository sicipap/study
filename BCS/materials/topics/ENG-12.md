# Ben Jonson, John Donne (Metaphysical), Milton

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask about Jonson's comedy of humours (*Volpone*), the term "metaphysical poets" (who coined it), Donne's famous lines, and Milton's epics (*Paradise Lost*, number of books, Satan, "to justify the ways of God to men").

## Ben Jonson (1572–1637)
- Friend and rival of Shakespeare; the **first Poet Laureate** in practice (royal pension, 1616) — the first *official* Poet Laureate was **Dryden (1668)**.
- Father of the **Comedy of Humours** 🔥 (characters dominated by one "humour" or trait).
- Leader of the "**Tribe of Ben**" / "**Sons of Ben**" (Cavalier poets like Herrick followed him).
- Wrote **masques** for the court.
- Tribute to Shakespeare: "**Sweet Swan of Avon**", "**He was not of an age, but for all time!**"

## Metaphysical poetry 🔥
- The term was used by **Dryden** (Donne "affects the metaphysics") and made popular by **Dr. Samuel Johnson** in his ***Life of Cowley*** (*Lives of the Poets*). Johnson is usually credited with coining "metaphysical poets".
- Features: **wit**, **conceits** (far-fetched comparisons), argument, colloquial openings, learned images from science and philosophy.
- Johnson's criticism: "**The most heterogeneous ideas are yoked by violence together.**"
- **T. S. Eliot** revived them (essay "The Metaphysical Poets", 1921; "**dissociation of sensibility**").
- Poets: **John Donne** (leader), **George Herbert**, **Andrew Marvell**, **Henry Vaughan**, **Richard Crashaw**, **Abraham Cowley**.

## John Donne (1572–1631) 🔥
- **Father / leader of metaphysical poetry**. Became **Dean of St Paul's Cathedral** (1621).

| Work | Fact / famous line |
|---|---|

| "**A Valediction: Forbidding Mourning**" | Famous **compass conceit** (lovers as the two legs of a compass) |
| "**The Flea**" | Lovers' blood mixed in a flea |
| "**Go and catch a falling star**" (*Song*) | |
| ***Holy Sonnets*** | "**Death, be not proud**" (Holy Sonnet 10); "**Batter my heart, three-person'd God**" |

Other metaphysicals: **Marvell** — "**To His Coy Mistress**" ("Had we but world enough, and time"); **Herbert** — *The Temple* ("The Collar", "Virtue"); **Vaughan** — *Silex Scintillans* ("The Retreat").

## John Milton (1608–1674) 🔥🔥
- Puritan poet; secretary (Latin Secretary) to **Oliver Cromwell's** government.
- Became **totally blind by 1652**; he **dictated *Paradise Lost***.
- Epithets: "**The organ voice of England**" (Tennyson called him "God-gifted organ-voice of England"); the **"Epic poet of England"**; Wordsworth: "**Milton! thou shouldst be living at this hour**" (sonnet *London, 1802*).

| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***On the Morning of Christ's Nativity*** | 1629 | Early ode |
| ***L'Allegro*** and ***Il Penseroso*** | c. 1631 | Twin poems: the cheerful man and the thoughtful man |
| ***Comus*** | 1634 | A masque |
| ***Lycidas*** 🔥 | **1637** | **Pastoral elegy** on his friend **Edward King**, drowned at sea |
| ***Areopagitica*** 🔥 | **1644** | Prose; defence of **freedom of the press**: "**A good book is the precious life-blood of a master spirit**" |
| ***Paradise Lost*** 🔥 | **1667** (10 books); **1674** (**12 books**) | Epic in **blank verse** on the Fall of Man |
| ***Paradise Regained*** | **1671** | 4 books; Christ resists Satan's temptation |
| ***Samson Agonistes*** | **1671** | Closet tragedy on the Greek model |
| Sonnet "**On His Blindness**" | c. 1652–55 | "**They also serve who only stand and wait.**" |

### Paradise Lost 🔥
- Theme: **the Fall of Man** — **Adam and Eve** disobey God by eating the forbidden fruit; **Satan** (tempts Eve in the form of a **serpent**).
- Aim: "**to justify the ways of God to men**" (Book I).
- Opening: "**Of Man's first disobedience, and the fruit / Of that forbidden tree…**"
- Satan's lines: "**Better to reign in Hell, than serve in Heaven.**" — "**The mind is its own place, and in itself / Can make a Heaven of Hell, a Hell of Heaven.**"
- Satan is often called the **real hero** (Blake: Milton was "of the Devil's party without knowing it").
- **Pandemonium** — capital of Hell (Milton coined the word).
- Written in **blank verse** (unrhymed iambic pentameter); style is called the **"grand style"**.

## Quick revision
- 🔥 Ben Jonson: **comedy of humours**, ***Volpone***, *The Alchemist*.
- 🔥 **Dr. Johnson** named the metaphysical poets (in *Life of Cowley*).
- 🔥 Donne: **father of metaphysical poetry**; "**No man is an island**"; "**Death, be not proud**".
- Compass conceit — "A Valediction: Forbidding Mourning".
- 🔥 *Paradise Lost*: **12 books** (1674 edition), **blank verse**, "to justify the ways of God to men".
- "They also serve who only stand and wait" — **On His Blindness**.
- ***Lycidas*** = pastoral elegy; ***Areopagitica*** = freedom of the press.
