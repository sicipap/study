# Geometric progression & series

> **Why it matters:** Geometric progression (গুণোত্তর ধারা) questions — nth term, sum, infinite sum, geometric mean — and standard series sums (squares, cubes) appear in BCS and bank papers, usually as one quick question.

## Key concepts
- A **geometric progression (GP)** is a sequence in which each term is the previous term multiplied by a **fixed number**, the **common ratio (সাধারণ অনুপাত), r**.
  - 2, 6, 18, 54, … (a = 2, r = 3)
  - 81, 27, 9, … (r = 1/3)
- r = any term ÷ its previous term.
- **Geometric mean (গুণোত্তর গড়)** of a and b = **√(ab)**. Three numbers in GP can be taken as a/r, a, ar.
- **Infinite GP:** a sum to infinity exists only if **−1 < r < 1**. Then the terms get smaller and smaller.
- A **series (ধারা)** is the sum of the terms of a sequence (অনুক্রম).

## Formula and shortcut table 🔥
| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| 🔥 nth term | **tₙ = a × rⁿ⁻¹** |
| Sum of n terms (r > 1) | Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) |
| Sum of n terms (r < 1) | Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) |
| 🔥 Sum to infinity (−1 < r < 1) | **S∞ = a/(1 − r)** |
| Geometric mean of a and b | √(ab) |
| Given tₚ and t_q | r^(q − p) = t_q / tₚ |
| Sum 1 + 2 + … + n | n(n + 1)/2 |
| Sum of squares 1² + … + n² | n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6 |
| Sum of cubes 1³ + … + n³ | [n(n + 1)/2]² |
| For positive a, b | AM ≥ GM ≥ HM |

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Find the 8th term of 2, 6, 18, …
Steps: a = 2, r = 3. t₈ = 2 × 3⁷ = 2 × 2187 = 4374.
**Answer: 4374**

**Example 2.** Find the sum of the first 6 terms of 1, 2, 4, …
Steps: 1 × (2⁶ − 1)/(2 − 1) = 63.
**Answer: 63**

**Example 3.** Find the sum to infinity of 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + …
Steps: a = 1, r = 1/2. S∞ = 1/(1 − 1/2) = 2.
**Answer: 2**

**Example 4.** Which term of 3, 6, 12, … is 384?
Steps: 3 × 2ⁿ⁻¹ = 384 → 2ⁿ⁻¹ = 128 = 2⁷ → n = 8.
**Answer: 8th term**

**Example 5.** Show that 0.333… = 1/3 using a GP.
Steps: 0.3 + 0.03 + 0.003 + … : a = 0.3, r = 0.1. S∞ = 0.3/0.9 = 1/3.
**Answer: 1/3**

**Example 6.** Find 1² + 2² + … + 10².
Steps: 10 × 11 × 21/6 = 385.
**Answer: 385**

## Common traps
- nth term uses the power **n − 1**, not n.
- Sum to infinity works only for −1 < r < 1; for r = 2 the sum grows without limit.
- With a negative ratio (1 − 1/2 + 1/4 − …) r = −1/2, so S∞ = 1/(1 + 1/2) = 2/3.
- Geometric mean of 4 and 9 is 6, not 6.5 (that is the arithmetic mean).

## Quick revision
- tₙ = arⁿ⁻¹.
- Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1).
- S∞ = a/(1 − r), only when −1 < r < 1.
- GM = √(ab).
- Σn² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6; Σn³ = [n(n + 1)/2]².

## Practice MCQ
**1.** The common ratio of 81, 27, 9, … is:
(a) 3 (b) −3 (c) 1/3 (d) 1/9

**2.** The 6th term of 3, 6, 12, … is:
(a) 96 (b) 192 (c) 48 (d) 36

**3.** 1 + 3 + 9 + … + 243 = ?
(a) 363 (b) 364 (c) 729 (d) 365

**4.** The sum to infinity of 9 + 3 + 1 + … is:
(a) 13 (b) 12 (c) 27 (d) 13.5

**5.** The geometric mean of 2 and 32 is:
(a) 17 (b) 16 (c) 8 (d) 64

**6.** Which term of 2, 4, 8, … is 512?
(a) 8th (b) 9th (c) 10th (d) 256th

**7.** 1² + 2² + 3² + … + 10² = ?
(a) 385 (b) 55 (c) 3025 (d) 505

**8.** 1³ + 2³ + 3³ + … + 10³ = ?
(a) 385 (b) 1000 (c) 3025 (d) 5050

**9.** The sum to infinity of 1 − 1/2 + 1/4 − 1/8 + … is:
(a) 2 (b) 2/3 (c) 1/2 (d) 3/2

**10.** In a GP, the 3rd term is 12 and the 6th term is 96. The first term is:
(a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 6 (d) 3

## Answer key

| 4 | d | 9/(1 − 1/3) = 13.5 |
| 5 | c | √(2 × 32) = 8 |
| 6 | b | 2 × 2ⁿ⁻¹ = 512 → 2ⁿ = 512 = 2⁹ |
