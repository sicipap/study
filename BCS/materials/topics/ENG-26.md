# Modern: Yeats, T. S. Eliot

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask about *The Waste Land* (1922), Eliot's Nobel Prize (1948), "April is the cruellest month", "objective correlative", Yeats's Nobel (1923), "The Second Coming", "Sailing to Byzantium" and the Irish Literary Revival.

## Modernism (c. 1901–1939)
- A break with Victorian tradition after the shock of **World War I**: fragmentation, alienation, loss of faith, **free verse**, allusion, myth, **stream of consciousness** in fiction.
- **Imagism** (c. 1912–17): clear, precise images — **Ezra Pound** ("In a Station of the Metro"), H. D., Amy Lowell. Pound's motto: "**Make it new.**"
- **War poets:** **Wilfred Owen** ("Dulce et Decorum Est", "Anthem for Doomed Youth"; "**My subject is War, and the pity of War**"), **Siegfried Sassoon**, **Rupert Brooke** ("The Soldier": "some corner of a foreign field / That is for ever England").
- **Georgian poets:** Walter de la Mare, John Masefield ("Sea-Fever"), W. H. Davies ("Leisure": "What is this life if, full of care, / We have no time to stand and stare").

## W. B. Yeats (1865–1939) 🔥
- **Irish** poet and dramatist; won the **Nobel Prize in Literature in 1923** 🔥 — the first Irish writer to win it.
- Leader of the **Irish Literary Revival (Celtic Revival)**; co-founded the **Abbey Theatre, Dublin (1904)** with **Lady Gregory**.
- Became a **Senator** of the Irish Free State (1922).
- Loved the Irish revolutionary **Maud Gonne**, who refused his proposals.
- Interested in mysticism and the occult; his system is in ***A Vision*** (1925): history moves in **gyres** (cycles of about 2,000 years).
- Widely regarded as one of the **greatest 20th-century poets in English**; his style moved from dreamy Celtic romanticism to hard, modern symbolism.

## T. S. Eliot (1888–1965) 🔥
- Born in **St Louis, USA**; settled in England and became a **British citizen in 1927** (same year he joined the Church of England).
- Won the **Nobel Prize in Literature in 1948** 🔥 and the **Order of Merit**.
- Worked at Lloyds Bank, then as an editor at **Faber and Faber**; edited the journal *The Criterion*.
- The most influential **poet-critic of modernism**; his essays shaped the later "New Criticism".

### Eliot's criticism 🔥
| Essay / book | Key idea |
|---|---|
| "**Tradition and the Individual Talent**" (**1919**) | "**Impersonal theory of poetry**": "Poetry is not a turning loose of emotion, but an **escape from emotion**"; the poet as a **catalyst** (platinum) |
| "**Hamlet and His Problems**" (1919) | "**Objective correlative**"; he called *Hamlet* "most certainly an artistic failure" |
| "**The Metaphysical Poets**" (1921) | "**Dissociation of sensibility**" after the 17th century |
| ***The Sacred Wood*** (1920) | His first collection of essays |

- Eliot described himself as "**classicist in literature, royalist in politics, and Anglo-Catholic in religion**".

## Quick revision
- 🔥 **Yeats** — Irish, **Nobel 1923**, Abbey Theatre, "**Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold**" (*The Second Coming*).
- "**A terrible beauty is born**" — *Easter, 1916*; "**That is no country for old men**" — *Sailing to Byzantium*.
- 🔥 **Eliot** — ***The Waste Land* (1922)**, **Nobel 1948**, "**April is the cruellest month**".
- *The Waste Land* dedicated to **Ezra Pound**; ends "**Shantih shantih shantih**".
- "**Not with a bang but a whimper**" — *The Hollow Men*.
- ***Murder in the Cathedral*** — Becket; **objective correlative** — Eliot.
- **Prufrock**: "measured out my life with coffee spoons".
