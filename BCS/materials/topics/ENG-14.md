# Restoration & Neo-classical: Dryden, Pope, Swift, Defoe

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask about the first Poet Laureate (Dryden), Pope's mock-epic *The Rape of the Lock*, Swift's *Gulliver's Travels* (Lilliput, Brobdingnag, Houyhnhnms), *Robinson Crusoe* (first English novel), and famous lines like "To err is human".

## Background
- **Restoration (1660):** Charles II returns; theatres reopen; women act on stage for the first time.
- **Neo-classical / Augustan Age (1700–1745):** writers imitate Greek and Roman models; value **reason, order, wit, correctness**; favourite form is the **heroic couplet**; favourite mode is **satire**.
- **Comedy of manners** (Restoration comedy): **William Congreve** — ***The Way of the World*** (1700), *Love for Love*; **Wycherley** — *The Country Wife*.
- **John Bunyan** — ***The Pilgrim's Progress*** (1678), allegory; hero **Christian** travels from the City of Destruction to the Celestial City.
- **Periodical essay:** **Richard Steele** — ***The Tatler*** (1709); **Joseph Addison and Steele** — ***The Spectator*** (1711), with **Sir Roger de Coverley**.

## John Dryden (1631–1700) 🔥
- **First official Poet Laureate of England (1668)**.
- Epithets: **"Father of English criticism"** (Dr. Johnson), dominant figure of the **Age of Dryden**.
- He called Chaucer the "**Father of English poetry**" and said of *The Canterbury Tales*: "**Here is God's plenty**."

| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***Absalom and Achitophel*** 🔥 | **1681** | Political **satire** in heroic couplets (Achitophel = Earl of Shaftesbury) |
| ***MacFlecknoe*** | 1682 | Mock-heroic satire on the poet **Shadwell** |
| ***An Essay of Dramatic Poesy*** | **1668** | Critical dialogue (Neander = Dryden) |
| ***All for Love*** | **1677** | Tragedy on **Antony and Cleopatra** in blank verse |
| *Annus Mirabilis* | 1667 | "Year of wonders" (1666: war and Great Fire) |
| *The Hind and the Panther* | 1687 | Religious allegory |
| *Alexander's Feast* | 1697 | Ode |

## Alexander Pope (1688–1744) 🔥
- Greatest poet of the Augustan age (**Age of Pope**); master of the **heroic couplet**; a Catholic, physically small and ill.
- Translated Homer's ***Iliad*** and ***Odyssey***.

| Work | Year | Fact / famous line |
|---|---|---|
| ***An Essay on Criticism*** | **1711** | 🔥 "**To err is human, to forgive divine.**" — "**A little learning is a dangerous thing.**" — "**Fools rush in where angels fear to tread.**" |
| ***The Rape of the Lock*** 🔥 | **1712/1714** | **Mock-epic**; Lord Petre cuts a lock of **Belinda's** (Arabella Fermor's) hair |
| ***The Dunciad*** | 1728 | Mock-epic satire on dull writers ("dunces") |
| ***An Essay on Man*** | **1733–34** | 🔥 "**The proper study of mankind is Man.**" — "**Hope springs eternal in the human breast.**" — "Whatever is, is right." |
| *Epistle to Dr Arbuthnot* | 1735 | Satire on **Addison** as "Atticus" |
| *Ode on Solitude* | | "Happy the man whose wish and care / A few paternal acres bound" |

## Jonathan Swift (1667–1745) 🔥
- Irish (Anglo-Irish) satirist; **Dean of St Patrick's Cathedral, Dublin**.
- The **greatest prose satirist** in English.

| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***Gulliver's Travels*** 🔥 | **1726** | Satire on mankind; hero **Lemuel Gulliver**, a ship's surgeon |
| ***A Tale of a Tub*** | 1704 | Satire on religious corruption |
| ***The Battle of the Books*** | 1704 | Ancients vs Moderns ("**sweetness and light**" — bee's image; later used by Arnold) |
| ***A Modest Proposal*** | **1729** | Ironic proposal that the poor Irish sell their babies as food |
| *Journal to Stella* | | Letters to Esther Johnson ("Stella") |

### Gulliver's four voyages 🔥
| Part | Land | People |
|---|---|---|
| I | **Lilliput** | Tiny people (**6 inches**); Lilliput vs **Blefuscu**; Big-Endians and Little-Endians |
| II | **Brobdingnag** | **Giants** |
| III | **Laputa** (flying island), Balnibarbi, Glubbdubdrib, Luggnagg | Impractical scientists; the immortal **Struldbrugs** |
| IV | **Land of the Houyhnhnms** | Rational **horses (Houyhnhnms)**; brutish human-like **Yahoos** |

## Daniel Defoe (c. 1660–1731) 🔥
- Journalist and novelist; some books call him the "Father of the English novel", but most exam guides give that title to **Henry Fielding** (see the 18th-century novel note).
- ***Robinson Crusoe*** (**1719**) is often called the **first English novel** 🔥. Crusoe is shipwrecked on an island for **28 years**; his servant is **Friday**. Based partly on the real sailor **Alexander Selkirk**.

| Work | Year |
|---|---|
| ***Robinson Crusoe*** | **1719** |
| *Captain Singleton* | 1720 |
| ***Moll Flanders*** | **1722** |
| ***A Journal of the Plague Year*** | 1722 |
| *Roxana* | 1724 |
| *The Shortest Way with the Dissenters* | 1702 (satire; he was pilloried) |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 **Dryden** = first Poet Laureate (1668), "Father of English criticism".
- 🔥 ***The Rape of the Lock*** (Pope) = mock-epic; heroine **Belinda**.
- 🔥 "**To err is human, to forgive divine**" and "**A little learning is a dangerous thing**" — Pope, *An Essay on Criticism*.
- 🔥 ***Gulliver's Travels* (1726)**: **Lilliput** (tiny), **Brobdingnag** (giants), **Houyhnhnms** (horses), **Yahoos**.
- ***Robinson Crusoe* (1719)** — Defoe; servant **Friday**.
- *The Pilgrim's Progress* — **Bunyan**; *The Way of the World* — **Congreve**.
- *The Spectator* — **Addison & Steele**.
