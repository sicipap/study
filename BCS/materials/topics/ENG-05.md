# Adjective & degrees of comparison

> **Why it matters:** Exams test irregular comparatives (later/latter, farther/further, elder/older), "superior to" not "than", Latin comparatives, double comparatives, and "one of the + superlative + plural noun".

## Kinds of adjective
| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Quality (descriptive)** | good, brave, honest, Bangladeshi |
| **Quantity** | much, little, some, enough, whole, all |
| **Number** | one, first, few, many, several, each, every |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those, such |
| **Interrogative** | which, what, whose (+ noun) |
| **Possessive** | my, your, his, their |
| **Proper** | Bangladeshi, English, Shakespearean |

- **Attributive** use: before the noun ("a **red** rose"). **Predicative** use: after the verb ("The rose is **red**").
- Some adjectives are **only predicative**: **afraid, asleep, alive, alone, awake, aware** ("The child is asleep", not "an asleep child").

## Few / a few / the few, little / a little / the little 🔥
| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **few** (countable) | hardly any (negative) | **Few** men are honest. |
| **a few** | some, a small number (positive) | I have **a few** friends. |
| **the few** | all the (small number) there are | **The few** books he had were lost. |
| **little** (uncountable) | hardly any | There is **little** hope. |
| **a little** | some | There is **a little** milk. |
| **the little** | all the (small amount) there is | **The little** money he had was stolen. |

- **many** + countable; **much** + uncountable. **fewer** + countable (fewer people); **less** + uncountable (less water).

## Degrees of comparison
| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| happy | happier | happiest |

### Irregular forms 🔥
| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| good / well | **better** | **best** |
| bad / ill | **worse** | **worst** |
| much / many | **more** | **most** |
| little | **less / lesser** | **least** |
| **late** | **later** (time) / **latter** (order) | **latest** (time) / **last** (order) |
| **old** | **older** (age, persons or things) / **elder** (family, used before a noun) | **oldest / eldest** |
| **far** | **farther** (distance) / **further** (more, additional) | **farthest / furthest** |
| **near** | nearer | **nearest** (distance) / **next** (order) |
| fore | former | **foremost / first** |

- "My **elder** brother" (not *elder than*); "He is **older than** me."
- "The **latest** news" (newest); "the **last** chapter" (final). "The **latter**" = the second of two.

### Latin comparatives take "to", not "than" 🔥
**superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, anterior, posterior, major, minor**, and also **prefer, preferable**.
- "He is **senior to** me." — "This cloth is **superior to** that." — "I **prefer** tea **to** coffee."

### Adjectives not compared
**unique, perfect, ideal, complete, round, square, empty, full, excellent, chief, supreme, universal, infinite**. So **not** "more unique", "most perfect".

## Important rules 🔥
1. **No double comparative/superlative**: "more better", "most best" are wrong.
2. **Comparative + than + any other + singular noun** — "Dhaka is **bigger than any other city** in Bangladesh."
3. **One of the + superlative + plural noun + singular verb** — "Rahim **is one of the best boys** in the class." "**One of the students is** absent."
4. **the + comparative …, the + comparative** — "**The more** you read, **the more** you learn."
5. Comparative with **of the two** takes **the** — "Karim is **the taller of the two**."
6. Compare like with like — "The climate of Dhaka is hotter than **that of** Sylhet" (not "than Sylhet").
7. **Superlative with "of all"** — "He is the **best of all**."
8. Positive: **as … as** (affirmative); **so/as … as** (negative) — "He is not **so** tall **as** his brother."

## Order of adjectives
Opinion – size – age – shape – colour – origin – material – purpose + noun: "a **beautiful little old round red Chinese** wooden tea box". Numbers come first: "**the first two** boys" (not "the two first").

## Common mistakes
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| He is more better than me. | He is **better** than I. |
| He is senior than me. | He is **senior to** me. |
| This is the most unique design. | This is a **unique** design. |
| He is elder than me. | He is **older than** I. |
| Dhaka is bigger than any city in Bangladesh. | … than **any other** city … |
| He is one of the best boy. | He is one of the best **boys**. |
| I prefer tea than coffee. | I prefer tea **to** coffee. |
| Less people came. | **Fewer** people came. |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 **Latin comparatives** (senior, superior, prior, prefer) take **to**.
- 🔥 **farther** = distance, **further** = additional.
- **later/latest** = time; **latter/last** = order.
- **elder** is only for family members and never with "than".
- **Unique, perfect, round** have no degrees.
- "**than any other** + singular noun".
- **few** = hardly any; **a few** = some.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** "He is senior ___ me in service."
(a) than (b) to (c) from (d) of

**2.** Which sentence is correct?
(a) He is more better than his brother. (b) He is elder than me. (c) He is the taller of the two. (d) This is the most unique idea.

**3.** "Dhaka is larger than ___ city in Bangladesh."
(a) any (b) all (c) any other (d) every

**4.** Choose the right word: "I need ___ information about the course."
(a) further (b) farther (c) furthest (d) farthest

**5.** "___ boys are absent today; most are present."
(a) Little (b) A little (c) The few (d) Few

**6.** Which adjective cannot be compared?
(a) perfect (b) tall (c) wise (d) strong

**7.** "Of the two plans, I prefer the ___."
(a) later (b) latter (c) last (d) latest

**8.** "The more you practise, ___ you become."
(a) more perfect (b) better (c) the better (d) the best

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | senior is a Latin comparative and takes "to" |
| 2 | c | "the" + comparative with "of the two" |
| 3 | c | than any other + singular noun |
| 4 | a | further = additional |
| 5 | d | few = hardly any, fits "most are present" |
| 6 | a | perfect is an absolute adjective |
| 7 | b | latter = the second of two |
| 8 | c | the + comparative, the + comparative |
