# Number series

> **Why it matters:** "Find the next / missing / wrong number" (সংখ্যা ধারা) is one of the most common mental ability questions in BCS, bank and primary exams. Most series follow one of about ten patterns.

## Step-by-step method 🔥
1. **Check the differences** between terms. If they are equal → arithmetic series.
2. If differences are not equal, **take the differences of the differences** (second difference).
3. If the numbers grow fast, **check ratios** (× 2, × 3, × 1.5) or **× n + k** patterns.
4. Look for **squares, cubes, primes** or **n² ± 1, n³ ± 1**.
5. If the series goes up and down, try **two alternating series** (odd and even positions).
6. For "wrong term" questions, find the rule from most terms, then find the one that breaks it.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** 6, 11, 21, 36, 56, ?
Differences: 5, 10, 15, 20 → next difference 25. 56 + 25 = **81**.

**Example 2.** 2, 3, 8, 27, 112, ?
Pattern: 2 × 1 + 1 = 3; 3 × 2 + 2 = 8; 8 × 3 + 3 = 27; 27 × 4 + 4 = 112; 112 × 5 + 5 = **565**.

**Example 3.** 2, 10, 4, 20, 8, 40, ?
Odd positions: 2, 4, 8 (× 2). Even positions: 10, 20, 40 (× 2). The next term is in the odd series: **16**.

**Example 4 (wrong term).** 3, 5, 9, 17, 35, 65
Rule × 2 − 1: 3 → 5 → 9 → 17 → **33** → 65. So **35** is wrong.

**Example 5.** 4, 6, 9, 13.5, ?
Each term × 1.5: 13.5 × 1.5 = **20.25**.

## Quick revision
- Find the difference first, then the second difference, then the ratio.
- Numbers rising very fast suggest multiplication or powers.
- Numbers going up and down suggest two alternating series.
- Know squares to 25², cubes to 12³ and primes to 60 by heart.
- Wrong-term questions: build the rule from the majority of terms.
- n(n + 1): 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 42. n² + 1: 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, 37.
