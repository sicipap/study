# Numerical ability shortcuts

> **Why it matters:** Mental ability and maths sections reward speed. Quick multiplication, divisibility tests, percentage and fraction equivalents, and unit-digit tricks can save 20–40 seconds per question in BCS, bank and primary exams.

## Fast multiplication 🔥
| Trick | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Square of a number ending in 5 | n5² = n × (n + 1), then write 25 | **45² = 4 × 5 = 20 → 2025** |
| Multiply a 2-digit number by 11 | Write the digits apart and put their sum between (carry if 10 or more) | **36 × 11 = 3 (3+6) 6 = 396**; 57 × 11 = 5 (12) 7 = 627 |
| Multiply by 5 | × 10 ÷ 2 | 468 × 5 = 4680 ÷ 2 = 2340 |
| Multiply by 25 | × 100 ÷ 4 | **64 × 25 = 6400 ÷ 4 = 1600** |
| Multiply by 9, 99, 999 | × 10ⁿ − the number | **47 × 999 = 47000 − 47 = 46953** |
| Numbers near 100 | (100 − a)(100 + a) = 10000 − a² | **98 × 102 = 10000 − 4 = 9996** |
| Square near 50 | (50 + a)² = 2500 + 100a + a² | 52² = 2500 + 200 + 4 = 2704 |

## Divisibility rules 🔥
| Divisor | Test |
|---|---|
| 2 | Last digit even |
| 3 | Digit sum divisible by 3 |
| 4 | Last two digits divisible by 4 |
| 5 | Last digit 0 or 5 |
| 6 | Divisible by 2 **and** 3 |
| 8 | Last three digits divisible by 8 |
| 9 | Digit sum divisible by 9 |
| 10 | Last digit 0 |
| 11 | (Sum of digits in odd places) − (sum in even places) = 0 or a multiple of 11 |

Example for 11: 1353 → (1 + 5) − (3 + 3) = 0 → divisible (1353 = 11 × 123).

## Percentage change shortcuts
- **Two successive changes a% and b%:** net change = **a + b + ab/100** (use minus signs for decreases). +10% then +20% → 10 + 20 + 2 = **32%**. +10% then −10% → **−1%**.
- **Price rises by r%; to keep spending the same, cut consumption by** r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 %. Rise 25% → 25/125 × 100 = **20%**.
- If A is r% more than B, then B is r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 % **less** than A.

## Unit (last) digit of powers
| Last digit of base | Cycle | Cycle length |
|---|---|---|
| 0, 1, 5, 6 | Always the same digit | 1 |
| 4 | 4, 6 | 2 |
| 9 | 9, 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 2, 4, 8, 6 | 4 |
| 3 | 3, 9, 7, 1 | 4 |
| 7 | 7, 9, 3, 1 | 4 |
| 8 | 8, 4, 2, 6 | 4 |

Method: divide the power by 4 and use the remainder (remainder 0 → the 4th value in the cycle). **7²³:** 23 ÷ 4 leaves 3 → 3rd value in 7, 9, 3, 1 → **3**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** 65² = 6 × 7 = 42 → **4225**.

**Example 2.** 12.5% of 480 = 480 ÷ 8 = **60**.

**Example 3.** 999 × 47 = 47000 − 47 = **46953**.

**Example 4.** Is 7358 divisible by 11? (7 + 5) − (3 + 8) = 1 → **No**.

**Example 5.** The price of sugar rises 25%. By what percent must a family reduce use to spend the same? 25 ÷ 125 × 100 = **20%**.

## Quick revision
- n5² = n(n + 1) followed by 25.
- × 11: put the digit sum in the middle.
- × 25 = × 100 ÷ 4; × 5 = × 10 ÷ 2.
- 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/6 = 16⅔%, 1/12 = 8⅓%.
- Successive change: a + b + ab/100.
- Unit digits of 2, 3, 7, 8 repeat every 4 powers.
- Divisible by 11: alternate-digit difference is 0 or a multiple of 11.
