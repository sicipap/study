# Rocks, volcanoes, plate tectonics

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the three types of rock with examples (granite, sandstone, marble), metamorphic changes (limestone → marble), Earth's layers, Wegener's continental drift, plate boundary types, how the Himalayas formed, and famous volcanoes and the "Ring of Fire".

## Earth's interior 🔥
| Layer | Depth / facts |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Crust (ভূত্বক)** | Thinnest layer. **Continental crust** about **30–70 km** (lighter, granite-type, **SIAL** = silica + aluminium); **oceanic crust** about **5–10 km** (heavier, basalt-type, **SIMA** = silica + magnesium) |
| **Mantle (গুরুমণ্ডল)** | Down to about **2,900 km**; hot, partly molten upper part (**asthenosphere**) on which the plates move |
| 🔥 **Core (কেন্দ্রমণ্ডল)** | **Outer core liquid**, **inner core solid**; made mostly of **nickel and iron (NIFE)**; Earth's magnetic field comes from the moving outer core |

- **Lithosphere** = crust + uppermost solid mantle (broken into plates).
- **Boundaries (discontinuities):** **Mohorovičić (Moho)** — crust/mantle; **Gutenberg** — mantle/core; **Lehmann** — outer/inner core.
- 🔥 **Most abundant elements in the crust:** **oxygen (about 46%)**, **silicon (about 28%)**, **aluminium (most abundant metal)**, iron.

## Rocks (শিলা) 🔥🔥
| Type | How formed | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔥 **Igneous (আগ্নেয় শিলা)** | Cooling and hardening of **magma/lava** | Called the **primary rock**; no layers, no fossils; crystalline | **Intrusive/plutonic** (cooled slowly underground, large crystals): **granite**, gabbro. **Extrusive/volcanic** (cooled quickly on surface): **basalt**, pumice, obsidian |
| 🔥 **Sedimentary (পাললিক শিলা)** | Layers of **sediment** (sand, mud, shells) pressed and cemented over time | **Layered (strata)**; **contain fossils**; cover about **75% of the land surface** (but a small share of the crust's volume); hold **coal, oil and gas** | **Sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate, coal**, chalk |
| 🔥 **Metamorphic (রূপান্তরিত শিলা)** | Existing rocks changed by great **heat and pressure** | Harder, often banded/foliated | **Marble, slate, quartzite, gneiss, schist** |

### Metamorphic changes 🔥
| Original rock | Becomes |
|---|---|
| **Limestone** | **Marble** |
| **Sandstone** | **Quartzite** |
| **Shale / clay** | **Slate** |
| **Granite** | **Gneiss** |
| **Coal** | **Graphite** (via anthracite) |

- **Rock cycle:** igneous → weathering → sediments → sedimentary → heat/pressure → metamorphic → melting → magma → igneous again.
- **Bangladesh:** most of the land is made of **loose sedimentary deposits (alluvium)**. **Hard rock (granite-type)** is mined at **Maddhapara, Dinajpur**; **coal** at **Barapukuria, Dinajpur**; **limestone** in Sunamganj and **Joypurhat**.

## Continental drift and plate tectonics 🔥🔥
- 🔥 **Alfred Wegener** (German) proposed **continental drift** (**1912**; book *The Origin of Continents and Oceans*, 1915).
- About 250–300 million years ago all continents formed one supercontinent, 🔥 **Pangaea**, surrounded by the ocean **Panthalassa**. Pangaea split into **Laurasia** (north) and **Gondwanaland** (south), separated by the **Tethys Sea**.
- Evidence: fit of coastlines (South America–Africa), matching fossils and rocks across oceans.
- **Sea-floor spreading** (**Harry Hess**, 1960s) and **plate tectonics theory** (1960s) explained how continents move.
- **Plates:** large, rigid pieces of the lithosphere moving a few cm a year. **Major plates:** **Pacific (largest)**, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian (often split into Indian and Australian), Antarctic.

### Plate boundaries 🔥
| Boundary | Movement | Result | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔥 **Divergent (constructive)** | Plates **move apart** | New crust forms; **mid-ocean ridges**, rift valleys | **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**, Iceland, **East African Rift Valley** |
| 🔥 **Convergent (destructive)** | Plates **move towards each other** | **Subduction**, ocean trenches, volcanoes, **fold mountains**, earthquakes | **Himalayas** (Indian plate hitting Eurasian plate), **Andes**, **Mariana Trench**, Japan |
| **Transform (conservative)** | Plates **slide past** each other | Earthquakes; crust neither made nor destroyed | **San Andreas Fault**, California |

- 🔥 The **Himalayas** are **young fold mountains** formed by the collision of the **Indian and Eurasian plates**; they are still rising slowly.

### Types of mountains
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Fold mountains** (most common, highest) | **Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies**, Atlas |
| **Block mountains** (from faulting: horst and graben) | Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany), Sierra Nevada |
| **Volcanic mountains** | **Mount Fuji** (Japan), **Kilimanjaro**, Mauna Loa |
| **Residual mountains** (worn-down old mountains) | **Aravalli** (India), Eastern Ghats |

## Volcanoes (আগ্নেয়গিরি) 🔥
- A **volcano** is an opening in the crust through which **magma, gases and ash** erupt.
- **Magma** = molten rock **below** the surface; **lava** = molten rock **on** the surface.
- Parts: **magma chamber**, **vent**, **crater** (opening at the top). **Caldera:** a very large crater formed when the top collapses.
- **By activity:** **active** (erupts now or recently: **Etna**, **Stromboli**, **Kilauea**, **Mauna Loa**), **dormant** (sleeping, may erupt again), **extinct** (not expected to erupt again).
- **By shape:** **shield volcano** (broad, gentle slopes, runny lava — **Mauna Loa**), **composite/stratovolcano** (steep cone, explosive — **Fuji, Vesuvius**), cinder cone.

| Famous volcano / event | Fact |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Vesuvius** (Italy) | Buried **Pompeii** and Herculaneum in **79 CE** |
| **Stromboli** (Italy) | Called the **"Lighthouse of the Mediterranean"** |
| **Etna** (Sicily, Italy) | Europe's highest active volcano |
| **Mauna Loa** (Hawaii) | **Largest active volcano** on Earth |
| **Krakatoa** (Indonesia) | Huge eruption in **1883** |
| **Tambora** (Indonesia) | **1815**, largest eruption in recorded history → 1816 **"Year Without a Summer"** |
| **Mount St. Helens** (USA) | Major eruption in 1980 |
| **Ojos del Salado** (Chile–Argentina) | Highest active volcano |

- 🔥 **Pacific "Ring of Fire":** a horseshoe-shaped belt of volcanoes and earthquakes around the Pacific Ocean; about **75% of the world's active volcanoes** are here.
- **Indonesia** has the most active volcanoes of any country. **Iceland** is called the **"Land of Fire and Ice"**.
- **Hot spot:** a fixed plume of magma under a plate (**Hawaii**).
- **Geyser:** hot spring that shoots water and steam (**Old Faithful**, Yellowstone, USA); the word comes from **Geysir** in Iceland.
- Bangladesh has **no active volcano**.

## Quick revision
- Earth: **crust → mantle → core** (core = **NIFE**, outer core liquid).
- Crust's most abundant element: **oxygen**; metal: **aluminium**.
- Igneous = **primary rock** (granite, basalt); sedimentary = **layers and fossils**; metamorphic = heat/pressure.
- **Limestone → marble; sandstone → quartzite; shale → slate; granite → gneiss.**
- Continental drift: **Alfred Wegener**; supercontinent **Pangaea**.
- Himalayas: **Indian + Eurasian plate collision** (convergent, fold mountains).
- Mid-Atlantic Ridge = **divergent**; San Andreas = **transform**.
- Vesuvius buried **Pompeii (79 CE)**; Stromboli = **Lighthouse of the Mediterranean**; Ring of Fire = **Pacific**.
