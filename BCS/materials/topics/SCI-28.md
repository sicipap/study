# Medical technology: X-ray, CT, MRI, ECG

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask what each machine uses (X-ray, magnet, sound, electrical signals), who invented it, what it diagnoses, which ones use harmful radiation, and normal clinical values (pulse, BP, SpO₂).

## Imaging machines at a glance 🔥🔥🔥
| Technique | What it uses | Best for | Inventor / Nobel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **X-ray (রঞ্জন রশ্মি)** | **X-rays** (ionising EM waves) | **Bone fractures**, chest (**TB, pneumonia**), teeth, mammography | **Wilhelm Röntgen, 8 November 1895** (first Nobel in Physics, 1901) |
| **CT / CAT scan** (computed tomography) | **X-rays from many angles + computer** → cross-sectional images | **Head injury, stroke**, tumours, internal bleeding | **Godfrey Hounsfield & Allan Cormack** (Nobel Medicine **1979**); first scanner early 1970s |
| **MRI** (magnetic resonance imaging) | **Strong magnetic field + radio waves** (acts on **hydrogen nuclei** in body water); **no ionising radiation** | **Soft tissues: brain, spinal cord, joints, ligaments**, tumours | **Paul Lauterbur & Peter Mansfield** (Nobel Medicine **2003**); based on NMR (Bloch & Purcell) |
| **Ultrasonography (USG)** | **Ultrasound** (high-frequency sound, several MHz); **no radiation** | **Pregnancy (fetus)**, gallstones, liver, kidney, uterus | Developed in the 1950s (Ian Donald in obstetrics) |
| **Echocardiography** | Ultrasound of the **heart** | Heart valves, pumping | — |
| **Doppler ultrasound** | Doppler effect of sound | **Blood flow** in vessels | — |
| **PET scan** | **Radioactive tracer** (e.g. fluorine-18 glucose) emitting **positrons** | **Cancer spread**, brain activity | — |
| **Endoscopy** | **Optical fibre** (**total internal reflection**) with a camera | Inside **stomach**, food pipe, colon; also biopsy | — |
| **Angiography** | X-ray + **contrast dye** | **Blocked blood vessels** (coronary arteries) | — |

**Key points:**
- **X-ray and CT use ionising radiation** (risk with repeated exposure); **MRI and ultrasound do not**. Pregnant women are scanned by **ultrasound**, not X-ray.
- In an X-ray, **bones look white** because **calcium absorbs X-rays** strongly; soft tissue looks grey/dark. X-rays are produced in a **Coolidge tube** with a **tungsten** target. Staff wear **lead aprons**.
- **MRI** uses superconducting magnets (typically **1.5 or 3 tesla**) cooled by **liquid helium**. People with **pacemakers or some metal implants** may not be able to have an MRI.
- **Radiotherapy** for cancer uses **cobalt-60** gamma rays or a **linear accelerator (LINAC)**; **iodine-131** treats thyroid disease; **technetium-99m** is the most used diagnostic isotope.

## Recording the body's electrical signals 🔥
| Test | Records | Used for | Inventor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ECG / EKG** (electrocardiogram) | **Electrical activity of the heart** | **Heart attack**, irregular heartbeat (arrhythmia) | **Willem Einthoven** (1903, string galvanometer; Nobel **1924**) |
| **EEG** (electroencephalogram) | **Electrical activity of the brain** | **Epilepsy**, sleep disorders, brain death | **Hans Berger** (1920s) |
| **EMG** (electromyogram) | Electrical activity of **muscles** | Nerve–muscle disorders | — |

- A normal ECG has a **P wave, QRS complex and T wave**. The **QRS complex** shows ventricular contraction.

## Life-support and therapeutic devices 🔥
| Device / procedure | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Artificial pacemaker** | Gives electrical pulses to keep the heartbeat regular; first implanted in **1958 (Sweden)** |
| **Dialysis machine ("artificial kidney")** | Filters blood when kidneys fail; developed by **Willem Kolff (1943)** |
| **Heart–lung machine** | Takes over heart and lung function during open-heart surgery; **John Gibbon (1953)** |
| **Angioplasty and stent** | A **balloon** opens a blocked coronary artery and a **stent** (mesh tube) keeps it open; first balloon angioplasty **Andreas Grüntzig (1977)** |
| **Bypass surgery (CABG)** | Uses a vessel graft to route blood around a blocked artery |
| **Ventilator** | Machine that helps or takes over **breathing** (ICU) |
| **Defibrillator** | Electric shock to restore normal heart rhythm |
| **LASIK** | **Laser** reshapes the cornea to correct vision |
| **Robotic surgery** | e.g. the da Vinci system; small cuts, high precision |
| **Gamma knife** | Focused gamma rays to treat brain tumours without opening the skull |

| Instrument | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Stethoscope** (**René Laennec, 1816**) | Heart and lung sounds |
| **Sphygmomanometer** | **Blood pressure** (mmHg) |
| **Clinical thermometer** (Thomas Allbutt made a short, practical one, 1867) | Body temperature |
| **Pulse oximeter** | **Blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂)** and pulse, using red/infrared light through the fingertip |

|---|---|

| **Breathing rate** | **≈ 12–20 per minute** |

| Fasting blood glucose | ≈ 3.9–5.5 mmol/L (70–100 mg/dL) |
| Blood pH | 7.35–7.45 |

## Quick revision
- **X-ray: Röntgen (1895)**; bones look white because calcium absorbs X-rays.
- **CT = many X-rays + computer** (Hounsfield & Cormack, Nobel 1979).
- **MRI = magnet + radio waves**, no ionising radiation; best for **brain, spinal cord, soft tissue** (Nobel 2003).
- **Ultrasonography = high-frequency sound**; safe in **pregnancy**.
- **ECG = heart's electrical activity** (Einthoven); **EEG = brain** (Hans Berger).
- **Endoscopy** uses **optical fibre** (total internal reflection).
- Pacemaker keeps heartbeat regular; dialysis = artificial kidney (Kolff).
- Normal: **pulse 72/min, BP 120/80, SpO₂ 95–100%, temperature 37 °C**.
