# Famous quotations & lines

> **Why it matters:** "Who said / wrote…?" and "This line is from…" are among the most common English literature questions in BCS and other exams. Shakespeare and the Romantic poets supply most of them.

## Quick revision
- "To be, or not to be" → **Hamlet**; "Frailty, thy name is woman" → **Hamlet**.
- "Et tu, Brute?" and "Cowards die many times" → **Julius Caesar**.
- "A thing of beauty is a joy for ever" → **Keats**, *Endymion*.
- "If Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?" → **Shelley**.
- "The Child is father of the Man" → **Wordsworth**.
- "To err is human, to forgive divine" / "A little learning is a dangerous thing" → **Pope**.
- "No man is an island" → **Donne**.
- "Reading maketh a full man" → **Bacon**, "Of Studies".

## Practice MCQ
**1.** "A thing of beauty is a joy for ever" was written by:
(a) Shelley (b) Keats (c) Wordsworth (d) Byron

**2.** "Frailty, thy name is woman!" is from:
(a) Othello (b) Macbeth (c) King Lear (d) Hamlet

**3.** "If Winter comes, can Spring be far behind?" is a line from:
(a) Ode to the West Wind (b) To a Skylark (c) Ode to a Nightingale (d) To Autumn

**4.** "To err is human, to forgive divine" was said by:
(a) John Milton (b) John Dryden (c) Alexander Pope (d) Samuel Johnson

**5.** "No man is an island" was written by:
(a) Francis Bacon (b) John Donne (c) Ben Jonson (d) Thomas Gray

**6.** "Reading maketh a full man" is from Bacon's essay:
(a) Of Truth (b) Of Friendship (c) Of Studies (d) Of Travel

**7.** "Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown" occurs in:
(a) Henry IV, Part 2 (b) Richard III (c) Julius Caesar (d) The Tempest

**8.** "The Child is father of the Man" was written by:
(a) Coleridge (b) Blake (c) Tennyson (d) Wordsworth

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Opening line of Keats's *Endymion* |
| 2 | d | Hamlet speaks of his mother Gertrude |
| 3 | a | Last line of Shelley's ode |
| 4 | c | Pope, *An Essay on Criticism* |
| 5 | b | Donne, *Meditation XVII* |
| 6 | c | Bacon's "Of Studies" |
| 7 | a | King Henry IV's line |
| 8 | d | Wordsworth, "My Heart Leaps Up" |
