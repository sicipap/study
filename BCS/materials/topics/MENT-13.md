# Syllogism / logical deduction

> **Why it matters:** Syllogism (ন্যায় অনুমান/সিলোজিজম) questions give two or more statements, which you must accept as true even if they sound silly, and ask which conclusions follow. Venn diagrams and a small table of rules make them quick and safe.

## The four types of statement 🔥
| Code | Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | All S are P | All cats are animals | Universal positive |
| **E** | No S is P | No cat is a dog | Universal negative |
| **I** | Some S are P | Some cats are black | Particular positive |
| **O** | Some S are not P | Some cats are not black | Particular negative |

"Some" means **at least one, possibly all**.

## Conversion (turning a statement round) 🔥
| Statement | Valid conversion |
|---|---|
| A: All S are P | **Some P are S** (not "All P are S") |
| E: No S is P | **No P is S** |
| I: Some S are P | **Some P are S** |
| O: Some S are not P | **No valid conversion** |

## Combining two statements (middle term linked) 🔥
Arrange the pair so that the **predicate of the first = subject of the second** (All A are **B**; All **B** are C).

| 1st + 2nd | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| A + A | **A**: All A are C |
| A + E | **E**: No A is C |
| E + A | **O reversed**: Some C are not A |
| E + I | **O reversed**: Some C are not A |
| I + A | **I**: Some A are C |
| I + E | **O**: Some A are not C |
| A + I, I + I, A + O, E + E, O + any | **No definite conclusion** |

Two short rules: **two particular (some) statements give no conclusion**, and **two negative statements give no conclusion**.

## "Either … or" case
If neither conclusion follows on its own, but the two conclusions have the **same subject and predicate** and form a pair like **"Some A are C" and "No A is C"**, one of them must be true → answer **"Either I or II follows"**.

## Venn diagram check
Draw circles for each term using the **least overlap the statements allow**. A conclusion follows only if it is true in **every** possible drawing. "Some A are B" means the circles overlap; "All A are B" means A sits inside B; "No A is B" means separate circles.

## Other logical deduction
- **If P then Q; P is true → Q is true** (valid).
- **If P then Q; Q is false → P is false** (valid).
- If P then Q; Q is true → P **may or may not** be true (a common trap).

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** All cats are dogs. All dogs are cows. Conclusions: I. All cats are cows. II. Some cows are cats.
A + A → All cats are cows (I follows). Converting it gives Some cows are cats (II follows). **Both follow.**

**Example 2.** All pens are books. Some books are copies. I. Some pens are copies. II. Some copies are books.
A + I → no conclusion, so I does not follow. II is the conversion of "Some books are copies" → follows. **Only II.**

**Example 3.** No man is a bird. All birds are animals. I. Some animals are not men. II. No animal is a man.
E + A → Some animals are not men. **Only I.**

**Example 4.** Some flowers are red. Some red things are pens. I. Some flowers are pens. II. No flower is a pen.
I + I → no conclusion; but I and II form a complementary pair → **Either I or II follows.**

## Quick revision
- A = All, E = No, I = Some, O = Some … not.
- A converts to I; E to E; I to I; O cannot be converted.
- A + A = A; A + E = E; I + A = I; I + E = O; E + A and E + I = O reversed.
- Two "some" statements or two negative statements: no conclusion.
- "Some A are C" + "No A is C" → Either–or.
- Accept the statements as true even if they are against common sense.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Statements: All cats are dogs. All dogs are cows. Conclusions: I. All cats are cows. II. Some cows are cats.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**2.** Statements: All pens are books. Some books are copies. Conclusions: I. Some pens are copies. II. Some copies are books.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**3.** Statements: No man is a bird. All birds are animals. Conclusions: I. Some animals are not men. II. No animal is a man.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**4.** Statements: Some flowers are red. Some red things are pens. Conclusions: I. Some flowers are pens. II. No flower is a pen.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Either I or II follows (d) Neither follows

**5.** Statements: All teachers are honest. Rahim is a teacher. Which conclusion follows?
(a) Rahim is dishonest (b) Rahim is honest (c) All honest people are teachers (d) Rahim may not be honest

**6.** Statements: No car is a bus. No bus is a truck. Conclusions: I. No car is a truck. II. All trucks are cars.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**7.** Statements: Some students are players. All players are singers. Conclusions: I. Some students are singers. II. All singers are students.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**8.** "All roses are flowers." Which of the following must be true?
(a) All flowers are roses (b) Some flowers are roses (c) No flower is a rose (d) Some roses are not flowers

**9.** Statements: All A are B. No B is C. Conclusions: I. No A is C. II. All C are A.
(a) Only conclusion II follows (b) Neither follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Only conclusion I follows

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | A + A gives All cats are cows; its conversion gives II. |
| 2 | b | A + I gives no conclusion; II is the conversion of the I statement. |
| 3 | a | E + A gives Some animals are not men. |
| 4 | c | No definite conclusion, but I and II are a complementary pair. |
| 5 | b | Rahim belongs to "teachers", who are all honest. |
| 6 | d | Two negatives give no conclusion; II is not a complementary pair with I. |
| 7 | a | I + A gives Some students are singers. |
| 8 | b | A statement converts to I: Some flowers are roses. |
| 9 | d | A + E gives No A is C. |
