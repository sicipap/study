# Oceans, currents, tides

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the largest/deepest ocean, the deepest trench, warm vs cold currents (Gulf Stream, Kuroshio, Humboldt), where warm and cold currents meet, and the causes of spring and neap tides.

## Oceans (মহাসাগর) 🔥
- Oceans cover about **71%** of Earth's surface and hold about **97%** of Earth's water.
- Earth has **five oceans** (largest → smallest):

| Rank | Ocean | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🔥 **Pacific (প্রশান্ত মহাসাগর)** | **Largest and deepest**; covers about a third of Earth's surface; named by **Magellan** ("peaceful"). Deepest point: **Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench** (about **11,000 m**; figures vary from 10,935 to 11,034 m). **"Ring of Fire"** of volcanoes around it |
| 2 | **Atlantic** | **S-shaped**; **busiest ocean for trade**; deepest point: **Puerto Rico Trench** |
| 3 | 🔥 **Indian** | **Only ocean named after a country** (India); deepest point: **Java (Sunda) Trench**; the **Bay of Bengal** is part of it |
| 4 | **Southern (Antarctic)** | Surrounds Antarctica; recognised by the International Hydrographic Organization in **2000** |
| 5 | 🔥 **Arctic** | **Smallest and shallowest**; mostly frozen |

- **Largest bay:** **Bay of Bengal (বঙ্গোপসাগর)**. **Largest gulf:** **Gulf of Mexico**.
- **Sargasso Sea** (North Atlantic): the **only sea with no land coast** — bounded by ocean currents; full of floating **Sargassum** seaweed.
- **Average ocean salinity:** about **35 parts per thousand (35‰)**. The **Dead Sea** (really a lake) is among the saltiest water bodies; the **Red Sea** is the saltiest open sea.

### Ocean floor (relief)
**Continental shelf** (shallow, up to about 200 m deep; rich in fish, oil and gas) → **continental slope** → **deep-sea plain (abyssal plain)** → **ocean deeps/trenches**. **Mid-ocean ridges** are underwater mountain chains.
- **Swatch of No Ground (সোয়াচ অব নো গ্রাউন্ড):** a deep **submarine canyon** in the Bay of Bengal off Bangladesh; declared Bangladesh's **first marine protected area (2014)**.

### Law of the sea (UNCLOS, 1982)
| Zone | Width from the baseline |
|---|---|
| **Territorial sea** | **12 nautical miles** |
| **Contiguous zone** | **24 nautical miles** |
| 🔥 **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)** | **200 nautical miles** |
| Continental shelf | Up to 350 nautical miles (in some cases) |

- Bangladesh won its maritime boundary cases against **Myanmar (ITLOS, 2012)** and **India (Permanent Court of Arbitration, 2014)**, gaining about **1,18,813 sq km** of sea area.

## Ocean currents (সমুদ্রস্রোত) 🔥🔥
- Caused mainly by **planetary winds**, differences in **temperature and salinity (density)**, Earth's rotation (**Coriolis force**) and the shape of coasts.
- **Warm currents** flow from the **equator towards the poles**; **cold currents** flow from the **poles towards the equator**.
- In the Northern Hemisphere, currents move **clockwise**; in the Southern Hemisphere, **anti-clockwise**.

| Current | Warm/Cold | Ocean / location | Key fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔥 **Gulf Stream** | **Warm** | North Atlantic, from the Gulf of Mexico | Continues as the **North Atlantic Drift**; keeps **NW Europe's ports ice-free** |
| 🔥 **Kuroshio** (Japan current) | **Warm** | North Pacific, east of Japan | Called the **"Black Current"** |
| **Brazil, East Australian, Agulhas** | Warm | S. Atlantic, S. Pacific, Indian | |
| 🔥 **Labrador** | **Cold** | NW Atlantic, near Canada | **Meets the Gulf Stream near Newfoundland** → thick **fog** and the rich **Grand Banks** fishing ground |
| **Oyashio (Kurile)** | Cold | NW Pacific | **Meets the Kuroshio near Japan** → rich fishing |
| 🔥 **Humboldt (Peru)** | **Cold** | West coast of **South America** | Very rich fishery; helps create the **Atacama Desert** |
| **California** | Cold | West coast of the USA | |
| **Canary** | Cold | NW Africa | |
| **Benguela** | Cold | SW Africa | Linked to the **Namib Desert** |
| **West Australian** | Cold | Western Australia | |
| **Antarctic Circumpolar (West Wind Drift)** | Cold | Around Antarctica | **Largest ocean current** |

- 🔥 **Rule:** where **warm and cold currents meet**, there is **fog** and **rich fishing grounds** (plankton grow well).
- **Cold currents along west coasts** of continents in the tropics help form **hot deserts** (Atacama, Namib, Kalahari, Australian).
- **El Niño:** unusual **warming** of the eastern Pacific near Peru; brings drought to South Asia/Australia and floods to South America. **La Niña:** the opposite (cooling).
- **Indian Ocean currents reverse direction** with the monsoon seasons.

## Tides (জোয়ার-ভাটা) 🔥🔥
- The **regular rise and fall of sea level**, caused mainly by the **gravitational pull of the Moon** (and less by the Sun) and by Earth's rotation.
- The **Moon's tide-producing force is about 2.2 times the Sun's**, because the Moon is much closer.
- Most coasts get **two high tides and two low tides** in about **24 hours 50 minutes**. So a high tide comes about every **12 hours 25 minutes**, and the tide is about **50 minutes later** each day.
- **Primary tide (মুখ্য জোয়ার):** on the side facing the Moon. **Secondary tide (গৌণ জোয়ার):** on the opposite side (caused by centrifugal force).

| Type | When | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 🔥 **Spring tide (ভরা কটাল)** | **New moon (অমাবস্যা) and full moon (পূর্ণিমা)** — Sun, Moon and Earth **in a straight line** | **Highest high tides and lowest low tides** |
| 🔥 **Neap tide (মরা কটাল)** | **First and last quarter (অষ্টমী)** — Sun and Moon **at right angles** to Earth | **Smallest tidal range** |

- **Highest tidal range in the world:** **Bay of Fundy, Canada** (about 16 m).
- **Tidal bore (বান):** a wall of water that rushes up a river mouth at high tide (seen in the Meghna estuary).
- **Tides help** navigation into river ports (e.g. Kolkata, Mongla), fishing, and **tidal power**.

## Quick revision
- Largest and deepest ocean: **Pacific**; deepest point: **Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench**.
- Smallest ocean: **Arctic**; only ocean named after a country: **Indian**.
- Largest bay: **Bay of Bengal**; sea with no coast: **Sargasso Sea**.
- EEZ = **200 nautical miles**; territorial sea = **12 nautical miles**.
- Gulf Stream and Kuroshio = **warm**; Labrador, Humboldt, Canary, Benguela = **cold**.
- Gulf Stream + Labrador meet near **Newfoundland** → fog, fishing.
- Spring tide: **full/new moon**, all in a line; neap tide: **quarter moons**, right angle.
- Tides are caused mainly by the **Moon**.
