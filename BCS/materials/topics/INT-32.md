# Soft/hard power, diplomacy, terrorism, Belt & Road

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask who coined "soft power", the meaning of diplomatic terms (persona non grata, agrément, chargé d'affaires), the Vienna Conventions, types of diplomacy (ping-pong, shuttle, gunboat), key terrorism facts (9/11, FATF, Holey Artisan), and the Belt and Road Initiative (launched 2013; Bangladesh joined 2016).

## Hard, soft and smart power (ক্ষমতার ধরন) 🔥
| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard power** | Getting results by **coercion**: military force, sanctions, payments | Invasion, economic sanctions |
| 🔥 **Soft power** | Getting results by **attraction**: culture, values, policies | Hollywood, K-pop, universities, scholarships |
| **Smart power** | A **mix** of hard and soft power | |
| **Sharp power** | Using influence to **manipulate** or censor abroad (term from 2017) | Disinformation |

- 🔥 **Joseph S. Nye** (Harvard) **coined "soft power"** in ***Bound to Lead* (1990)**; explained it in ***Soft Power: The Means to Success in World Politics* (2004)**.
- Three sources of soft power (Nye): **culture**, **political values**, **foreign policy**.

## Diplomacy (কূটনীতি) 🔥
- 🔥 **Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations: 1961** (in force 1964) — rules for embassies and **diplomatic immunity**.
- **Vienna Convention on Consular Relations: 1963.**
- **Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties: 1969.**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Persona non grata** | A diplomat declared **unwelcome**; must leave the host country |
| **Agrément** | Host country's **approval** of a proposed ambassador |
| **Letter of credence / credentials** | Letter an ambassador presents to the **head of state** of the host country |
| 🔥 **Chargé d'affaires** | Diplomat who **heads a mission in the absence of an ambassador** |
| 🔥 **High Commissioner** | Title of an ambassador between **Commonwealth** countries (e.g. Bangladesh–India, Bangladesh–UK) |
| **Doyen (dean) of the diplomatic corps** | The longest-serving ambassador in a capital |
| **Attaché** | Specialist diplomat (defence, trade, press attaché) |
| **Consul** | Official who serves citizens and trade in a city; works in a consulate |
| **Diplomatic immunity** | Protection of diplomats from arrest and local courts |
| **Diplomatic bag (pouch)** | Official mail that cannot be opened or detained |
| **Extraterritoriality** | Embassy premises are **inviolable** (host police cannot enter without permission) |
| **Démarche** | Formal diplomatic protest or request |
| **Rapprochement** | Re-establishing friendly relations |
| **Détente** | Easing of tension (US–USSR, 1970s) |

### Types of diplomacy 🔥
| Type | Example / origin |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Ping-pong diplomacy** | **USA–China, 1971** table-tennis visits; led to **Nixon's visit to China (1972)** |
| 🔥 **Shuttle diplomacy** | **Henry Kissinger**, Middle East after the 1973 war |
| **Gunboat diplomacy** | Pressure with the threat of naval/military force |
| **Dollar diplomacy** | US President **William Taft**: using economic power abroad |
| **Cricket diplomacy** | **India–Pakistan** (e.g. General Zia's 1987 visit to watch a match) |
| **Track I / Track II** | Official government talks / unofficial talks between experts and citizens |
| **Public diplomacy** | Reaching foreign publics directly (media, exchanges) |
| **Vaccine diplomacy** | Giving COVID-19 vaccines to gain goodwill |
| **Wolf-warrior diplomacy** | Aggressive style of some Chinese diplomats (from a film name) |
| **Debt-trap diplomacy** | Claim that loans are used to gain leverage; example often cited: **Hambantota port (Sri Lanka)** leased to a Chinese firm for **99 years (2017)** |

- 🔥 **Bangladesh's foreign-policy principle:** "**Friendship to all, malice towards none**" — based on **Article 25** of the Constitution.

## Terrorism (সন্ত্রাসবাদ) 🔥
- There is **no single UN-agreed definition** of terrorism; a **Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism** has been under negotiation for years (proposed by **India**, 1996).
- 🔥 **9/11 attacks (11 September 2001):** **al-Qaeda** hijacked planes and hit the **World Trade Center** (New York) and the **Pentagon**; about **3,000** killed. → US "**War on Terror**" and the invasion of Afghanistan.
- **UNSC Resolution 1373 (2001):** binding counter-terrorism duties; set up the **Counter-Terrorism Committee**.
- **UN Global Counter-Terrorism Strategy (2006)**; **UN Office of Counter-Terrorism (2017)**.
- 🔥 **FATF (Financial Action Task Force):** set up in **1989** by the **G7**, based in **Paris**; fights **money laundering and terror financing**; keeps a "**grey list**" and a "**black list**".

| Group | Base / leader | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| 🔥 **Al-Qaeda** | Founded **1988** by **Osama bin Laden** | Bin Laden killed by US forces in **Abbottabad, Pakistan, 2 May 2011** |
| 🔥 **ISIS / Islamic State (Daesh)** | Iraq & Syria; **Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi** declared a "caliphate" in **2014** | Baghdadi killed in **2019** |
| **Boko Haram** | **Nigeria** | Kidnapped the Chibok schoolgirls (2014) |
| **Al-Shabaab** | **Somalia** | |
| **Taliban** | Afghanistan | Took power in 2021 (see INT-16) |

- **Bangladesh:** 🔥 **Holey Artisan Bakery attack, Gulshan, Dhaka, 1 July 2016** (22 people killed, including 17 foreigners, besides attackers). **Anti-Terrorism Act, 2009**. Policy of "**zero tolerance**" against terrorism.

## Belt and Road Initiative (BRI / বেল্ট অ্যান্ড রোড) 🔥
- 🔥 Announced by Chinese President **Xi Jinping** in **2013**; first called **"One Belt, One Road" (OBOR)**.
  - 🔥 **"Silk Road Economic Belt"** (land) — announced in **Astana, Kazakhstan, September 2013**.
  - 🔥 **"21st-Century Maritime Silk Road"** (sea) — announced in **Jakarta, Indonesia, October 2013**.
- Aim: build roads, railways, ports, power plants and pipelines linking **Asia, Europe and Africa**, reviving the ancient **Silk Road**.
- **Economic corridors** include 🔥 **CPEC (China–Pakistan Economic Corridor)**, ending at **Gwadar port** (Balochistan), and the proposed **BCIM (Bangladesh–China–India–Myanmar)** corridor.
- **Belt and Road Forums** in Beijing: **2017, 2019, 2023** (10th anniversary).
- **Italy** was the only G7 country to join (2019); it **left in 2023**. **India opposes BRI** because CPEC passes through Pakistan-held Kashmir.
- Criticism: **debt burden**, lack of transparency ("debt-trap" debate).
- 🔥 **Bangladesh joined BRI in October 2016** during **Xi Jinping's visit to Dhaka**. Chinese-financed projects include the **Padma Bridge Rail Link**, **Karnaphuli (Bangabandhu) Tunnel**, and the **Payra power plant**.
- Western alternatives: **PGII** (G7, 2022), **EU Global Gateway** (2021), **IMEC** (India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor, announced at the **G20 New Delhi summit, 2023**).

## Quick revision
- "Soft power": **Joseph Nye** (*Bound to Lead*, 1990).
- **Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations 1961**; consular **1963**.
- Persona non grata = unwelcome diplomat; chargé d'affaires heads a mission without an ambassador; **High Commissioner** = Commonwealth ambassador.
- Ping-pong diplomacy: **USA–China 1971**; shuttle diplomacy: **Kissinger**.
- 9/11: **2001**; bin Laden killed **2011, Abbottabad**; FATF **1989, Paris**.
- Holey Artisan attack: **1 July 2016**.
- BRI: **2013, Xi Jinping** (Astana + Jakarta); **CPEC → Gwadar**; Bangladesh joined **October 2016**.
