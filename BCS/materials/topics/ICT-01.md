# History & generations of computers

> **Why it matters:** Almost every exam asks "father of the computer", "first programmer", the technology of each generation, and the first computer in Bangladesh. These are easy marks if you memorise the tables.

## Early calculating devices
| Device | Inventor / year | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Abacus** | Ancient Mesopotamia/China | **Earliest calculating device** |
| **Napier's Bones** | **John Napier**, 1617 | Also invented **logarithms** |
| **Slide rule** | **William Oughtred**, 1622 | Based on logarithms |
| 🔥 **Pascaline** | **Blaise Pascal, 1642** | **First mechanical calculator** (addition/subtraction only) |
| **Stepped Reckoner** | **Gottfried Leibniz**, 1673 | Could also **multiply and divide** |
| **Punched-card loom** | **Joseph Marie Jacquard**, 1801 | Idea of punched cards to store instructions |

## Pioneers 🔥
| Person | Remember them for |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Charles Babbage** (UK) | **Father of the computer (কম্পিউটারের জনক)**. Designed the **Difference Engine** (1822) and the **Analytical Engine** (1830s). The Analytical Engine had a **"mill"** (like a CPU) and a **"store"** (like memory) |
| 🔥 **Ada Lovelace** | **First computer programmer**. Wrote an algorithm for the Analytical Engine. The programming language **Ada** is named after her |
| **Herman Hollerith** | Punched-card **tabulating machine** for the **1890 US census**. His company later became **IBM** |
| **Alan Turing** | **Turing Machine** (1936). Father of theoretical computer science and **AI**. Proposed the **Turing Test** (1950) |
| **John von Neumann** | **Stored-program concept**: program and data kept in the same memory (von Neumann architecture) |
| **Howard Aiken** | **Mark I** (1944), an electromechanical computer at Harvard with IBM |
| **J. Presper Eckert & John Mauchly** | Built **ENIAC** and later **UNIVAC I** |

### Landmark machines
- **ENIAC** (1946): the **first general-purpose electronic computer**. It used about **18,000 vacuum tubes**.
- **EDVAC**: an early stored-program design based on von Neumann's report.
- **UNIVAC I** (1951): the **first commercial computer**.
- **Some sources** call the **ABC (Atanasoff–Berry Computer)** the first electronic digital computer; in exams the usual answer for "first electronic computer" is **ENIAC**.

## Generations of computers 🔥🔥 (most asked)
| Generation | Period | Main technology | Features / examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | 1946–1958 | **Vacuum tube** | Huge, very hot, costly; **machine language**; punched cards; magnetic drum memory. ENIAC, EDVAC, UNIVAC I, IBM 650 |
| **2nd** | 1959–1964 | **Transistor** (invented **1947**, **Bell Labs**) | Smaller, faster, less heat; **assembly & high-level languages (FORTRAN, COBOL)**; **magnetic core memory**. IBM 1401, **IBM 1620** |
| **3rd** | 1965–1971 | **Integrated Circuit (IC)** (Jack Kilby & Robert Noyce) | **Operating systems**, keyboard and monitor, time-sharing. **IBM System/360** |
| **4th** | 1971–present | **Microprocessor**, LSI/**VLSI** | **Personal computers**. 🔥 **First microprocessor: Intel 4004 (1971)**. IBM PC (1981), Apple Macintosh (1984) |
| **5th** | Present & future | **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**, ULSI, parallel processing | Natural-language processing, expert systems, robotics, quantum computing |

**Memory hook:** *Tube → Transistor → IC → Microprocessor → AI*

**Notes that exams test:**
- Transistor inventors: **William Shockley, John Bardeen and Walter Brattain** (Bell Labs, 1947).
- IC was invented by **Jack Kilby** (Texas Instruments, 1958); **Robert Noyce** developed it independently.
- Intel 4004 was a **4-bit** microprocessor designed by **Ted Hoff** and colleagues.
- Scale of integration: **SSI → MSI → LSI → VLSI → ULSI** (more transistors per chip at each step).

## Computers in Bangladesh 🔥
- The **first computer in Bangladesh** was an **IBM 1620** (a **2nd-generation** computer).
- It was installed in **1964** at the **Atomic Energy Centre, Dhaka (পরমাণু শক্তি কেন্দ্র)**.
- **Bijoy** Bangla keyboard/software: **Mustafa Jabbar** (1988).
- **Avro** keyboard (free, open source): **Mehdi Hasan Khan** (2003).

## Quick revision
- Earliest calculating device: **Abacus**.
- First mechanical calculator: **Pascaline (Blaise Pascal, 1642)**.
- Father of the computer: **Charles Babbage**; first programmer: **Ada Lovelace**.
- Stored-program concept: **John von Neumann**.
- First general-purpose electronic computer: **ENIAC (1946)**, about 18,000 vacuum tubes.
- First commercial computer: **UNIVAC I (1951)**.
- Generations: **Vacuum tube → Transistor → IC → Microprocessor → AI**.
- First microprocessor: **Intel 4004 (1971)**.
- First computer in Bangladesh: **IBM 1620, 1964, Atomic Energy Centre, Dhaka**.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Who is known as the father of the computer?
(a) Alan Turing (b) Charles Babbage (c) John von Neumann (d) Blaise Pascal

**2.** Which technology was used in first-generation computers?
(a) Transistor (b) Integrated circuit (c) Vacuum tube (d) Microprocessor

**3.** Who is regarded as the first computer programmer?
(a) Ada Lovelace (b) Grace Hopper (c) Herman Hollerith (d) Howard Aiken

**4.** The first microprocessor was:
(a) Intel 8086 (b) Motorola 6800 (c) Intel 8080 (d) Intel 4004

**5.** The first computer in Bangladesh was installed in 1964 at:
(a) BUET (b) Atomic Energy Centre, Dhaka (c) University of Dhaka (d) Bangladesh Bank

**6.** Integrated circuits (IC) are the main technology of which generation?
(a) Third (b) Second (c) Fourth (d) Fifth

**7.** The stored-program concept was given by:
(a) Charles Babbage (b) John Mauchly (c) Alan Turing (d) John von Neumann

**8.** Which was the first commercial computer?
(a) ENIAC (b) Mark I (c) UNIVAC I (d) EDVAC

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Babbage designed the Difference and Analytical Engines |
| 2 | c | 1st generation (1946–58) used vacuum tubes |
| 3 | a | Ada Lovelace wrote an algorithm for the Analytical Engine |
| 4 | d | Intel 4004 (1971), a 4-bit chip |
| 5 | b | IBM 1620 at the Atomic Energy Centre, Dhaka |
| 6 | a | 3rd generation (1965–71) used ICs |
| 7 | d | Von Neumann: program and data in the same memory |
| 8 | c | UNIVAC I (1951) was the first commercial computer |
