# Concepts: state, sovereignty, geopolitics, balance of power

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask for the elements of a state, the "father" of each theory (Bodin, Mackinder, Mahan, Morgenthau), and who coined a term ("geopolitics", "soft power", "Iron Curtain"). These are short, one-line MCQs that come up again and again.

## State (রাষ্ট্র) 🔥
A state has **four elements (রাষ্ট্রের উপাদান)**:
1. **Population** (জনসমষ্টি)
2. **Territory** (নির্দিষ্ট ভূখণ্ড)
3. **Government** (সরকার)
4. **Sovereignty** (সার্বভৌমত্ব). 🔥 **This is the most important element.** It separates a state from other organizations (a province, a city corporation or an NGO has the first three but not sovereignty).

- The **Montevideo Convention (1933)** set the legal criteria for statehood: **permanent population, defined territory, government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states**.
- **Nation (জাতি)** is a people united by language, culture, history and a common feeling. **State** is a political-legal unit. A nation can exist without a state: the **Kurds** and the **Palestinians** are examples.
- **Nation-state (জাতিরাষ্ট্র):** a state whose people largely share one national identity, e.g. Bangladesh, Japan.
- **Aristotle** is called the **father of political science**; he said "man is by nature a political animal".

## Sovereignty (সার্বভৌমত্ব) 🔥
- Sovereignty means **supreme power**, both **internal** (over every person and group inside the state) and **external** (freedom from outside control).
- 🔥 **Jean Bodin (জ্যাঁ বোদাঁ)**, a French thinker, is the **father of the modern theory of sovereignty**. Book: *Six Books of the Commonwealth* (**1576**).
- **Thomas Hobbes** wrote ***Leviathan*** (**1651**): people give up their rights to an absolute sovereign to escape the "state of nature", where life is "solitary, poor, nasty, brutish and short".
- **John Locke** (*Two Treatises of Government*, 1689): limited government, natural rights to life, liberty and property.
- **John Austin** (England) gave the **legal (monistic) theory** of sovereignty: "law is the command of the sovereign".
- **Rousseau** gave the idea of **popular sovereignty** and the "general will" (*The Social Contract*, **1762**).
- **Pluralists** (Harold Laski and others) opposed the idea of one absolute sovereign.
- 🔥 The **Treaty (Peace) of Westphalia (1648)** ended the Thirty Years' War and began the **modern sovereign nation-state system** ("Westphalian sovereignty").

| Kind of sovereignty | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Legal sovereignty | The authority that makes law (e.g. Parliament) |
| Political sovereignty | The power behind the law: the voters/people |
| De jure | Sovereignty by law |
| De facto | Sovereignty in fact (actual control) |
| Popular sovereignty | Final power lies with the people (Rousseau) |

## Geopolitics (ভূ-রাজনীতি) 🔥
Geopolitics studies how **geography** (location, resources, seas, borders) shapes politics and power.

| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Rudolf Kjellén** (Sweden) | **First used the term "geopolitics"** (1899) |
| **Friedrich Ratzel** (Germany) | **"Lebensraum" (living space)**; state as a living organism |
| **Karl Haushofer** (Germany) | German geopolitics school; his ideas were used by the Nazis |
| **Alfred Thayer Mahan** (USA) | **Sea-power theory**, *The Influence of Sea Power upon History* (1890) |
| 🔥 **Halford Mackinder** (UK) | **Heartland theory** ("The Geographical Pivot of History", 1904). His famous dictum (1919): "Who rules East Europe commands the Heartland; who rules the Heartland commands the World-Island; who rules the World-Island commands the World" |
| **Nicholas Spykman** (USA) | **Rimland theory**: control of the coastal rim of Eurasia is the key |

- **Bangladesh's geopolitical importance:** it sits at the head of the **Bay of Bengal**, between South Asia and South-East Asia, close to India's north-east and near the Indo-Pacific sea lanes.

## Balance of power (ক্ষমতার ভারসাম্য) 🔥
- Power is spread so that **no single state can dominate** the others. If one state grows too strong, others unite (form an alliance) against it.
- Classic example: the **Congress of Vienna (1815)** and the **"Concert of Europe"** after Napoleon's defeat.
- Ways to keep the balance: alliances, arms build-up, buffer states, compensation, divide and rule.
- **World systems (বিশ্বব্যবস্থা):**
  - **Unipolar** (এককেন্দ্রিক): one superpower, e.g. the USA after 1991.
  - **Bipolar** (দ্বিকেন্দ্রিক): two superpowers, e.g. USA vs USSR in the Cold War.
  - **Multipolar** (বহুকেন্দ্রিক): many major powers, e.g. Europe before WWI.
- **Collective security** is different: all members promise to act together against any aggressor (the idea behind the League of Nations and the UN).

| **Enclave** (ছিটমহল) | Territory of one state fully surrounded by another |
| **Landlocked state** | A state with no sea coast (e.g. Nepal, Bhutan, Afghanistan) |

## Quick revision
- Four elements of a state: population, territory, government, **sovereignty** (most important).
- Statehood criteria: **Montevideo Convention, 1933**.
- Father of the modern theory of sovereignty: **Jean Bodin** (1576).
- *Leviathan*: **Hobbes** (1651); *The Social Contract*: **Rousseau** (1762); legal (monistic) sovereignty: **John Austin**.
- Modern nation-state system began with the **Peace of Westphalia, 1648**.
- Term "geopolitics": **Rudolf Kjellén**; Heartland: **Mackinder**; Rimland: **Spykman**; sea power: **Mahan**.
- Balance of power classic example: **Congress of Vienna, 1815**.
- *Politics Among Nations*: **Morgenthau**; soft power: **Joseph Nye**.
