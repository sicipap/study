# Motion, force, Newton's laws, gravity

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask which Newton's law explains an everyday event, the value of g, escape velocity, where weight is greatest/least, and scalar vs vector quantities.

## Scalar and vector (স্কেলার ও ভেক্টর রাশি) 🔥
| Scalar (magnitude only) | Vector (magnitude + direction) |
|---|---|
| Mass, distance, speed, time, work, energy, power, temperature, density, electric current | **Displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, weight, momentum**, torque, electric field |

- **Speed (দ্রুতি)** = distance/time (scalar). **Velocity (বেগ)** = displacement/time (vector).
- **Acceleration (ত্বরণ)** = rate of change of velocity, unit **m/s²**.
- Equations of motion (uniform acceleration): **v = u + at**, **s = ut + ½at²**, **v² = u² + 2as**.

## Newton's laws of motion 🔥🔥
Isaac Newton published them in ***Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica* (1687)**.

| Law | Statement | Everyday examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1st law (law of inertia, জড়তার সূত্র)** | A body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless an external force acts on it | Passengers fall **backward when a bus suddenly starts** and **forward when it suddenly stops**; dust leaves a carpet when beaten |
| **2nd law** | Rate of change of momentum ∝ applied force; **F = ma** | Cricketer pulls hands back while catching (more time, less force); defines the unit of force |
| **3rd law** | **Every action has an equal and opposite reaction** | **Rocket and jet propulsion**, gun **recoil**, swimming, walking, a boat moving back when you jump ashore |

- **Inertia (জড়তা)**: tendency to resist change of state. **Mass is the measure of inertia.** Two types: inertia of rest, inertia of motion. Galileo first described it.
- **Momentum (ভরবেগ)** p = mv, unit kg·m/s. **Law of conservation of momentum** explains rocket motion and gun recoil.
- **1 newton** = force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s². 1 kg-wt ≈ 9.8 N.

## Friction and circular motion
- **Friction (ঘর্ষণ)** opposes motion. Static friction > sliding friction > rolling friction. That is why **ball bearings / wheels** reduce friction. Lubricants (oil, grease) also reduce it.
- Friction is needed for walking and for brakes; we slip on ice/banana peel because friction is low.
- **Centripetal force (কেন্দ্রমুখী বল)** keeps a body in circular motion, directed toward the centre. Roads and railway tracks are **banked** at curves to provide it. A cyclist leans inward on a turn.
- A **washing-machine dryer / cream separator** works on the "centrifugal" effect.

## Gravitation (মহাকর্ষ ও অভিকর্ষ) 🔥
- **Newton's law of universal gravitation**: F = G m₁m₂ / d². Force ∝ product of masses, ∝ 1/(distance)².
- **G (universal gravitational constant) = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²**; it is the same everywhere. It was first measured by **Henry Cavendish (1798)**.
- **Gravity (অভিকর্ষ)** = Earth's pull on a body. **g (acceleration due to gravity) ≈ 9.8 m/s²** (9.81) at Earth's surface.
- **Mass (ভর)** is constant everywhere; **weight (ওজন) = mg** changes with g. Weight is a force (unit N); mass unit kg.

| Where | Value of g / weight |
|---|---|
| **Poles (মেরু)** | **Maximum** g (Earth is flattened at poles, radius smaller) |
| **Equator (বিষুবরেখা)** | **Minimum** g on the surface |
| **Centre of the Earth** | **g = 0**, weight zero |
| Going up (height) or down (depth) | g decreases |
| **Moon** | g ≈ **1/6** of Earth's → weight on Moon is **1/6** of that on Earth; mass is the same |
| Freely falling lift / orbiting spacecraft | **Weightlessness (ওজনহীনতা)** |

- **Galileo**: in vacuum, all bodies fall with the **same acceleration** regardless of mass (a feather and a coin fall together in vacuum). He is said to have tested it at the Leaning Tower of Pisa.
- Lift going **up with acceleration** → apparent weight increases; **going down with acceleration** → decreases; **free fall** → zero.

## Escape velocity and satellites 🔥
| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| **Escape velocity (মুক্তিবেগ) of Earth** | **11.2 km/s** |
| Escape velocity of Moon | ≈ 2.4 km/s (that is why the Moon has no atmosphere) |
| **Orbital velocity** near Earth's surface | ≈ **7.9 km/s** (≈ 8 km/s) |
| **Geostationary (ভূস্থির) satellite** height | ≈ **36,000 km** (35,786 km) above the equator; period **24 hours**; used for communication and TV. **Bangabandhu Satellite-1** (launched 12 May 2018) is geostationary at **119.1° E** |

## Kepler's laws of planetary motion
Johannes **Kepler** (1609–1619):
1. Planets move in **elliptical orbits** with the Sun at one **focus**.
2. The line joining planet and Sun sweeps **equal areas in equal times** (planet moves faster near the Sun).
3. **T² ∝ r³** (square of the period ∝ cube of mean distance).

## Pressure and floating (quick facts)
- **Pascal's law** → hydraulic press, hydraulic brake, hydraulic lift.
- **Archimedes' principle** → buoyancy; a floating body displaces its own weight of liquid (law of floatation). Ships float, ice floats (ice density < water).
- **Bernoulli's principle** → aeroplane lift, atomizer/sprayer.

## Quick revision
- Newton's 1st law = **law of inertia**; mass measures inertia.
- **F = ma** (2nd law); **rocket works on the 3rd law** / conservation of momentum.
- **g = 9.8 m/s²**; G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (Cavendish).
- g is **maximum at the poles**, minimum at the equator, **zero at Earth's centre**.
- Weight on the Moon is **1/6** of Earth weight; mass remains same.
- **Escape velocity 11.2 km/s**; geostationary satellite ≈ 36,000 km, period 24 h.
- Velocity, force, momentum, displacement are vectors; speed, mass, work are scalars.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** A passenger falls forward when a moving bus stops suddenly. This is due to —
(a) inertia of rest (b) inertia of motion (c) Newton's third law (d) friction

**2.** Rocket propulsion is based on —
(a) Newton's first law (b) Newton's law of gravitation (c) Newton's third law (d) Kepler's second law

**3.** The escape velocity from the Earth's surface is about —
(a) 7.9 km/s (b) 9.8 km/s (c) 2.4 km/s (d) 11.2 km/s

**4.** Where is the value of g maximum on the Earth's surface?
(a) At the poles (b) At the equator (c) At the Tropic of Cancer (d) Same everywhere

**5.** Which of the following is a vector quantity?
(a) Speed (b) Mass (c) Momentum (d) Work

**6.** If a person's mass is 60 kg on Earth, his mass on the Moon will be —
(a) 10 kg (b) 60 kg (c) 360 kg (d) zero

**7.** Who first measured the universal gravitational constant G?
(a) Newton (b) Galileo (c) Kepler (d) Cavendish

**8.** A geostationary satellite completes one revolution around the Earth in —
(a) 12 hours (b) 24 hours (c) 27.3 days (d) 90 minutes

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | The body tends to keep moving: inertia of motion (1st law) |
| 2 | c | Action (gas pushed back) and reaction (rocket forward) |
| 3 | d | Earth's escape velocity ≈ 11.2 km/s |
| 4 | a | Earth's radius is smallest at the poles, so g is greatest |
| 5 | c | Momentum p = mv has direction |
| 6 | b | Mass does not change; only weight becomes 1/6 |
| 7 | d | Henry Cavendish, 1798 |
| 8 | b | Period equals Earth's rotation, 24 hours |
