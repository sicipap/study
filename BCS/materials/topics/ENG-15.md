# Adverb

> **Why it matters:** Exams test adjective vs adverb confusion (good/well, hard/hardly, late/lately), the position of "only", "enough", "too … to", "very/much", and inversion after negative adverbs ("Never have I seen").

## What an adverb does
An **adverb** modifies a **verb, an adjective, another adverb** or a whole sentence.
- "He runs **fast**." (verb) — "She is **very** beautiful." (adjective) — "He spoke **quite** clearly." (adverb) — "**Fortunately**, nobody was hurt." (sentence)

## Kinds of adverb
| Kind | Answers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | when? | now, then, today, soon, already, yet, ago, lately |
| **Place** | where? | here, there, everywhere, inside, above |
| **Manner** | how? | quickly, slowly, well, bravely, carefully |
| **Frequency** | how often? | always, often, never, seldom, rarely, sometimes, once |
| **Degree / quantity** | how much? | very, too, quite, almost, enough, fully, rather |
| **Affirmation / negation** | | yes, certainly, surely, no, not, never |
| **Reason** | why? | therefore, hence, so |
| **Interrogative** | | when? where? how? why? |
| **Relative** | joins clauses | "This is the place **where** he was born." |

## Adjective or adverb? 🔥
| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **hard** | with effort | "He works **hard**." |
| **hardly** | scarcely, almost not | "He **hardly** works." (= he does little work) |
| **late** | not on time | "He came **late**." |
| **lately** | recently | "I have not seen him **lately**." |
| **near** | close by | "He lives **near**." |
| **nearly** | almost | "I **nearly** missed the bus." |
| **high / highly** | height / very much | "The kite flew **high**." "He is **highly** educated." |
| **most / mostly** | superlative / mainly | "The guests were **mostly** students." |
| **good** (adj) / **well** (adv) | | "He is a **good** singer; he sings **well**." "I am **well**" (healthy — here *well* is an adjective). |

- **Linking verbs** (be, seem, look, feel, smell, taste, sound, appear, become, grow) take an **adjective**, not an adverb 🔥: "The rose **smells sweet**" (not *sweetly*). "She **looks sad**." "I **feel bad**." "The food **tastes good**."
- Same form as adjective and adverb: **fast, early, daily, straight, far, long, enough**. "**Fastly**" does not exist.

## Very / much / too / enough 🔥
- **very** + positive adjective/present participle: "**very** good", "**very** interesting".
- **much** + comparative/past participle: "**much better**", "**much** surprised", "**much** obliged".
- **too** = more than needed (negative sense): "The tea is **too hot to drink**." Don't use *too* for *very*: "He is **very** kind" (not *too kind* as praise).
- **too … to** = so … that … not: "He is **too weak to walk**" = "He is **so weak that he cannot walk**."
- **enough** comes **after** an adjective/adverb but **before** a noun: "He is **old enough** to vote." "We have **enough money**."
- **so … that**: "He is **so** tired **that** he can't work."

## Position of adverbs
- **Frequency adverbs** go **before the main verb** but **after "be"** and after the first auxiliary: "He **always** comes late." "He **is always** late." "I **have never** seen a tiger."
- **only** stands **next to the word it modifies** 🔥:
  - "**Only** Rahim passed." (no one else)
  - "Rahim **only** passed." / "Rahim passed **only** in English." — meaning changes with position.
- Order at the end of a sentence: **manner – place – time** (MPT): "He worked **hard at the office yesterday**."
- **Inversion after negative adverbs** at the start 🔥: **never, seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely, not only, nowhere, little, at no time, on no account** → auxiliary before subject:
  - "**Never have I** seen such a sight."
  - "**Seldom does he** come here."
  - "**Little did he** know the danger."
  - "**Not only did he** help me, but he also lent me money."

## Other points
- **ago** is used with past simple; **before** with past perfect: "He left two years **ago**." "He **had** left two years **before**."
- **already** (affirmative) vs **yet** (negative/question): "I have **already** eaten." "Has he come **yet**?"
- **hardly/scarcely/barely** are negatives: don't add *not* ("I can **hardly** hear you", not *can't hardly*).
- **ever** after superlatives and in questions; **never** is negative.

## Common mistakes
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| He sings very good. | He sings very **well**. |
| The flower smells sweetly. | The flower smells **sweet**. |
| I am very much happy. | I am **very** happy. |
| He is enough strong. | He is **strong enough**. |
| He can't hardly walk. | He can **hardly** walk. |
| Never I have seen him. | Never **have I** seen him. |
| He is very much taller than me. | He is **much** taller than I. |
| He comes always late. | He **always comes** late. |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 **hardly** = almost not; **lately** = recently; **nearly** = almost.
- 🔥 Linking verbs (**look, smell, taste, feel, seem**) + adjective.
- 🔥 **very** + positive; **much** + comparative/past participle.
- **enough** after an adjective, before a noun.
- **too … to** = so … that … not.
- Negative adverb first → **inversion**: "Never have I…".
- Frequency adverbs: before main verb, after "be".

## Practice MCQ
**1.** "She sings ___."
(a) good (b) well (c) goodly (d) nicely good

**2.** "The rose smells ___."
(a) sweetly (b) sweetness (c) sweet (d) more sweetly

**3.** "He is ___ better today."
(a) very (b) too (c) so (d) much

**4.** "I have not met him ___."
(a) lately (b) late (c) latter (d) latest

**5.** "Never ___ such a beautiful place."
(a) I have seen (b) have I seen (c) I saw (d) I had seen

**6.** Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is enough tall. (b) He is tall enough. (c) He enough is tall. (d) He is enoughly tall.

**7.** "He is too weak to walk" means —
(a) He is weak but can walk. (b) He walks weakly. (c) He is so weak that he cannot walk. (d) He is weak because he walks.

**8.** "He works hard" — here "hard" is a/an —
(a) noun (b) adjective (c) preposition (d) adverb

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | well is the adverb of manner |
| 2 | c | Linking verb "smells" + adjective |
| 3 | d | much + comparative |
| 4 | a | lately = recently |
| 5 | b | Inversion after a negative adverb |
| 6 | b | enough comes after an adjective |
| 7 | c | too … to = so … that … not |
| 8 | d | It modifies the verb "works" |
