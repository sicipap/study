# Electricity & magnetism

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask Ohm's law, units, which metal/alloy is used in filament, heater and fuse, household supply (220 V, 50 Hz), AC/DC, transformer and generator principles, and magnetic materials.

## Charge and current 🔥
- **Charge (আধান)** unit **coulomb (C)**. Charge of one electron = **−1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C**. Like charges repel, unlike attract (**Coulomb's law**: F ∝ q₁q₂/r²).
- **Current (তড়িৎ প্রবাহ)** I = Q/t, unit **ampere (A)**. Conventional current flows from + to −; **electrons flow from − to +**.
- **Potential difference (বিভব পার্থক্য)** unit **volt (V)**; measured by a **voltmeter (connected in parallel)**. Current is measured by an **ammeter (connected in series)**. A **galvanometer** detects small currents.

## Ohm's law and resistance 🔥🔥
- **Ohm's law** (Georg Simon **Ohm**, 1827): at constant temperature, **V = IR**.
- **Resistance (রোধ)** unit **ohm (Ω)**. R = ρL/A — increases with **length**, decreases with **cross-sectional area**, depends on **material** and **temperature** (for metals it rises with temperature).
- **Series:** R = R₁ + R₂ + … (same current). **Parallel:** 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + … (same voltage; total resistance smaller).
- **Household wiring is in parallel**, so each appliance gets full voltage and works independently.
- Electrical power **P = VI = I²R = V²/R**; energy in **kWh** (1 unit).

| Material | Type / use |
|---|---|
| **Silver** | **Best conductor** of electricity (and heat) |
| **Copper**, aluminium | Wires and cables (copper cheaper than silver) |
| **Tungsten** | **Bulb filament** — very high melting point (≈ 3,400 °C) |
| **Nichrome** (Ni + Cr) | **Heater / iron coil** — high resistance, doesn't oxidise |
| **Fuse wire: tin + lead alloy** | **Low melting point**; placed **in series in the live wire**; melts when current is excessive |
| Rubber, glass, plastic, dry wood, mica | **Insulators** (অন্তরক) |
| **Silicon (Si), germanium (Ge)** | **Semiconductors** — diode, transistor, IC, solar cell |
| Superconductor | **Zero resistance** at very low temperature (discovered by **Kamerlingh Onnes, 1911**, in mercury) |

- A bulb is filled with an **inert gas (argon/nitrogen)** so the filament does not burn/evaporate quickly.
- **Semiconductor doping**: adding pentavalent impurity (P, As) → **n-type**; trivalent (B, Al, Ga) → **p-type**.
- **Diode** acts as a **rectifier (AC → DC)**. **Transistor** (1947, Bell Labs: **Bardeen, Brattain, Shockley**) amplifies and switches.

## Cells and batteries 🔥
| Cell | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Voltaic pile** | First battery, **Alessandro Volta (1800)** |
| **Dry cell (Leclanché)** | Zinc case (−), **carbon rod (+)**, MnO₂ and **ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl)** paste; **1.5 V** |
| **Lead-acid (storage) battery** | Car/IPS battery; electrolyte **dilute sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)**; **2 V per cell** (12 V battery = 6 cells); rechargeable |
| **Lithium-ion** | Mobile phones, laptops, EVs (Nobel Chemistry 2019: Goodenough, Whittingham, Yoshino) |

- A battery converts **chemical energy → electrical energy** and gives **DC**.

## AC, DC and supply in Bangladesh 🔥
- **DC (direct current)**: flows one way (battery, solar panel). **AC (alternating current)**: changes direction periodically (power grid).
- **Bangladesh household supply: 220 V, 50 Hz AC** (USA: 110–120 V, 60 Hz).
- **Transformer** changes AC voltage (**step-up / step-down**); works **only on AC**, on the principle of **mutual induction**. Power is transmitted at **high voltage** to reduce loss (I²R).
- **Earthing**: the earth wire (green / green-yellow) protects against shock. A **circuit breaker** also protects circuits.
- **Lightning conductor** was invented by **Benjamin Franklin** (he also showed lightning is electricity, kite experiment, 1752).

## Electromagnetism 🔥
| Discovery | Scientist |
|---|---|
| **Current produces a magnetic field** (1820) | **Hans Christian Oersted** |
| **Electromagnetic induction** (1831) — basis of **generator/dynamo** and transformer | **Michael Faraday** |
| Direction of induced current (Lenz's law) | Heinrich Lenz |
| Electromagnetic theory of light | James Clerk Maxwell |
| Motor rule / generator rule | Fleming's **left-hand rule (motor)**, **right-hand rule (generator)** |

- **Generator**: mechanical → electrical (electromagnetic induction). **Motor**: electrical → mechanical.
- **Electromagnet**: coil around a **soft iron** core; magnetism lasts only while current flows. Used in electric bells, cranes, relays, MRI, loudspeakers.

## Magnetism (চুম্বকত্ব)
- **Natural magnet: magnetite / lodestone (Fe₃O₄)**.
- **Ferromagnetic (strongly attracted)**: **iron, cobalt, nickel** (and their alloys). Copper, gold, wood are non-magnetic.
- **Permanent magnets**: **steel, alnico** (Al-Ni-Co), neodymium magnets. **Temporary**: soft iron.
- Like poles repel, unlike attract; **repulsion is the sure test** of a magnet.
- A freely suspended magnet (compass) points **north–south**. The Earth behaves like a giant magnet; Earth's magnetic poles are not exactly at the geographic poles (angle between them = **declination**).
- Heating above the **Curie temperature** destroys ferromagnetism; hammering also weakens a magnet.
- Units: magnetic flux **weber (Wb)**; magnetic flux density **tesla (T)**.
- **Maglev trains** use magnetic levitation.

## Quick revision
- **V = IR** (Ohm); unit of resistance ohm, of current ampere.
- Filament **tungsten**; heater **nichrome**; fuse **tin–lead** (low melting point), in series.
- **Silver** is the best conductor; **copper** used in wiring.
- Bangladesh supply: **220 V, 50 Hz AC**; house wiring in **parallel**.
- **Transformer works only on AC**; generator: **Faraday's electromagnetic induction** (1831).
- Dry cell **1.5 V**; lead-acid cell **2 V** with dilute H₂SO₄.
- Oersted: current → magnetism (1820); Franklin: lightning conductor.
- Iron, cobalt, nickel are ferromagnetic; natural magnet = magnetite (Fe₃O₄).
