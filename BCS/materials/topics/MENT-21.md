# Mechanical: gears, pulleys, levers

> **Why it matters:** Mechanical reasoning questions (common in defence, police, NSI and some bank and BCS papers) ask which way a gear turns, how fast it turns, what class a lever is, and how much effort a pulley needs. A few rules cover them all.

## Gears 🔥
| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Two meshing gears turn in opposite directions** | A clockwise → B anticlockwise |
| **Gears in a chain alternate** | 1st, 3rd, 5th … turn the **same** way; 2nd, 4th, 6th … turn the **opposite** way |
| **Idler gear** | A gear placed between two others makes them turn the same way; it does not change the speed ratio |
| **Speed and teeth** | **Teeth × rpm is the same for meshing gears: T₁ × N₁ = T₂ × N₂** |
| Small gear drives big gear | The big gear turns **slower** but with **more turning force (torque)** |
| Gears on the **same shaft** | Turn in the same direction at the same speed |

## Belts and chains 🔥
- **Open (straight) belt:** both wheels turn in the **same direction**.
- **Crossed belt:** wheels turn in **opposite directions**.
- A bicycle chain links two sprockets, so they turn the **same** way.
- Speed rule for belts: **diameter × rpm is the same** for both wheels.

## Pulleys 🔥
| Pulley system | Mechanical advantage (MA, ideal) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Single fixed pulley** | **1** | Only changes the **direction** of the force (a flagpole) |
| **Single movable pulley** | **2** | Halves the effort; you pull twice the rope length |
| **Block and tackle** | **Number of rope segments supporting the load** | 4 supporting segments → effort = load ÷ 4 |

**MA = load ÷ effort.** Work is never saved: less effort means more distance.

## Levers 🔥
A lever has a **fulcrum (F, আলম্ব)**, a **load (L, ভার)** and an **effort (E, বল/প্রযুক্ত বল)**. The class depends on what is in the **middle**: remember **"F-L-E = 1-2-3"**.
| Class | In the middle | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **First class** | **Fulcrum** | See-saw, **scissors**, **crowbar**, pliers, beam balance, hand pump handle |
| **Second class** | **Load** | **Wheelbarrow**, **nutcracker**, bottle opener, door |
| **Third class** | **Effort** | **Tongs**, **forceps / tweezers**, fishing rod, broom, **human forearm** |

- **Law of the lever:** **effort × effort arm = load × load arm**.
- **Second-class levers always have MA > 1**; **third-class levers always have MA < 1** (they gain speed and distance, not force).

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Gear A (20 teeth) turns at 30 rpm and drives gear B (60 teeth). Speed of B?
20 × 30 = 60 × N → **N = 10 rpm**.

**Example 2.** Six gears are in a row. The first turns clockwise. How does the sixth turn?
Even-numbered gears turn opposite to the first → **anticlockwise**.

**Example 3.** A load of 200 N is 0.5 m from the fulcrum. The effort arm is 2 m. What effort is needed?
E × 2 = 200 × 0.5 → **E = 50 N**.

**Example 4.** A load of 400 N is lifted by a block and tackle with 4 supporting rope segments. Ideal effort?
400 ÷ 4 = **100 N**.

**Example 5.** Why is a wheelbarrow a second-class lever?
The wheel is the fulcrum, the load is in the middle and you lift at the handles → **load in the middle**.

## Quick revision
- Meshing gears turn opposite; odd-numbered gears in a chain turn like the first.
- Teeth × rpm stays the same.
- Open belt → same direction; crossed belt → opposite.
- Fixed pulley MA 1; movable pulley MA 2; block and tackle MA = supporting segments.
- F-L-E in the middle = class 1-2-3.
- Scissors 1st; wheelbarrow and nutcracker 2nd; tongs, forceps and forearm 3rd.
- Effort × effort arm = load × load arm.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Gear A turns clockwise and meshes with gear B; gear B meshes with gear C. Which way does C turn?
(a) Clockwise (b) Anticlockwise (c) It does not move (d) Alternately both ways

**2.** A gear with 20 teeth turns at 30 rpm and drives a gear with 60 teeth. What is the speed of the larger gear?
(a) 90 rpm (b) 10 rpm (c) 20 rpm (d) 30 rpm

**3.** Two wheels are connected by a crossed belt. How do they turn?
(a) In the same direction (b) At twice the speed (c) In opposite directions (d) They do not move

**4.** A wheelbarrow is an example of which class of lever?
(a) First (b) Third (c) None (d) Second

**5.** Tongs and forceps are levers of which class?
(a) Third (b) First (c) Second (d) None

**6.** A load of 200 N is 0.5 m from the fulcrum and the effort arm is 2 m. What effort is needed to balance it?
(a) 100 N (b) 50 N (c) 400 N (d) 25 N

**7.** What is the ideal mechanical advantage of a single movable pulley?
(a) 1 (b) 4 (c) 2 (d) 0.5

**8.** Six gears are arranged in a row, each meshing with the next. The first turns clockwise. How does the sixth turn?
(a) Clockwise (b) It stops (c) Faster clockwise (d) Anticlockwise

**9.** A see-saw is an example of which class of lever?
(a) First (b) Second (c) Third (d) Compound

**10.** A load of 400 N is lifted with a block and tackle that has 4 rope segments supporting the load. What is the ideal effort?
(a) 400 N (b) 200 N (c) 100 N (d) 50 N

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a | A clockwise, B anticlockwise, C clockwise. |
| 2 | b | 20 × 30 = 60 × N, so N = 10 rpm. |
| 3 | c | A crossed belt reverses the direction. |
| 4 | d | The load is in the middle: second class. |
| 5 | a | The effort is in the middle: third class. |
| 6 | b | E × 2 = 200 × 0.5, so E = 50 N. |
| 7 | c | A movable pulley is supported by two rope segments. |
| 8 | d | Even-numbered gears turn opposite to the first. |
| 9 | a | The fulcrum is in the middle: first class. |
| 10 | c | 400 ÷ 4 = 100 N. |
