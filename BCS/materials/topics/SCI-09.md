# Matter, atomic structure, periodic table

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask states of matter, sublimation examples, physical vs chemical change, atomic models and their authors, electron configuration, and periodic-table facts (Mendeleev, groups, noble gases, most abundant elements, liquid elements).

## Matter and its states (পদার্থ ও তার অবস্থা) 🔥
- Matter has mass and occupies space. Three common states: **solid, liquid, gas**. **Plasma** is called the **4th state** (Sun, stars, lightning, neon sign). **Bose–Einstein condensate** is called the **5th state** (predicted by S. N. Bose & Einstein; made in 1995).
- **Sublimation (ঊর্ধ্বপাতন)**: solid → gas directly. Examples: **camphor (কর্পূর), naphthalene, iodine, ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl), dry ice (solid CO₂)**.
- **Physical change (ভৌত পরিবর্তন)**: no new substance, usually reversible — melting of ice, boiling water, dissolving salt/sugar, magnetising iron, making a solution.
- **Chemical change (রাসায়নিক পরিবর্তন)**: new substance forms — **rusting of iron, burning of paper/candle wax (burning), curdling of milk, digestion of food, photosynthesis, ripening of fruit**.
  - Candle burning shows both: melting wax (physical), burning wax (chemical).
- **Element (মৌল)**: one kind of atom. **Compound (যৌগ)**: elements combined in fixed ratio (water, salt). **Mixture (মিশ্রণ)**: no fixed ratio (air, sea water, soil, alloy like brass).

## Atomic models 🔥
| Model / idea | Scientist | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| "Atomos" (indivisible) | **Democritus** (Greek) | First idea of atoms |
| **Atomic theory (1803)** | **John Dalton** | Atoms are indivisible; elements combine in simple ratios |
| **Plum-pudding model** | **J. J. Thomson** (1904) | Electrons embedded in positive sphere |
| **Nuclear / solar-system model (1911)** | **Ernest Rutherford** | Tiny, dense, positive **nucleus**; most of the atom is empty space (gold-foil experiment) |
| **Bohr model (1913)** | **Niels Bohr** | Electrons move in **fixed orbits (energy levels)**; energy emitted/absorbed only when jumping between them |
| Quantum/wave model | Schrödinger, Heisenberg (uncertainty principle) | Orbitals |

- Rutherford's model failed to explain atomic **stability**; Bohr fixed this.
- **Electron configuration**: max electrons in a shell = **2n²** (K = 2, L = 8, M = 18, N = 32). Examples: Na (11) = 2, 8, 1; Cl (17) = 2, 8, 7; Ca (20) = 2, 8, 8, 2.
- **Valency (যোজনী)**: combining capacity, based on outer-shell electrons. **Noble gases have valency 0** (stable octet; helium has a duplet).
- **Avogadro's number = 6.022 × 10²³** particles per **mole**. Molar volume of gas at STP = **22.4 litres**.
- **Ion**: charged atom — cation (+, lost electrons), anion (−, gained electrons).

## The periodic table (পর্যায় সারণি) 🔥🔥
| Step | Scientist |
|---|---|
| Law of triads | Döbereiner (1829) |
| Law of octaves | Newlands (1864) |
| **First periodic table (1869)**, based on **atomic mass**; left gaps for undiscovered elements | **Dmitri Mendeleev** (Russia) — **father of the periodic table** |
| **Modern periodic law (1913)**: properties depend on **atomic number** | **Henry Moseley** |

- **Modern table: 7 periods (horizontal rows, পর্যায়) and 18 groups (vertical columns, গ্রুপ)**. **118 elements** are known; about **92–94 occur naturally**.
- Newest elements (named 2016): **113 Nihonium (Nh), 115 Moscovium (Mc), 117 Tennessine (Ts), 118 Oganesson (Og)**.
- **Metals on the left**, **non-metals on the right**, **metalloids** along the zigzag line (**B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te**). **Lanthanides and actinides** are placed separately at the bottom (f-block).

| Group | Name | Members / facts |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Alkali metals** | Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr — very reactive, kept under **kerosene** |
| **2** | **Alkaline earth metals** | Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra |
| 3–12 | **Transition metals** | Fe, Cu, Zn, Ag, Au, Hg … |
| **17** | **Halogens** ("salt-formers") | **F, Cl, Br, I, At** — **fluorine** is the most reactive non-metal and **most electronegative element** |
| **18** | **Noble / inert gases** | **He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn** — Rn is radioactive |

## Record-holding elements 🔥
| Fact | Element |
|---|---|
| **Most abundant in the universe**; lightest element | **Hydrogen** |
| **Most abundant in Earth's crust** | **Oxygen** (≈46%), then **silicon** |
| **Most abundant metal in the crust** | **Aluminium**, then iron |
| **Most abundant in the atmosphere** | **Nitrogen** (≈78%) |
| Most abundant in the human body (by mass) | **Oxygen**, then carbon |
| **Liquid at room temperature** | **Mercury** (metal) and **bromine** (non-metal) |
| **Lightest metal** | **Lithium** |
| **Densest element** | **Osmium** |
| **Hardest natural substance** | **Diamond** (a form of carbon) |
| **Best conductor** of electricity | Silver |
| Metal with lowest melting point | Mercury (−39 °C) |
| Highest melting point metal | Tungsten |

## Uses of noble gases
- **Helium**: filling balloons/airships (light, non-flammable); with O₂ for **deep-sea divers**; liquid He for cooling (MRI magnets).
- **Neon**: red advertising signs. **Argon**: filling electric bulbs, welding. **Xenon**: photographic flash, car headlamps. **Radon**: cancer radiotherapy (radioactive).

## Quick revision
- Plasma = **4th state**; BEC = 5th state (S. N. Bose).
- **Sublimation**: camphor, naphthalene, iodine, NH₄Cl, dry ice.
- **Rusting and burning are chemical changes**; melting and dissolving are physical.
- Nucleus: **Rutherford (1911)**; fixed orbits: **Bohr (1913)**; atomic theory: **Dalton (1803)**.
- Shell capacity **2n²**; Avogadro's number **6.022 × 10²³**.
- **Mendeleev (1869)** — atomic mass; **Moseley** — atomic number.
- **7 periods, 18 groups, 118 elements**; group 17 halogens, 18 noble gases.
- Liquid elements: **mercury and bromine**; crust: **O > Si > Al**.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Who is known as the father of the periodic table?
(a) Moseley (b) Mendeleev (c) Dalton (d) Newlands

**2.** Which of the following sublimes on heating?
(a) Sugar (b) Common salt (c) Camphor (d) Wax

**3.** Which non-metal is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Bromine (b) Mercury (c) Iodine (d) Chlorine

**4.** The most abundant element in the Earth's crust is —
(a) silicon (b) aluminium (c) iron (d) oxygen

**5.** Who proposed that electrons revolve in fixed orbits (energy levels)?
(a) Rutherford (b) Thomson (c) Niels Bohr (d) Dalton

**6.** How many groups are there in the modern periodic table?
(a) 7 (b) 18 (c) 8 (d) 16

**7.** Which of the following is a chemical change?
(a) Rusting of iron (b) Melting of ice (c) Dissolving sugar in water (d) Magnetising iron

**8.** Which is called the fourth state of matter?
(a) Liquid crystal (b) Bose–Einstein condensate (c) Vapour (d) Plasma

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Mendeleev made the first periodic table in 1869 |
| 2 | c | Camphor changes directly from solid to vapour |
| 3 | a | Bromine is the only liquid non-metal; mercury is a metal |
| 4 | d | Oxygen ≈ 46% of the crust |
| 5 | c | Bohr model, 1913 |
| 6 | b | 18 groups and 7 periods |
| 7 | a | Rust (hydrated iron oxide) is a new substance |
| 8 | d | Plasma is the 4th state; BEC is the 5th |
