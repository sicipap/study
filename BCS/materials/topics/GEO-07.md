# Climate change, global warming, ozone

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the greenhouse gases, the main greenhouse gas, the Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement years, IPCC facts, where COP meetings were held, the ozone layer and CFCs, and Bangladesh's climate vulnerability. This topic is also central in BCS written papers.

## Greenhouse effect and global warming 🔥
- **Greenhouse effect (গ্রিনহাউস প্রভাব):** some gases in the atmosphere let sunlight in but **trap the heat (infrared radiation)** that Earth gives off, keeping the planet warm. Without it, Earth would be about **−18 °C** on average instead of about **15 °C**.
- **Global warming (বৈশ্বিক উষ্ণায়ন):** the rise in Earth's average temperature because humans add extra greenhouse gases, mainly by **burning fossil fuels** (coal, oil, gas) and **deforestation**.
- **Joseph Fourier** described the effect (1820s); **Svante Arrhenius** (1896) first calculated how CO₂ warms Earth.

### Greenhouse gases 🔥🔥
| Gas | Main sources | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 🔥 **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** | Burning fossil fuels, deforestation, cement | **Largest contributor** to human-caused warming |
| 🔥 **Methane (CH₄)** | **Paddy fields**, livestock, landfills, gas leaks | Far more powerful per molecule than CO₂ (about 28 times over 100 years) |
| **Nitrous oxide (N₂O)** | **Chemical fertiliser**, industry | |
| **CFCs, HFCs, PFCs** | Refrigerators, ACs, aerosols | Very powerful; CFCs also destroy ozone |
| **Sulphur hexafluoride (SF₆)** | Electrical equipment | **Highest global warming potential** |
| **Water vapour** | Natural evaporation | **Most abundant** greenhouse gas (a feedback, not directly controlled) |

- **Kyoto Protocol's six gases:** CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs, PFCs, SF₆.
- Earth has warmed by about **1.1–1.2 °C** above pre-industrial (1850–1900) levels; **2024** was the **warmest year on record** and the first calendar year about **1.5 °C** above pre-industrial levels (WMO).

> 🔄 Update: temperature records and CO₂ levels are broken almost every year; check the latest WMO report.

### Effects
- **Sea-level rise** (melting glaciers and ice sheets, thermal expansion of sea water), more intense **cyclones**, floods and droughts, heatwaves, **salinity intrusion**, coral bleaching, loss of species, **climate refugees**.
- **Mitigation (প্রশমন)** = reducing emissions (renewable energy, forests). **Adaptation (অভিযোজন)** = adjusting to impacts (embankments, saline-tolerant rice, floating gardens).
- **Carbon sink:** absorbs CO₂ (forests, oceans). **Net zero / carbon neutral:** emissions balanced by removals. **Carbon credit:** a permit to emit one tonne of CO₂ that can be traded.

## Global climate institutions and agreements 🔥🔥
| Item | Key facts |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **IPCC** (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change) | Set up in **1988** by **WMO and UNEP**; HQ **Geneva**; shared the **2007 Nobel Peace Prize** with **Al Gore**. Latest full cycle: **Sixth Assessment Report (AR6), 2021–2023** |
| 🔥 **UNFCCC** (UN Framework Convention on Climate Change) | Adopted at the **Rio Earth Summit, 1992**; in force **1994**; secretariat in **Bonn, Germany**. Its yearly meeting is the **COP** (Conference of the Parties); **COP1: Berlin, 1995** |
| 🔥 **Kyoto Protocol** | Adopted at **COP3, Kyoto (Japan), 1997**; in force **2005**; **binding emission cuts for developed (Annex I) countries**; introduced carbon trading and the **Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)** |
| 🔥 **Paris Agreement** | Adopted at **COP21, Paris, December 2015**; in force **4 November 2016**. Goal: keep warming **well below 2 °C** and try to limit it to **1.5 °C**; every country submits **NDCs** (Nationally Determined Contributions) |

### Recent COPs
| COP | Place | Year | Key outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| COP26 | **Glasgow**, UK | 2021 | Glasgow Climate Pact; "phase down" coal |
| COP27 | **Sharm el-Sheikh**, Egypt | 2022 | 🔥 Agreement to set up a **Loss and Damage Fund** |
| COP28 | **Dubai**, UAE | 2023 | Loss and Damage Fund made operational; first call to "**transition away from fossil fuels**" |
| COP29 | **Baku**, Azerbaijan | 2024 | New climate-finance goal: at least **US$300 billion a year by 2035** for developing countries |
| COP30 | **Belém**, Brazil | 2025 | Held in the Amazon region |

## Bangladesh and climate change 🔥
- Bangladesh is one of the **most climate-vulnerable countries** because of its low, flat delta, dense population and cyclone-prone coast.
- A **1 m sea-level rise** could flood about **17–18%** of Bangladesh's land (the commonly cited estimate).
- **BCCSAP** (Bangladesh Climate Change Strategy and Action Plan): **2009** — among the first such plans in developing countries.
- **Bangladesh Climate Change Trust Fund** (from the country's own budget): set up in **2009–10** under the **Climate Change Trust Act 2010**.
- **National Adaptation Plan (NAP) 2023–2050**: submitted to the UNFCCC in **2022**.
- Bangladesh has chaired the **Climate Vulnerable Forum (CVF)**.
- Adaptation examples: **floating gardens (ভাসমান বাগান)**, saline-tolerant rice (e.g. BRRI varieties), cyclone shelters, raised homesteads.

## Ozone layer (ওজোন স্তর) 🔥🔥
- **Ozone (O₃)** is a molecule of **three oxygen atoms**. The **ozone layer** is in the **stratosphere** (about **15–35 km**).
- It **absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV-B and UV-C) rays** that cause **skin cancer, cataracts** and crop damage.
- Measured in **Dobson Units (DU)**. An **"ozone hole"** means ozone below **220 DU**.
- 🔥 The **ozone hole over Antarctica** was reported in **1985** by scientists of the **British Antarctic Survey**. It is worst in the southern **spring (September–October)**.
- 🔥 **Main ozone-depleting substances:** **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)** — used in **refrigerators, air conditioners, aerosol sprays, foam**; also **halons** (fire extinguishers), carbon tetrachloride, methyl bromide. One chlorine atom can destroy thousands of ozone molecules.
- **Mario Molina, Sherwood Rowland and Paul Crutzen** won the **1995 Nobel Prize in Chemistry** for their work on ozone depletion.
- **Ground-level ozone** is a harmful **air pollutant** (part of smog), unlike stratospheric ozone.

| Treaty | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Vienna Convention** for the Protection of the Ozone Layer | **1985** | Framework treaty |
| 🔥 **Montreal Protocol** | **16 September 1987** (in force 1989) | **Phases out CFCs and other ozone-depleting substances**; the first UN treaty ratified by **every UN member state**; seen as the most successful environmental treaty |
| **Kigali Amendment** | **2016** (Kigali, Rwanda) | Phases **down HFCs** (which don't harm ozone but are strong greenhouse gases) |

- **16 September = International Day for the Preservation of the Ozone Layer.**
- The ozone layer is **recovering**; it is expected to return to 1980 levels around the middle of this century.

## Important days
**5 June — World Environment Day** (first held **1974**, set up after the 1972 Stockholm Conference); **22 April — Earth Day**; **16 September — Ozone Day**; **23 March — World Meteorological Day**.

## Quick revision
- Main human greenhouse gas: **CO₂**; methane from **paddy fields and livestock**; most abundant GHG: **water vapour**.
- IPCC: **1988**, WMO + UNEP, Nobel **2007**.
- UNFCCC: **Rio 1992**; Kyoto Protocol: **1997** (in force 2005); Paris Agreement: **2015** (in force 2016).
- Paris goal: **well below 2 °C, aim 1.5 °C**; countries submit **NDCs**.
- Loss and Damage Fund agreed at **COP27 (Sharm el-Sheikh, 2022)**.
- Ozone layer: **stratosphere**, absorbs **UV**; unit **Dobson**.
- CFCs destroy ozone; **Montreal Protocol 1987**; **Kigali Amendment 2016** (HFCs).
- BCCSAP **2009**; NAP **2023–2050**.
