# Statement & conclusion / assumption

> **Why it matters:** These critical-reasoning questions give a short statement (a notice, advertisement, news item or remark) and ask which conclusions follow or which assumptions are implicit (অন্তর্নিহিত). They test careful reading, not general knowledge.

## Key definitions 🔥
| Term | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Conclusion (সিদ্ধান্ত)** | Something that **follows directly** from the statement | "If the statement is true, must this be true?" |
| **Assumption (অনুমান/পূর্বানুমান)** | Something **taken for granted** by the speaker **before** making the statement; it is unstated | "Would the speaker say this if he did not believe it?" |
| **Inference** | A reasonable deduction from the facts given | Must not go beyond the facts |

## Rules for conclusions
1. Use **only the information in the statement**. Do not add your own knowledge.
2. A conclusion that **simply repeats** the statement in other words follows.
3. 🔥 Conclusions with **extreme words** (always, never, only, all, none, definitely, must) usually **do not follow**, unless the statement itself uses them.
4. Words like "may", "some", "probably" are safer.
5. A conclusion **about the future** usually does not follow unless the statement supports it.

## Rules for assumptions 🔥
1. A **suggestion, advice or request** assumes that it **will be followed** and that it **will work**.
2. An **advertisement** assumes that people **read or notice** it and that it will **attract** buyers.
3. A **notice or rule** assumes that people will **obey** it and that there is a **reason** for it.
4. A decision to do something assumes it is **possible and useful**.
5. An assumption is **never stated** in the statement; it is behind it.
6. Extreme assumptions ("everyone will …", "nobody else …") are usually **not implicit**.

## Answer format (4 choices used here)
- (a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II. Some exams add "Either I or II" as a fifth choice, used when the two options are opposites and one must be true.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** Statement: "Use X toothpaste for strong and healthy teeth." (advertisement)
I. People want strong and healthy teeth. II. People are influenced by advertisements.
An advertisement assumes that people want the benefit and that the advertisement works. **Both are implicit.**

**Example 2.** Statement: "Only graduates can apply for the post."
I. All graduates will be selected. II. Non-graduates cannot apply.
I goes too far (applying is not being selected). II restates the statement. **Only II follows.**

**Example 3.** Statement: "Sumon is the best player in the team."
I. Sumon is the best player in the country. II. The team has no other good player.
Both go beyond the statement. **Neither follows.**

**Example 4.** Statement: "The government has asked citizens to save electricity during peak hours."
I. There is pressure on the electricity supply during peak hours. II. Citizens will ignore the request.
A request assumes a reason exists and that it will be followed. **Only I is implicit.**

## Quick revision
- A conclusion must follow from the statement alone.
- An assumption is unstated and taken for granted.
- Extreme words (always, only, all, never) usually make a conclusion invalid.
- Advice or requests assume they will be followed and will work.
- Advertisements assume they will be read and will attract buyers.
- Do not bring outside knowledge into the answer.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Statement: "Use X toothpaste for strong and healthy teeth." Assumptions: I. People want strong and healthy teeth. II. People are influenced by advertisements.
(a) Only I is implicit (b) Only II is implicit (c) Both I and II are implicit (d) Neither is implicit

**2.** Statement: "All the students of Class Ten passed the exam. Rina is a student of Class Ten." Conclusions: I. Rina passed the exam. II. Rina got GPA 5.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**3.** Statement: "Only graduates can apply for the post." Conclusions: I. All graduates will be selected. II. Non-graduates cannot apply.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**4.** Statement: "Sumon is the best player in the team." Conclusions: I. Sumon is the best player in the country. II. The team has no other good player.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**5.** Statement: "The government has asked citizens to save electricity during peak hours." Assumptions: I. There is pressure on the electricity supply during peak hours. II. Citizens will ignore the request.
(a) Only I is implicit (b) Only II is implicit (c) Both I and II are implicit (d) Neither is implicit

**6.** Statement: Rahim told Karim, "Take the 8 a.m. train to reach Chattogram on time." Assumptions: I. The 8 a.m. train will reach Chattogram on time. II. Karim wants to reach Chattogram on time.
(a) Only I is implicit (b) Only II is implicit (c) Both I and II are implicit (d) Neither is implicit

**7.** Statement: "Most students in the class are girls." Conclusions: I. There are no boys in the class. II. There are fewer boys than girls in the class.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither follows

**8.** Statement: "The school has decided to start a computer class for Class Eight students." Assumptions: I. All Class Eight students already know computers well. II. Computer classes are useless.
(a) Only I is implicit (b) Only II is implicit (c) Both I and II are implicit (d) Neither is implicit

**9.** Which word in a conclusion most often makes it invalid?
(a) may (b) some (c) always (d) probably

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | An advertisement assumes people want the benefit and are influenced by ads. |
| 2 | a | Passing follows; GPA 5 is not stated. |
| 3 | b | II restates the statement; applying does not mean being selected. |
| 4 | d | Both go beyond "best in the team". |
| 5 | a | A request assumes a real need; it does not assume it will be ignored. |
| 6 | c | Advice assumes it will work and that the listener wants the result. |
| 7 | b | "Most" means more than half, so boys are fewer; there may still be boys. |
| 8 | d | Starting a class assumes the class is useful, the opposite of both. |
| 9 | c | Extreme words like "always" usually go beyond the statement. |
