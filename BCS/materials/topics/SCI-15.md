# Plants & photosynthesis

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the photosynthesis equation, where oxygen comes from, which metal is in chlorophyll, plant hormones and their jobs, stomata/transpiration, modified stems and roots (potato, ginger, sweet potato), and famous botanists.

## Photosynthesis (সালোকসংশ্লেষণ) 🔥🔥
**6CO₂ + 12H₂O → (sunlight, chlorophyll) → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O**

- Green plants make **glucose** from **carbon dioxide and water** using **sunlight**; **oxygen** is released. Energy change: **light → chemical energy**.
- Takes place in the **chloroplast**, mainly in **leaves** (the **"food factory"** of the plant; mesophyll cells).
- The **oxygen released comes from water**, not from CO₂ (proved with isotope ¹⁸O by Ruben and Kamen).
- **Chlorophyll** contains **magnesium (Mg)**. It absorbs mostly **red and blue light** and **reflects green**, so leaves look green. Photosynthesis is **least in green light**.
- Two phases:
  - **Light reaction** — in the **grana (thylakoids)**; water splits (**photolysis**), O₂ and ATP/NADPH form.
  - **Dark reaction (Calvin cycle)** — in the **stroma**; CO₂ is fixed into sugar. Worked out by **Melvin Calvin** (Nobel 1961).
- Glucose is stored as **starch**. **Iodine test**: starch turns **blue-black**.
- Factors: light, CO₂, temperature, water, chlorophyll.
- Maize, sugarcane are **C₄ plants** (efficient in hot climates); rice, wheat are C₃.
- **Respiration** is the reverse: food + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + energy; it happens **day and night** in all living cells.

## Water, stomata and transport 🔥
- **Stomata (পত্ররন্ধ্র)**: tiny pores mostly on the **lower surface of leaves**, controlled by **guard cells**; used for **gas exchange** and **transpiration**.
- **Transpiration (প্রস্বেদন)**: loss of water vapour from leaves; it cools the plant and creates **"transpiration pull"** that lifts water.
- **Roots** absorb water by **osmosis** through root hairs. **Xylem** carries water up; **phloem** carries food.
- **Mangrove** plants (**Sundarbans**) have **pneumatophores (breathing roots, শ্বাসমূল)** because the mud lacks oxygen. The main tree of the Sundarbans is **Sundari (Heritiera fomes)**.

## Plant hormones (উদ্ভিদ হরমোন) 🔥
| Hormone | Main role |
|---|---|
| **Auxin** (first discovered; F. W. Went, 1928) | Growth of stem/root tip, **phototropism** (bending toward light), rooting of cuttings, seedless fruit |
| **Gibberellin** | **Stem elongation**, bolting, seed germination, larger fruit |
| **Cytokinin** | **Cell division**, delays ageing of leaves |
| **Ethylene** (a gas) | **Fruit ripening** |
| **Abscisic acid (ABA)** | **"Stress hormone"**; closes stomata, causes **dormancy and leaf fall** |

- **Florigen** is the proposed flowering hormone. **Photoperiodism**: flowering depends on day length.
- **Parthenocarpy**: fruit without fertilisation → **seedless** fruit (banana, seedless grapes).

## Reproduction and nutrition
- **Pollination (পরাগায়ন)**: transfer of pollen; by **insects** (colourful, scented flowers), **wind** (rice, maize, grasses), water, birds, animals.
- **Seed germination** needs **water, air (oxygen) and suitable temperature** (light is not essential for most seeds).
- **Nitrogen fixation**: **Rhizobium** bacteria in **root nodules of legumes** (pulses, peas, beans) fix atmospheric nitrogen. Blue-green algae (Anabaena) do this in rice fields.
- Insectivorous plants (pitcher plant, sundew, Venus flytrap) trap insects for **nitrogen**. **Cuscuta (স্বর্ণলতা)** is a **parasite**; lichen is a symbiosis of **alga + fungus**.

## Quick revision
- Photosynthesis: **CO₂ + H₂O + light → glucose + O₂**, in the **chloroplast**.
- **O₂ released comes from water**; chlorophyll contains **Mg**.
- Light reaction in **grana**; dark reaction (Calvin cycle) in **stroma**.
- Stomata: gas exchange & transpiration; Sundarbans trees have **pneumatophores**.
- **Auxin** growth/phototropism; **gibberellin** stem elongation; **cytokinin** cell division; **ethylene** ripening; **ABA** stress/leaf fall.
- **Potato & ginger = stem; sweet potato & carrot = root**; cauliflower = flower.
- Rhizobium fixes nitrogen in legumes.
- Linnaeus: binomial names; J. C. Bose: crescograph.
