# Disasters: cyclone, flood, earthquake, landslide, drought

> **Why it matters:** Bangladesh is one of the most disaster-prone countries, so exams ask the names of cyclones in different regions, the deadliest cyclones (1970, 1991, Sidr, Aila, Amphan, Remal), port signal numbers, types of floods, the Richter scale and seismic waves, and landslide/drought-prone areas.

## Hazard vs disaster
- **Hazard (দুর্যোগের ঝুঁকি/আপদ):** a natural or human-made event that *can* cause harm (e.g. a cyclone at sea).
- **Disaster (দুর্যোগ):** when a hazard seriously harms people, property and livelihoods beyond a community's ability to cope.
- **Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability** (reduced by capacity).
- **Natural disasters:** cyclone, flood, earthquake, drought, landslide, tsunami, lightning. **Human-made:** fire, building collapse, industrial accidents.
- 🔥 In Bangladesh, **lightning (বজ্রপাত)** was **declared a natural disaster in 2016**.

## Cyclone (ঘূর্ণিঝড়) 🔥🔥
- A strong **low-pressure** storm with winds **spiralling inwards** — **anticlockwise in the Northern Hemisphere**, clockwise in the Southern.
- Forms over **warm seas (about 26–27 °C or more)**; the calm centre is the **eye (চোখ)**.
- **Storm surge (জলোচ্ছ্বাস):** the rise of sea water pushed onto land by cyclone winds — the **biggest killer** in coastal Bangladesh.

| Name | Region |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Cyclone** | **Indian Ocean / Bay of Bengal** |
| 🔥 **Hurricane** | **Atlantic** and north-east Pacific (USA, Caribbean) |
| 🔥 **Typhoon** | **North-west Pacific** (China, Japan, Philippines) |
| **Willy-willy** | Old name in **Australia** |
| **Tornado** | Small, very violent twister over land (USA) |

- Cyclones in the North Indian Ocean are named from a list prepared by **13 countries** (WMO/ESCAP Panel), including Bangladesh.

**Bangladesh wind classification (BMD / IMD):** depression (31–49 km/h) → deep depression (50–61) → **cyclonic storm (62–88)** → **severe cyclonic storm (89–117)** → very severe (118–165) → extremely severe (166–220) → **super cyclonic storm (221 km/h or more)**.

### Sea-port warning signals 🔥
| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1, 2 | **Distant** cautionary / warning |
| 3, 4 | **Local** cautionary / warning |
| 5, 6, 7 | **Danger** |
| 🔥 **8, 9, 10** | **Great danger (মহাবিপদ সংকেত)** |
| **11** | **Communication failure** with the warning centre |

- River ports use a separate system of **signals 1–4**.

## Flood (বন্যা) 🔥
| Type | Where / cause |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **Flash flood (আকস্মিক বন্যা)** | **Sylhet–Sunamganj haor region** and hill areas; heavy rain in upstream hills (Meghalaya, Tripura); water rises within hours; damages **Boro rice** in April–May |
| **Monsoon / riverine flood** | Overflow of the Padma, Jamuna, Meghna in **July–September** |
| **Rain-fed flood** | Heavy local rain with poor drainage (e.g. urban waterlogging in Dhaka, Chattogram) |
| **Tidal / storm-surge flood** | Coastal areas, during cyclones and spring tides |

- 🔥 **Major floods:** **1988** (about 60% of the country flooded) and **1998** (about two-thirds flooded, lasted more than two months — the longest); also 2004, 2007, **2022** (Sylhet flash flood), **August 2024** (Feni, Cumilla, Noakhali and other eastern districts).
- Normal floods (**বর্ষা**) bring fertile silt; damaging floods are **বন্যা**.

## Earthquake (ভূমিকম্প) 🔥
- Sudden shaking of the ground caused mainly by movement along **faults / tectonic plate boundaries**.
- **Focus (hypocentre):** point inside the Earth where it starts. 🔥 **Epicentre (উপকেন্দ্র):** point on the surface **directly above the focus** — greatest damage.
- 🔥 Recorded by a **seismograph**. **Magnitude** (energy) on the **Richter scale** (Charles Richter, 1935; now mostly the **moment magnitude scale**); **intensity** (damage) on the **Modified Mercalli scale (I–XII)**. Each whole number on the Richter scale = about **10 times** larger amplitude (about 32 times more energy).

| Seismic wave | Feature |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **P-wave (primary)** | **Fastest**; arrives first; travels through **solids, liquids and gases** |
| **S-wave (secondary)** | Slower; travels **only through solids** (shows Earth's outer core is liquid) |
| **Surface (L) waves** | Slowest; **most destructive** |

- **Bangladesh risk:** highest in the **north and north-east (Sylhet, Mymensingh, Rangpur)** and the Chattogram hill region, close to the Dauki fault and the Indian–Burmese plate boundary. **Dhaka** is highly vulnerable because of dense, poorly built buildings.
- **Great Indian (Assam) earthquake of 1897** (about magnitude 8) badly hit this region.
- **Tsunami (সুনামি):** Japanese for **"harbour wave"**; giant sea waves usually caused by **undersea earthquakes**. **26 December 2004** Indian Ocean tsunami (Sumatra, magnitude about 9.1) killed over 2 lakh people; **March 2011** Japan tsunami caused the **Fukushima** nuclear accident.

## Landslide (ভূমিধস) 🔥
- Mass movement of soil and rock down a slope.
- **Prone areas:** **Chattogram, Rangamati, Bandarban, Khagrachhari, Cox's Bazar** (including Rohingya camps).
- **Causes:** **heavy monsoon rain**, **hill cutting**, deforestation, settlement on slopes.
- **11 June 2007:** Chattogram city landslides, about **127** deaths. 🔥 **13 June 2017:** Rangamati and nearby districts, about **160** deaths — the deadliest landslide disaster in Bangladesh.

## Drought (খরা) 🔥
- A long period of **very low rainfall** causing water shortage.
- **Types:** meteorological (less rain), agricultural (soil moisture too low for crops), hydrological (rivers, groundwater fall).
- 🔥 **Most drought-prone:** **north-west Bangladesh — Rajshahi, Chapainawabganj, Naogaon (Barind Tract)**, Natore, Dinajpur.
- Reduced dry-season flow of the Ganges after the **Farakka Barrage (1975)** worsened drought and salinity in the south-west.

## Other hazards in Bangladesh
**River erosion (নদীভাঙন)** — especially along the **Jamuna and Padma**; **salinity intrusion** in the south-west coast; **cold waves**, **heatwaves**, **arsenic** in groundwater, **fire**, and building collapse (**Rana Plaza, 24 April 2013**).

## Quick revision
- Cyclone (Indian Ocean), hurricane (Atlantic), typhoon (NW Pacific), willy-willy (Australia).
- Deadliest cyclone: **12 November 1970** (Bhola); **29 April 1991**; **Sidr 2007**; **Aila 2009**; **Amphan 2020**; **Remal 2024**.
- Great danger signals: **8, 9, 10**; signal 11 = communication failure.
- Flash floods: **Sylhet haor region**; worst floods: **1988, 1998**.
- Richter scale = **magnitude**; Mercalli = **intensity**; seismograph records quakes.
- P-wave fastest; surface waves most destructive.
- Deadliest landslide: **13 June 2017**, Rangamati.
- Drought: **north-west (Barind)**; lightning declared disaster in **2016**.
