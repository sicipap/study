# Data sufficiency

> **Why it matters:** Data sufficiency (তথ্য পর্যাপ্ততা) questions give a question and two statements. You do **not** need to find the answer, only decide whether the statements give **enough information** to answer it. Bank officer exams use this type often.

## The standard answer choices 🔥
Most papers use these five choices (sometimes only four of them):
| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Statement I **alone** is sufficient, but II alone is not |
| B | Statement II **alone** is sufficient, but I alone is not |
| C | **Either** I alone **or** II alone is sufficient |
| D | **Both together** are needed; neither alone is enough |
| E | **Neither**, even together, is sufficient |

In this note the options are written out in full for each question, so read them carefully.

## Step-by-step method 🔥
1. Read the **question** and decide exactly what is needed: one value, or a yes/no answer.
2. Test **statement I alone**. Forget statement II completely.
3. Test **statement II alone**. Forget statement I completely.
4. Only if **neither** works alone, test them **together**.
5. Choose the matching option.

## Key points
- **"Sufficient" means one definite answer.** For a yes/no question, a definite "no" is also sufficient.
- If a statement gives **two possible values** (e.g. x² = 16 gives x = 4 or −4), it is **not sufficient** for "What is x?"
- Do not use **outside assumptions** (like "x is positive") unless the question says so.
- Do not waste time calculating the full answer; only check that it can be found.
- Common data needed: speed needs **distance and time**; area of a rectangle needs **length and breadth** (or one side plus perimeter); total in a row needs **positions from both ends**.

## Worked examples
**Example 1.** What is x? I. 2x + 3 = 11. II. x² = 16.
I gives x = 4 (one answer). II gives 4 or −4 (two answers). **I alone is sufficient.**

**Example 2.** What is Rahim's age? I. Rahim is 5 years older than Karim. II. Karim is 20.
Neither alone works; together Rahim = 25. **Both together are needed.**

**Example 3.** Is x even? I. x is a multiple of 4. II. x is a multiple of 6.
Every multiple of 4 is even; every multiple of 6 is even. **Either alone is sufficient.**

**Example 4.** What day is today? I. Yesterday was not Sunday. II. Tomorrow is Friday.
I leaves six possibilities. II gives Thursday. **II alone is sufficient.**

**Example 5.** What is the area of a rectangle? I. Its perimeter is 20 cm. II. Its length is 6 cm.
Together: breadth = 10 − 6 = 4, area = 24 cm². **Both together are needed.**

## Common traps
| Trap | Example | Correct view |
|---|---|---|
| Assuming a number is positive | "What is x? x² = 25" | x = 5 or −5: **not sufficient** |
| Forgetting that "no" is an answer | "Is n odd? n = 2k for some integer k" | Definitely no: **sufficient** |
| Using statement I while testing II alone | Mixing information | Test each statement on its own first |
| Direction questions without a fixed reference | "A is to the left of B" | Left of B depends on which way B faces: often **not sufficient** |
| Blood relations without gender | "P is the child of Q" | Son or daughter not known: **not sufficient** for "Is P the son of Q?" |

## Quick revision
- Test I alone, then II alone, then both together.
- Sufficient = exactly one answer (a definite "no" counts).
- Two possible values = not sufficient.
- No outside assumptions.
- You do not need to calculate the final answer.
- Speed needs distance and time; a row total needs positions from both ends.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** What is the value of x? I. 2x + 3 = 11. II. x² = 16.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) II alone is sufficient (c) Both together are needed (d) Neither is sufficient

**2.** What is Rahim's age? I. Rahim is 5 years older than Karim. II. Karim is 20 years old.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) II alone is sufficient (c) Both together are needed (d) Neither is sufficient

**3.** Is the integer x even? I. x is a multiple of 4. II. x is a multiple of 6.
(a) Either I alone or II alone is sufficient (b) I alone is sufficient, II is not (c) Both together are needed (d) Neither is sufficient

**4.** What is the area of a rectangle? I. Its perimeter is 20 cm. II. Its length is 6 cm.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) II alone is sufficient (c) Neither is sufficient (d) Both together are needed

**5.** What day of the week is today? I. Yesterday was not Sunday. II. Tomorrow is Friday.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) II alone is sufficient (c) Both together are needed (d) Neither is sufficient

**6.** Is P the brother of Q? I. P is male. II. P and Q have the same parents.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) Neither is sufficient (c) Both together are needed (d) II alone is sufficient

**7.** How many students are in the class? I. Rahim is 10th from the top. II. Rahim is 21st from the bottom.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) II alone is sufficient (c) Neither is sufficient (d) Both together are needed

**8.** What is the speed of a car? I. It covers 120 km in 2 hours at a uniform speed. II. At the same uniform speed, it covers 30 km in 30 minutes.
(a) Either I alone or II alone is sufficient (b) I alone is sufficient, II is not (c) Both together are needed (d) Neither is sufficient

**9.** Is the integer n divisible by 6? I. n is divisible by 3. II. n is divisible by 12.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) II alone is sufficient (c) Both together are needed (d) Neither is sufficient

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a | I gives x = 4; II gives 4 or −4. |
| 2 | c | Together: 20 + 5 = 25. |
| 3 | a | Multiples of 4 and multiples of 6 are all even. |
| 4 | d | Breadth = 10 − 6 = 4; area = 24 cm². |
| 5 | b | Tomorrow Friday means today is Thursday. |
| 6 | c | Same parents plus male = brother; neither alone is enough. |
| 7 | d | 10 + 21 − 1 = 30. |
| 8 | a | Each gives 60 km/h on its own. |
| 9 | b | Every multiple of 12 is a multiple of 6; a multiple of 3 may be odd. |
