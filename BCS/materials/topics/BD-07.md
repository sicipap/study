# 1905 partition, Muslim League, Lahore Resolution 1940, 1947

> **Why it matters:** Exams repeat: who partitioned Bengal (Curzon, 1905), when it was annulled (1911), where the Muslim League was founded (Dhaka, 1906), who moved the Lahore Resolution (A. K. Fazlul Huq) and the dates of 1947.

## Partition of Bengal 1905 (বঙ্গভঙ্গ) 🔥
- Done by Viceroy **লর্ড কার্জন**; took effect on **16 October 1905**.
- New province: **"পূর্ববঙ্গ ও আসাম" (Eastern Bengal and Assam)**, capital **ঢাকা**. First Lieutenant-Governor: **Sir Bampfylde Fuller**.
- Official reason: the old Bengal Presidency was too large to govern. Muslims of East Bengal generally **welcomed** it (new opportunities); many Hindu leaders **opposed** it.
- Opposition grew into the **স্বদেশী আন্দোলন** (boycott of British goods). Rabindranath Tagore wrote **"আমার সোনার বাংলা"** during this movement (1905).
- 🔥 **Annulled (বঙ্গভঙ্গ রদ): 12 December 1911**, announced by **King George V** at the **Delhi Durbar**. The capital of India moved from **Calcutta to Delhi** (1911 announcement; formally 1912).
- As compensation to East Bengal Muslims, the Government promised a university at Dhaka → **University of Dhaka (1921)**.

## All-India Muslim League 🔥
- **Simla Deputation (1 October 1906):** a Muslim delegation led by **স্যার আগা খান (তৃতীয়)** met Viceroy **Lord Minto** and asked for separate electorates.
- 🔥 **Founded 30 December 1906** at **ঢাকা** (**Shahbagh / Ahsan Manzil**, during the All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference). The proposal came from **নবাব স্যার সলিমুল্লাহ**. The session was chaired by **নবাব ভিকার-উল-মুলক**; **Sir Aga Khan III** became the first (permanent) president.
- **Morley–Minto Reform (1909):** gave Muslims a **separate electorate**.
- **Lucknow Pact (1916):** Congress–League agreement.
- **Khilafat and Non-cooperation movements (1919–1922).**
- **Bengal Pact (1923):** by **চিত্তরঞ্জন দাশ (C. R. Das)**, promising Muslims a fair share of jobs.

## 1930s–1940s in Bengal
- **Government of India Act 1935** → provincial elections **1937**. **এ. কে. ফজলুল হক** ("শেরে বাংলা"), leader of the **কৃষক প্রজা পার্টি**, became **Prime Minister of Bengal** in a coalition with the Muslim League. His government set up **ঋণ সালিশি বোর্ড** (debt settlement boards).
- The word **"Pakistan"** was coined by **চৌধুরী রহমত আলী** (1933).
- **Two-nation theory (দ্বিজাতি তত্ত্ব):** Hindus and Muslims are two separate nations. Put forward politically by **মুহম্মদ আলী জিন্নাহ** (the idea goes back to Syed Ahmad Khan and Allama Iqbal).

## Lahore Resolution 1940 (লাহোর প্রস্তাব) 🔥🔥
- Adopted at the Muslim League session in **Lahore**, **23 March 1940** (session 22–24 March), chaired by **Jinnah**.
- 🔥 Moved by **এ. কে. ফজলুল হক**.
- It demanded independent **"States"** (plural) for the Muslim-majority areas in the north-west and east of India.
- **Delhi Convention (April 1946):** the Muslim League legislators changed "States" to **one "State"**; the amendment was moved by **হোসেন শহীদ সোহরাওয়ার্দী**.
- Later, the Bengali leaders of 1966–71 argued that the original Lahore Resolution supported autonomy for East Bengal (the **Six-point** demand refers to it).

- Boundaries were drawn by the **Radcliffe Commission** (Sir Cyril Radcliffe) — the **Radcliffe Line**.
- East Bengal (পূর্ব বাংলা) became the eastern wing of Pakistan, separated from the west wing by about 1,600 km of India. **Khawaja Nazimuddin** became its first Chief Minister.

## Quick revision
- Partition of Bengal: **Lord Curzon**, effective **16 October 1905**; capital of new province **Dhaka**.
- Annulled: **12 December 1911**, Delhi Durbar, **King George V**.
- Muslim League: **30 December 1906, Dhaka**; proposed by **Nawab Salimullah**; first president **Aga Khan III**.
- Separate electorate: **Morley–Minto Reform 1909**.
- Lahore Resolution: **23 March 1940**, moved by **A. K. Fazlul Huq**; "States".
- "States" → "State": **Delhi Convention 1946**, moved by **Suhrawardy**.
- Name "Pakistan": **Chowdhury Rahmat Ali**.
- Last Viceroy: **Mountbatten**; plan **3 June 1947**.
- Pakistan **14 August**, India **15 August 1947**.
