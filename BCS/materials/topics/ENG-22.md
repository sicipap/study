# Victorian poets: Tennyson, Browning, Arnold

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask about *In Memoriam* (for Arthur Hallam), "Ulysses", Tennyson as Poet Laureate, Browning's dramatic monologue ("My Last Duchess"), Arnold's "Dover Beach" and his definition of poetry as "criticism of life".

## The Victorian Age (1832–1901)
- Named after **Queen Victoria** (reigned **1837–1901**).
- Themes: industrial progress and its costs, **conflict of science and faith** (Darwin's ***On the Origin of Species*, 1859**), doubt, moral earnestness, social reform.
- Other poets: **Elizabeth Barrett Browning** (*Sonnets from the Portuguese*: "**How do I love thee? Let me count the ways**"), **D. G. Rossetti** and the **Pre-Raphaelites**, **Christina Rossetti** (*Goblin Market*), **Swinburne**, **Gerard Manley Hopkins** ("sprung rhythm", published 1918), **Edward FitzGerald** (translated ***The Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam***, 1859).
- Prose thinkers: **Thomas Carlyle** (*Sartor Resartus*, *Heroes and Hero-Worship*), **John Ruskin** (*Unto This Last*, *Modern Painters*), **J. S. Mill** (*On Liberty*).

## Alfred, Lord Tennyson (1809–1892) 🔥
- **Poet Laureate from 1850 to 1892** (after Wordsworth) — the longest-serving; made a **Baron (Lord)** in 1884.
- Epithet: the **representative poet of the Victorian age**.

| Work | Year | Fact / famous line |
|---|---|---|
| "**The Lady of Shalott**" | 1832 | Arthurian ballad; "I am half sick of shadows" |
| "**The Lotos-Eaters**" | 1832 | From Homer's *Odyssey* |
| "**Ulysses**" 🔥 | written 1833, pub. **1842** | Dramatic monologue of the aged Greek hero: "**To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield.**" — "I am a part of all that I have met" |
| "**Break, Break, Break**" | 1842 | Grief for Hallam |
| "**Locksley Hall**" | 1842 | |
| ***The Princess*** | 1847 | Contains "Tears, idle tears" |

| "**The Charge of the Light Brigade**" 🔥 | **1854** | Crimean War: "**Theirs not to reason why, / Theirs but to do and die**" |

## Robert Browning (1812–1889) 🔥
- Master of the **dramatic monologue** 🔥 (one character speaks to a silent listener and reveals his own nature).
- Known for obscure, rough, energetic style and optimism: "**God's in his heaven — / All's right with the world!**" (*Pippa Passes*, 1841).
- Married poet **Elizabeth Barrett** in 1846; they lived in **Italy**. Buried in **Westminster Abbey**.

| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***Pauline***, ***Paracelsus***, ***Sordello*** | 1833–40 | Early long poems (*Sordello* famously obscure) |
| ***Pippa Passes*** | 1841 | Verse drama with the "God's in his heaven" song |
| "**My Last Duchess**" 🔥 | **1842** | Best-known dramatic monologue: the **Duke of Ferrara** shows a portrait of his dead wife |
| "**Porphyria's Lover**" | 1836/1842 | A lover strangles Porphyria with her hair |
| "**The Pied Piper of Hamelin**" | 1842 | Children's poem |
| ***Men and Women*** | **1855** | "Andrea del Sarto" ("**a man's reach should exceed his grasp, / Or what's a heaven for?**"), "Fra Lippo Lippi", "Love Among the Ruins" |
| ***Dramatis Personae*** | 1864 | "**Rabbi Ben Ezra**": "**Grow old along with me! / The best is yet to be**" |
| ***The Ring and the Book*** 🔥 | **1868–69** | Long poem: a Roman murder case told by **twelve** monologues |
| "**Prospice**", "**Home-Thoughts, from Abroad**" | | "Oh, to be in England / Now that April's there" |

## Matthew Arnold (1822–1888) 🔥
- Poet, critic, and **inspector of schools**; Professor of Poetry at Oxford. Son of **Dr. Thomas Arnold** of Rugby School.
- Definition of poetry 🔥: "**Poetry is at bottom a criticism of life.**"
- Critical ideas: "**sweetness and light**" (from Swift), "**touchstone method**", "**Hebraism and Hellenism**", "the best that has been thought and said", **Philistines** (the narrow middle class).
- On Shelley: "**a beautiful and ineffectual angel**".

| Work | Year | Fact / famous line |
|---|---|---|
| "**Dover Beach**" 🔥 | written c. 1851, pub. **1867** | Loss of faith: "**The Sea of Faith / Was once, too, at the full**"; "**And we are here as on a darkling plain … Where ignorant armies clash by night**" |
| "**The Scholar-Gipsy**" | 1853 | "this strange disease of modern life" |
| "**Thyrsis**" | 1866 | **Pastoral elegy** on his friend **Arthur Hugh Clough** |
| "**Sohrab and Rustum**" | 1853 | Epic episode from Persian: a father (**Rustum**) unknowingly kills his son (**Sohrab**) |
| "**Rugby Chapel**" | 1867 | Elegy on his father |
| "**Shakespeare**" (sonnet) | | "Others abide our question. Thou art free." |
| ***Essays in Criticism*** | 1865, 1888 | "The Study of Poetry" (touchstone method) |
| ***Culture and Anarchy*** 🔥 | **1869** | Culture as "sweetness and light"; Barbarians, **Philistines**, Populace |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 ***In Memoriam*** — Tennyson's elegy on **Arthur Hallam**; "'Tis better to have loved and lost".
- 🔥 "**To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield**" — *Ulysses*.
- Tennyson: **Poet Laureate 1850–1892**; ***Idylls of the King*** (Arthurian).
- 🔥 Browning = master of the **dramatic monologue**; "**My Last Duchess**".
- "Grow old along with me! The best is yet to be" — **Rabbi Ben Ezra**.
- 🔥 Arnold: "**Poetry is at bottom a criticism of life**"; "**Dover Beach**".
- "Thyrsis" — Arnold's elegy on **Clough**; "Sohrab and Rustum" — Arnold.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** "In Memoriam" was written in memory of —
(a) Edward King (b) Keats (c) Arthur Hallam (d) Clough

**2.** "To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield" is the last line of —
(a) Ulysses (b) Dover Beach (c) Rabbi Ben Ezra (d) Crossing the Bar

**3.** Robert Browning is famous for the —
(a) sonnet (b) dramatic monologue (c) pastoral elegy (d) mock-epic

**4.** "Poetry is at bottom a criticism of life" — who said it?
(a) Wordsworth (b) Tennyson (c) Coleridge (d) Matthew Arnold

**5.** "My Last Duchess" is a poem by —
(a) Tennyson (b) Arnold (c) Robert Browning (d) Rossetti

**6.** "Dover Beach" is mainly about —
(a) loss of religious faith (b) war in the Crimea (c) love of nature (d) King Arthur

**7.** "Theirs not to reason why, / Theirs but to do and die" is from —
(a) Ulysses (b) The Lady of Shalott (c) Sohrab and Rustum (d) The Charge of the Light Brigade

**8.** "Thyrsis" is an elegy by Arnold on —
(a) Arthur Hugh Clough (b) Arthur Hallam (c) his father (d) Keats

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | Tennyson's friend Hallam died in 1833 |
| 2 | a | Closing line of Ulysses |
| 3 | b | e.g. My Last Duchess |
| 4 | d | Arnold, in his essay on Wordsworth |
| 5 | c | Browning's 1842 monologue |
| 6 | a | "The Sea of Faith" is retreating |
| 7 | d | Tennyson on the Crimean War, 1854 |
| 8 | a | Pastoral elegy for Clough, 1866 |
