# International law: ICJ, ICC, UNCLOS

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the difference between the ICJ and the ICC, their seats and judges, the Rome Statute, UNCLOS zones (12 / 24 / 200 nautical miles), ITLOS in Hamburg, and Bangladesh's maritime victories against Myanmar (2012) and India (2014).

## International law basics (আন্তর্জাতিক আইন)
- 🔥 **Hugo Grotius** (Netherlands) is the **father of international law**; books *Mare Liberum* ("The Free Sea", 1609) and *On the Law of War and Peace* (1625).
- **Sources** (Article 38 of the ICJ Statute): **treaties**, **international custom**, **general principles of law**, and (as secondary means) **judicial decisions and the writings of experts**.
- **International Law Commission (ILC):** set up by the UN General Assembly in **1947** to codify international law.
- **Geneva Conventions (1949, four conventions)** and Additional Protocols (**1977**): **international humanitarian law** (laws of war); guarded by the **ICRC**.
- **Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA):** **1899**, **The Hague** — the oldest institution for international dispute settlement.

## ICJ (International Court of Justice / আন্তর্জাতিক বিচার আদালত) 🔥
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Status | 🔥 **Principal judicial organ of the UN** (Charter Chapter XIV) |
| Established | **1945** by the Charter; began work in **1946** |
| Predecessor | **Permanent Court of International Justice (PCIJ)**, 1922, under the League |
| 🔥 Seat | **Peace Palace, The Hague, Netherlands** |
| 🔥 Judges | **15**, elected for **9 years** by **both the General Assembly and the Security Council** (absolute majority in both); one-third renewed every 3 years |
| Official languages | **English and French** |
| Who can be a party | 🔥 **Only states** (not individuals) |
| Jurisdiction | **Contentious cases** between states (with their consent) and **advisory opinions** for UN organs and agencies |

- Famous cases: **Nicaragua v. USA (1986)**; **Kulbhushan Jadhav (India v. Pakistan, 2019)**; 🔥 **The Gambia v. Myanmar** (Rohingya genocide, filed 2019); **South Africa v. Israel** (Gaza, filed 2023); **Ukraine v. Russia** (2022).
- Advisory opinions: Israel's **separation wall** illegal (2004); Kosovo's declaration of independence (2010); **Chagos Islands** (2019); Israel's **occupation of Palestinian territory unlawful (2024)**; 🔥 **States' obligations on climate change (July 2025)**, requested at **Vanuatu's** initiative.
- **Nagendra Singh** (India) served as ICJ President (1985–88). No Bangladeshi has been an ICJ judge (as of 2026).

## ICC (International Criminal Court / আন্তর্জাতিক অপরাধ আদালত) 🔥
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Treaty | 🔥 **Rome Statute**, adopted **17 July 1998** (17 July = **World Day for International Justice**) |
| In force | 🔥 **1 July 2002** |
| Seat | **The Hague** |
| Status | 🔥 **Independent** — **not a UN organ** (linked by a relationship agreement) |
| Who is tried | 🔥 **Individuals** (not states) |
| 🔥 Four crimes | **Genocide**, **crimes against humanity**, **war crimes**, **crime of aggression** |
| Judges | **18**, 9-year terms |
| Members | About **125 states parties** (as of 2025) |
| Principle | **Complementarity**: acts only when national courts are unable or unwilling |

- 🔥 **Not members:** the **USA, Russia, China, India, Israel** (among others).
- 🔥 **Bangladesh ratified the Rome Statute on 23 March 2010** — the **first South Asian** state party. This let the ICC investigate the **deportation of Rohingya** into Bangladesh.
- Arrest warrants: **Omar al-Bashir** (Sudan, 2009, first for a sitting head of state); 🔥 **Vladimir Putin (March 2023)**; **Benjamin Netanyahu and Yoav Gallant (November 2024)**. Former Philippine President **Rodrigo Duterte** was arrested and sent to The Hague in **March 2025**.
- Hungary announced withdrawal in 2025; Burundi (2017) and the Philippines (2019) have left before.

### ICJ vs ICC 🔥
| | ICJ | ICC |
|---|---|---|
| Founded | 1945/46 (UN Charter) | 2002 (Rome Statute) |
| UN organ? | **Yes** | **No** |
| Parties | **States** | **Individuals** |
| Judges | 15 | 18 |
| Seat | The Hague | The Hague |

## UNCLOS (UN Convention on the Law of the Sea / জাতিসংঘ সমুদ্র আইন কনভেনশন) 🔥
- 🔥 Adopted **10 December 1982** at **Montego Bay, Jamaica**; 🔥 **in force 16 November 1994**. Called the "**constitution for the oceans**".
- **1 nautical mile = 1.852 km.**

| Zone (measured from the baseline) | Width | Rights |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal waters** | Landward side of the baseline | Full sovereignty |
| 🔥 **Territorial sea** | **12 nautical miles** | Sovereignty (ships have innocent passage) |
| 🔥 **Contiguous zone** | **24 nautical miles** | Customs, immigration, pollution control |
| 🔥 **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)** | **200 nautical miles** | Sole right to fish and exploit resources |
| **Continental shelf** | At least 200 nm, up to **350 nm** (or 100 nm from the 2,500 m depth line) | Seabed resources |
| **High seas** | Beyond national jurisdiction | Free for all |

**Bodies created under UNCLOS**
| Body | Seat |
|---|---|
| 🔥 **ITLOS** (International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea), 21 judges | **Hamburg, Germany** |
| **International Seabed Authority (ISA)** | **Kingston, Jamaica** |
| **Commission on the Limits of the Continental Shelf (CLCS)** | New York |

- 🔥 **BBNJ Agreement ("High Seas Treaty"):** adopted **June 2023** to protect marine biodiversity beyond national jurisdiction; **entered into force in January 2026**.

## Bangladesh's maritime boundary cases 🔥
| Case | Forum | Verdict | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔥 **Bangladesh v. Myanmar** | **ITLOS, Hamburg** | **14 March 2012** | Bangladesh's claim was largely upheld: a full **200-nm EEZ** and access to the **outer continental shelf** (Bangladeshi sources commonly cite **1,11,631 sq km** of sea area after this verdict) |
| 🔥 **Bangladesh v. India** | **Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA), The Hague** (UNCLOS Annex VII tribunal) | **7 July 2014** | Of **25,602 sq km** in dispute, Bangladesh got **19,467 sq km** |

- 🔥 In total Bangladesh secured about **1,18,813 sq km** of sea area (territorial sea + EEZ), plus continental-shelf rights — a big boost for the **blue economy**.
- Bangladesh ratified UNCLOS in **2001**.

## Quick revision
- Father of international law: **Hugo Grotius**.
- ICJ: UN organ, **The Hague**, **15 judges / 9 years**, cases between **states**.
- ICC: **Rome Statute 17 July 1998**, in force **1 July 2002**; tries **individuals**; **4 crimes**; not a UN organ.
- Bangladesh ratified the Rome Statute **23 March 2010** (first in South Asia).
- UNCLOS: **1982 Montego Bay**, in force **1994**; territorial sea **12 nm**, contiguous **24 nm**, EEZ **200 nm**.
- ITLOS **Hamburg**; ISA **Kingston**.
- Bangladesh v. Myanmar: **ITLOS, 14 March 2012**; Bangladesh v. India: **PCA, 7 July 2014** (19,467 of 25,602 sq km).
