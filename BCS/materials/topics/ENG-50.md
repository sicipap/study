# Punctuation & capitalization

> **Why it matters:** BCS written and preliminary papers, NTRCA and bank exams give an unpunctuated passage or ask "Which sentence is correctly punctuated/capitalized?". A few rules on commas, apostrophes, quotation marks and capital letters cover most questions.

## Punctuation marks at a glance
| Mark | Name | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| . | **Full stop (period)** | End of a statement or command; abbreviations (Mr., Dr., e.g.) |
| , | **Comma** | Short pause; lists; after introductory words; around extra information |
| ; | **Semicolon** | Joins two closely related independent clauses; separates items that already contain commas |
| : | **Colon** | Introduces a list, explanation or quotation |
| ? | **Question (interrogation) mark** | End of a direct question |
| ! | **Exclamation (note of exclamation)** | Strong feeling; after interjections |
| ' | **Apostrophe** | Possession; contractions |
| " " / ' ' | **Quotation marks (inverted commas)** | Direct speech; quoted words; titles of short works |
| - | **Hyphen** | Joins parts of a compound word (well-known, mother-in-law) |
| — | **Dash** | Sudden break or added comment |
| ( ) | **Brackets (parentheses)** | Extra information |

## Comma rules 🔥
1. **Lists:** *I bought rice, fish, eggs and vegetables.* (The comma before *and*, called the Oxford/serial comma, is optional.)
2. **Addressing someone (vocative):** *Rahim, come here.* / *Come here, Rahim.*
3. **After introductory words/clauses:** *However, he did not come.* / *If it rains, we will stay home.*
4. **Non-defining clause / apposition:** *Dhaka, the capital of Bangladesh, is crowded.* / *My father, who is a doctor, lives in Khulna.*
5. **Before a coordinating conjunction joining two long clauses:** *He worked hard, but he failed.*
6. **Before and after direct speech tags:** *He said, "I am busy."* / *"I am busy," he said.*
7. **Yes/No, interjections:** *Yes, I will go.* / *Alas, he is dead!* (or *Alas! he is dead.*)
8. **Dates and addresses:** *1 October 2026*; *Mirpur, Dhaka*.

## Semicolon and colon
- **Semicolon:** *He was ill; he could not attend the meeting.* / *Man proposes; God disposes.*
- Before words like *however, therefore* joining two clauses: *He was tired; however, he kept working.*
- **Colon:** *You need three things: patience, practice and hard work.* / *Remember this: honesty is the best policy.*

## Apostrophe 🔥
| Use | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular possessive | **'s** | the boy**'s** book |
| Plural ending in -s | only **'** | the boys**'** hostel |
| Plural not ending in -s | **'s** | the children**'s** park, women**'s** rights |
| Joint possession | 's on the last noun | Rahim and Karim**'s** shop (one shop) |
| Separate possession | 's on each noun | Rahim**'s** and Karim**'s** shops |
| Contraction | letters left out | don't, it's (= it is), can't |
| **its vs it's** 🔥 | *its* = possessive (no apostrophe); *it's* = it is | The dog wagged **its** tail. **It's** raining. |

Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe: **yours, hers, ours, theirs, its**.

## Quotation marks and direct speech
- *He said, "**I** am going home."* → comma before the quote, capital letter to start the quoted sentence, full stop **inside** the quotation marks.
- *"Where are you going?" she asked.* → question mark inside, because the quotation itself is a question.

## Capitalization rules 🔥
Use a **capital letter** for:
1. The **first word of a sentence** and of a direct quotation.
2. The pronoun **I** (always).
3. **Proper nouns:** names of people, places, rivers, mountains, organisations: *Rahim, Dhaka, the Padma, the Himalayas, the United Nations*.
4. **Days, months, festivals:** *Friday, October, Eid, Pohela Boishakh*. (Seasons are usually lower case: *spring, winter*.)
5. **Nationalities, languages, religions:** *Bangladeshi, English, Islam, Muslim, Hindu*.
6. **Titles before names:** *Dr. Yunus, President Lincoln* (but *the president of the club*).
7. **Main words in titles** of books, films, etc.: *The Old Man and the Sea*.
8. **God** and names of holy books: *God, the Quran, the Bible*.
9. **Historical events, periods, documents:** *the Liberation War, the Renaissance, the Constitution*.
10. **Abbreviations/acronyms:** *UN, BCS, NATO, UNESCO*.
11. **Geographical directions as regions** (not as directions): *the Middle East, North Bengal* but *go north*.

## Worked example
Unpunctuated: *rahim said to karim where are you going*
Correct: **Rahim said to Karim, "Where are you going?"**

Unpunctuated: *alas my friend is dead said he*
Correct: **"Alas! My friend is dead," said he.**

## Quick revision
- Full stop inside the quotation marks; comma before the opening quote.
- **it's** = it is; **its** = possessive.
- boy's (one), boys' (many), children's (plural without -s).
- Semicolon joins two complete, related clauses.
- Colon introduces a list or explanation.
- Always capitalize **I**, proper nouns, days, months, languages, God.
- Seasons usually take small letters.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Which sentence is correctly punctuated?
(a) He said "I am busy". (b) He said, "I am busy." (c) He said, "I am busy". (d) He said "I am busy."

**2.** Which is correct?
(a) The dog wagged it's tail. (b) The dog wagged its' tail. (c) The dog wagged its tail. (d) The dog wagged its's tail.

**3.** The possessive of "children" is:
(a) childrens' (b) children' (c) childrens (d) children's

**4.** Which sentence is correctly capitalized?
(a) I went to dhaka on friday. (b) I went to Dhaka on Friday. (c) i went to Dhaka on Friday. (d) I went to Dhaka on friday.

**5.** Which mark is used to join two closely related independent clauses without a conjunction?
(a) semicolon (b) hyphen (c) apostrophe (d) comma

**6.** Which word needs a capital letter?
(a) winter (b) river (c) monday (d) city

**7.** "The boys hostel" should be written as:
(a) The boys's hostel (b) The boy's hostel's (c) The boys hostel' (d) The boys' hostel

**8.** The mark used to introduce a list is the:
(a) question mark (b) colon (c) dash (d) exclamation mark

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Comma before the quote, full stop inside |
| 2 | c | its is possessive and has no apostrophe |
| 3 | d | Plural not ending in -s takes 's |
| 4 | b | Proper nouns and days take capitals |
| 5 | a | Semicolon joins related independent clauses |
| 6 | c | Days of the week are capitalized: Monday |
| 7 | d | Plural ending in -s takes only an apostrophe |
| 8 | b | Colon introduces a list |
