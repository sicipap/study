# Romantic: Wordsworth, Coleridge

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask about *Lyrical Ballads* (1798), Wordsworth's definition of poetry, "Poet of Nature", *The Prelude*, the Lake Poets, *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner* (albatross), "willing suspension of disbelief" and *Kubla Khan*.

## The Romantic Movement (1798–1832)
- Started with ***Lyrical Ballads* (1798)**, a joint collection by **Wordsworth and Coleridge** 🔥. The **Preface** (added in the **1800** edition, enlarged 1802) is the **manifesto of English Romanticism**.
- Features: **love of nature**, **imagination**, emotion, the **common man** and rustic life, the **supernatural**, individualism, return to the past, lyric poetry.
- Influences: the **French Revolution (1789)**, Rousseau ("Return to nature").
- **Pre-Romantics / forerunners:** Thomas Gray (*Elegy Written in a Country Churchyard*, 1751), William Collins, William Cowper, **Robert Burns**, **William Blake** (*Songs of Innocence and of Experience*).
- **First generation:** Wordsworth, Coleridge, Southey (the **Lake Poets**). **Second generation:** Byron, Shelley, Keats.
- **Lake Poets** 🔥: **Wordsworth, Coleridge and Robert Southey** — they lived in the **Lake District** of north-west England.

## William Wordsworth (1770–1850) 🔥
- Born at **Cockermouth**, Cumberland (Lake District). **Poet Laureate from 1843** (after Southey) till his death.
- Epithets: **"Poet of Nature"** / "High priest of Nature", "Worshipper of Nature". Keats called his style the "**egotistical sublime**".
- Definition of poetry 🔥: "**Poetry is the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings: it takes its origin from emotion recollected in tranquillity.**" (Preface to *Lyrical Ballads*)
- He said poetry should use "**a selection of the language really used by men**".
- Sister: **Dorothy Wordsworth** (journals).

## Samuel Taylor Coleridge (1772–1834) 🔥
- Poet, critic and philosopher; friend of Wordsworth. Addicted to **opium**.
- In *Lyrical Ballads*, Coleridge was to treat the **supernatural** so that it seems real; Wordsworth, the **ordinary** so that it seems new.
- Epithets: poet of the **supernatural**; a great **critic** ("the greatest English critic" in many guides).

| Work | Year | Fact / famous line |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*** 🔥 | **1798** | A sailor shoots an **albatross** and is punished. "**Water, water, every where, / Nor any drop to drink.**" "**He prayeth best, who loveth best / All things both great and small.**" The Mariner stops a **Wedding-Guest** to tell his tale. |
| ***Kubla Khan*** 🔥 | written 1797, pub. **1816** | Unfinished "**vision in a dream**" (opium); interrupted by "**a person from Porlock**". "**In Xanadu did Kubla Khan / A stately pleasure-dome decree**" |
| ***Christabel*** | pub. 1816 | Unfinished; supernatural (Geraldine) |
| "**Dejection: An Ode**" | 1802 | |
| "**Frost at Midnight**", "**This Lime-Tree Bower My Prison**" | 1798 / 1797 | Conversation poems |
| ***Biographia Literaria*** 🔥 | **1817** | Critical autobiography; theory of **Fancy and Imagination** (primary and secondary imagination); phrase "**willing suspension of disbelief**" |

- Coleridge's definition: poetry is "**the best words in the best order**" (*Table Talk*); prose = "words in their best order".

## Quick revision
- 🔥 ***Lyrical Ballads* (1798)** — Wordsworth and Coleridge — start of Romanticism.
- 🔥 "**Spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings**" — Wordsworth.
- 🔥 "**The Child is father of the Man**" — Wordsworth.
- ***The Prelude*** = "Growth of a Poet's Mind", published **1850**.
- 🔥 **Albatross** — *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*.
- "**Willing suspension of disbelief**" — Coleridge, *Biographia Literaria*.
- **Lake Poets:** Wordsworth, Coleridge, **Southey**.
- ***Kubla Khan*** — Xanadu; "person from Porlock".
