# Civil service values & code of conduct

> **Why it matters:** As a future civil servant, you will be asked about the core values of public service (integrity, impartiality, neutrality …), the Nolan principles, Weber's bureaucracy, the constitutional articles on the services, and the conduct and discipline rules for government employees in Bangladesh.

## Core civil service values 🔥
| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Integrity (সততা/শুদ্ধাচার)** | Honest, consistent conduct; putting public duty before private interest |
| **Impartiality (নিরপেক্ষতা)** | Treating all citizens equally, without favour or bias |
| **Political neutrality (রাজনৈতিক নিরপেক্ষতা)** | Serving any lawful government loyally; no party politics |
| **Objectivity (বস্তুনিষ্ঠতা)** | Decisions based on facts, evidence and merit |
| **Accountability (জবাবদিহিতা)** | Answering for one's decisions |
| **Transparency** | Openness in decisions |
| **Dedication to public service** | Commitment to citizens' welfare |
| **Empathy, tolerance and compassion** | Especially towards the weak and vulnerable |
| **Confidentiality** | Protecting official secrets |
| **Anonymity** | The minister takes public responsibility; the civil servant works behind the scenes |

## Nolan's Seven Principles of Public Life 🔥
Set out in **1995** by the UK **Committee on Standards in Public Life** (chaired by **Lord Nolan**):
1. **Selflessness**
2. **Integrity**
3. **Objectivity**
4. **Accountability**
5. **Openness**
6. **Honesty**
7. **Leadership**

Memory aid: **"SIO-AOHL"** → "**S**ee **I**f **O**thers **A**re **O**pen, **H**onest **L**eaders".

## Max Weber's bureaucracy
**Max Weber** (German sociologist) described the **ideal type of bureaucracy** based on **legal-rational authority**:
- **Hierarchy** of offices; **fixed rules** and procedures.
- **Division of labour / specialisation**.
- **Impersonality**: rules apply equally to all.
- **Selection and promotion by merit** and qualifications.
- **Written records** (files).
- Career service with fixed salary.

## Constitution of Bangladesh and the services 🔥
| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **21(2)** | "**Every person in the service of the Republic has a duty to strive at all times to serve the people.**" |
| **Part IX (133–141)** | **The Services of Bangladesh** |
| **133** | Parliament may regulate appointment and conditions of service by law |
| **134** | Service holders hold office **during the pleasure of the President** |
| **135** | Protection against dismissal, removal or reduction in rank without a chance to show cause |
| **137–141** | **Public Service Commissions** |

## Key laws and rules in Bangladesh 🔥
| Law / rule | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Government Servants (Conduct) Rules, 1979** (সরকারি কর্মচারী (আচরণ) বিধিমালা, ১৯৭৯) | Day-to-day behaviour of government employees |
| **Government Servants (Discipline and Appeal) Rules, 2018** (replaced the 1985 rules) | Misconduct, penalties, appeal |
| **Public Service Act, 2018** (সরকারি চাকরি আইন, ২০১৮) | Appointment, promotion, discipline and other terms of government service |
| **Annual Performance Agreement (APA)** | Yearly performance contract of ministries and offices (introduced from 2014–15) |
| **National Integrity Strategy 2012** | Ethics committees, integrity action plans, integrity award |

### Main points of the Conduct Rules 1979
- **Gifts:** accepting gifts that could influence official work is restricted; gifts above a set value must be reported.
- **Declaration of assets:** property must be declared on joining service and periodically (commonly every five years).
- **No private trade or business**, and no speculative investment.
- **No political activity**: may not join a party, take part in or help an election campaign (voting is allowed).
- **No public criticism of government policy** or communication with the press without permission.
- **No dowry**, no approach to foreign missions for favours, no unauthorised disclosure of official information.
- **No strikes or pressure groups** to force the government.

## Misconduct (অসদাচরণ)
Examples: disobeying lawful orders, gross negligence, unauthorised absence, corruption, unbecoming behaviour, sexual harassment. Penalties are **minor** (censure, stopping increment) or **major** (demotion, compulsory retirement, removal, dismissal).

## Quick revision
- Core values: integrity, impartiality, neutrality, objectivity, accountability, dedication.
- Nolan (UK, 1995): selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty, leadership.
- Weber: hierarchy, rules, impersonality, merit, written records.
- Article 21(2): duty to serve the people.
- Part IX (Articles 133–141): the Services; 137–141 PSC.
- Conduct Rules 1979; Discipline and Appeal Rules 2018; Public Service Act 2018.
- No party politics; declare assets; restrictions on gifts.

(a) 1972 (b) 1985 (c) 2018 (d) 1979
