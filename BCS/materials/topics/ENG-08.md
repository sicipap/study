# Shakespeare: tragedies

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask which play a character or quotation comes from, the four great tragedies, the tragic flaw of each hero, and basic facts about Shakespeare's life.

## William Shakespeare (1564–1616) 🔥
- Born **23 April 1564** (traditional date; baptised 26 April) at **Stratford-upon-Avon**; died **23 April 1616**.
- Wife: **Anne Hathaway**. Son **Hamnet** died young.
- Wrote about **37 plays** (some count 38 or 39 with collaborations), **154 sonnets** and two long poems (***Venus and Adonis***, ***The Rape of Lucrece***).
- Theatre company: **Lord Chamberlain's Men**, later the **King's Men**; theatre: **The Globe** (1599).
- First collected edition: the **First Folio (1623)**, by his fellow actors **John Heminges and Henry Condell**; it has **36 plays**.
- Epithets: **"The Bard of Avon"**, **"Swan of Avon"** (Ben Jonson), "the national poet of England". Ben Jonson wrote that he was "**not of an age, but for all time**".

## The four great tragedies 🔥🔥
| Play | Approx. date | Hero & tragic flaw | Setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Hamlet*** | c. **1600–01** | Prince of Denmark; **indecision / delay** | Denmark (Elsinore) |
| ***Othello*** | c. **1603–04** | The Moor of Venice; **jealousy** | Venice, Cyprus |
| ***King Lear*** | c. **1605–06** | Old king of Britain; **pride and poor judgement** | Britain |
| ***Macbeth*** | c. **1606** | Scottish general; **ambition** | Scotland |

**Memory hook:** *HOLM — Hamlet, Othello, Lear, Macbeth.* ***Macbeth* is the shortest** tragedy; ***Hamlet* is the longest** play.

## Characters and famous lines
### Hamlet
- Characters: **Hamlet**, his father's **Ghost**, uncle **Claudius** (murderer, now king), mother **Gertrude**, **Polonius**, his daughter **Ophelia** (Hamlet's love), son **Laertes**, friend **Horatio**, **Rosencrantz and Guildenstern**, **Yorick** (the dead jester's skull).
- Lines: 🔥 **"To be, or not to be: that is the question."** — **"Frailty, thy name is woman!"** — "Something is rotten in the state of Denmark." — "Brevity is the soul of wit." (Polonius) — "Neither a borrower nor a lender be." (Polonius) — "Alas, poor Yorick!" — "The rest is silence." — "There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio…"
- Play-within-a-play: ***The Mousetrap*** (The Murder of Gonzago).

### Othello
- **Othello** (black general), wife **Desdemona**, villain **Iago** (his ensign), **Cassio** (lieutenant), **Emilia** (Iago's wife), **Roderigo**, Desdemona's father **Brabantio**. The **handkerchief** is the "proof".
- Lines: **"O, beware, my lord, of jealousy; / It is the green-eyed monster…"** (Iago) — "one that loved not wisely but too well" (Othello).

### King Lear
- **Lear** divides his kingdom among daughters **Goneril, Regan** (flatterers) and **Cordelia** (honest, disowned). Subplot: **Gloucester** and his sons **Edgar** (loyal) and **Edmund** (bastard, villain). **The Fool**; **Kent**.
- Lines: "Nothing will come of nothing." — "How sharper than a serpent's tooth it is / To have a thankless child!" — "I am a man more sinned against than sinning." — "As flies to wanton boys are we to the gods; / They kill us for their sport." (Gloucester)

### Macbeth
- **Macbeth**, **Lady Macbeth**, **King Duncan** (murdered), **Banquo** (his ghost appears), **Macduff** (kills Macbeth; "from his mother's womb untimely ripped"), **Malcolm**, **the three Witches**.
- Lines: **"Fair is foul, and foul is fair."** (Witches) — **"Out, damned spot!"** (Lady Macbeth, sleepwalking) — "Is this a dagger which I see before me?" — 🔥 **"Life's but a walking shadow… a tale / Told by an idiot, full of sound and fury, / Signifying nothing."** — "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow." — "All the perfumes of Arabia will not sweeten this little hand."
- Called "**the Scottish play**" by superstitious actors.

## Other tragedies
| Play | Key fact / line |
|---|---|
| ***Romeo and Juliet*** (c. 1595) | Lovers from the **Montague** and **Capulet** families of **Verona**; "**What's in a name? That which we call a rose / By any other name would smell as sweet**"; "star-cross'd lovers" |
| ***Julius Caesar*** (1599) | **Brutus**, Cassius, **Mark Antony**; "**Et tu, Brute?**"; "**Friends, Romans, countrymen, lend me your ears**"; "Beware the Ides of March"; "Cowards die many times before their deaths" |
| ***Antony and Cleopatra*** | Roman general and Egyptian queen |
| *Titus Andronicus* | His earliest, bloody revenge tragedy |
| *Timon of Athens*, *Coriolanus* | Roman/Greek tragedies |

- **"The noblest Roman of them all"** — Antony about **Brutus**.
- A. C. Bradley's ***Shakespearean Tragedy* (1904)** is the classic study of the four great tragedies.

## Quick revision
- 🔥 Four great tragedies: **Hamlet, Othello, King Lear, Macbeth**.
- 🔥 "To be, or not to be" — **Hamlet**; "Frailty, thy name is woman" — **Hamlet**.
- 🔥 "Green-eyed monster" — **Othello** (spoken by Iago).
- "Out, damned spot" — **Lady Macbeth**; "Fair is foul" — the **Witches**.
- Cordelia = Lear's honest daughter; Desdemona = Othello's wife.
- "Et tu, Brute?" — ***Julius Caesar***.
- Shakespeare: **154 sonnets**, about **37 plays**, **First Folio 1623**.
