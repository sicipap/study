# American literature: Whitman, Frost, Hemingway, Arthur Miller

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask who wrote a poem, novel or play, famous lines ("The woods are lovely, dark and deep…"), characters (Santiago, Willy Loman), and epithets such as "father of free verse".

## Walt Whitman (1819–1892) 🔥
- Called the **father of free verse** and the "**Bard of Democracy**".
- ***Leaves of Grass*** (**1855**; he kept revising and enlarging it until 1892). Main poem: "**Song of Myself**", which opens "**I celebrate myself, and sing myself**".

| Poem | Key points |
|---|---|
| "**O Captain! My Captain!**" 🔥 | **Elegy on Abraham Lincoln** (1865); the "Captain" = Lincoln, the "ship" = the nation after the Civil War |
| "**When Lilacs Last in the Dooryard Bloom'd**" | Also an elegy for **Lincoln** |
| "I Hear America Singing" | Workers of America |
| "Crossing Brooklyn Ferry", "Passage to India" | |
| *Drum-Taps* (1865) | Civil War poems |

## Robert Frost (1874–1963) 🔥
- Poet of rural **New England**; won the **Pulitzer Prize four times** (a record for poetry).
- Recited "**The Gift Outright**" at **John F. Kennedy's inauguration (1961)**.
- Collections: ***A Boy's Will*** (1913, first), ***North of Boston*** (1914), *Mountain Interval* (1916), *New Hampshire* (1923).

| Poem | Famous lines |
|---|---|
| "**Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening**" 🔥 | "**The woods are lovely, dark and deep, / But I have promises to keep, / And miles to go before I sleep**" (Nehru kept these lines on his desk) |
| "**The Road Not Taken**" 🔥 | "Two roads diverged in a yellow wood… **I took the one less traveled by, / And that has made all the difference.**" |
| "**Mending Wall**" | "**Good fences make good neighbors**"; "Something there is that doesn't love a wall" |
| "Birches", "After Apple-Picking" | |
| "**Fire and Ice**" | "Some say the world will end in fire, / Some say in ice" |
| "The Death of the Hired Man", "Home Burial" | Dramatic narratives |
| "Nothing Gold Can Stay" | |

## Ernest Hemingway (1899–1961) 🔥🔥
- Nobel Prize in Literature **1954**; Pulitzer Prize **1953** for *The Old Man and the Sea*.
- Style: short, plain sentences; the "**iceberg theory**" (theory of omission). Member of the "**Lost Generation**" (Gertrude Stein's phrase). He died by suicide in 1961.

| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Sun Also Rises*** | 1926 | **Jake Barnes**, **Lady Brett Ashley**; epigraph "You are all a lost generation" |
| ***A Farewell to Arms*** | **1929** | WWI Italy; **Frederic Henry** and nurse **Catherine Barkley** |
| *To Have and Have Not* | 1937 | |
| ***For Whom the Bell Tolls*** | **1940** | **Spanish Civil War**; **Robert Jordan**, **Maria**; title from **John Donne**'s *Meditation XVII* |
| ***The Old Man and the Sea*** 🔥 | **1952** | Cuban fisherman **Santiago**, the boy **Manolin**, a giant **marlin**, sharks. "**A man can be destroyed but not defeated.**" |
| Short stories | | "**The Snows of Kilimanjaro**", "The Killers", "Hills Like White Elephants" |
| *A Moveable Feast* | 1964 (posthumous) | Memoir of Paris in the 1920s |

## Arthur Miller (1915–2005) 🔥
- Leading American playwright; wrote the essay "**Tragedy and the Common Man**" (1949): a common man can be a tragic hero. Was married to **Marilyn Monroe** (1956–1961).

| Play | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| ***All My Sons*** | 1947 | **Joe Keller** sold faulty aircraft parts in WWII |
| ***Death of a Salesman*** 🔥 | **1949** | **Pulitzer Prize**; **Willy Loman** (the failing salesman), wife **Linda**, sons **Biff** and **Happy**; the "American Dream". Linda: "**Attention must be paid.**" |
| ***The Crucible*** 🔥 | **1953** | **Salem witch trials** (1692) used as an **allegory of McCarthyism**; **John Proctor**, **Abigail Williams** |
| *A View from the Bridge* | 1955 | Eddie Carbone |
| *After the Fall*, *The Price* | 1964, 1968 | |

## Quick revision
- *Leaves of Grass* (1855): **Whitman**, father of free verse.
- "O Captain! My Captain!": elegy on **Abraham Lincoln**.
- "Miles to go before I sleep": **Frost**, "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening".
- "Good fences make good neighbors": **Frost**, "Mending Wall".
- *The Old Man and the Sea* (1952): **Santiago**; Hemingway Nobel **1954**.
- *For Whom the Bell Tolls*: **Spanish Civil War**; title from **John Donne**.
- *Death of a Salesman* (1949): **Willy Loman**.
- *The Crucible* (1953): Salem witch trials = **McCarthyism**.
