# 18th-century novel: Richardson, Fielding, Johnson, Goldsmith

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask who wrote the first English novel, which novel is epistolary (*Pamela*), who is the "Father of the English novel", Johnson's *Dictionary* (1755), and Goldsmith's works across genres.

## Rise of the novel
- The English novel rose in the 18th century with the growth of the **middle class**, literacy and printing.
- The "**big four**" of the 18th-century novel: **Richardson, Fielding, Smollett, Sterne**.
- **Defoe's *Robinson Crusoe* (1719)** is often called the first English novel; **Richardson's *Pamela* (1740)** is often called the **first modern English novel** / first psychological novel. Exams accept *Pamela* when "first English novel" is asked with Richardson among the options, but check the options for Defoe.

## Samuel Richardson (1689–1761) 🔥
- A printer by profession; master of the **epistolary novel** (a novel written as **letters**).

| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***Pamela, or Virtue Rewarded*** 🔥 | **1740** | A servant girl, **Pamela Andrews**, resists her master **Mr B.** and finally marries him |
| ***Clarissa*** | **1747–48** | Longest novel in English; tragic heroine **Clarissa Harlowe**, villain **Lovelace** |
| ***Sir Charles Grandison*** | 1753 | A perfect gentleman hero |

## Henry Fielding (1707–1754) 🔥
- **"Father of the English novel"** — the title most exam guides give (some books give it to Defoe or Richardson).
- Called his novels "**comic epic poems in prose**". Also a magistrate who founded the **Bow Street Runners**.

| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***Shamela*** | 1741 | Parody of *Pamela* |
| ***Joseph Andrews*** | **1742** | Began as a parody of *Pamela* (Joseph is Pamela's brother); **Parson Adams** |
| ***Jonathan Wild*** | 1743 | Satire on a criminal |
| ***Tom Jones*** 🔥 | **1749** | *The History of Tom Jones, a Foundling*; **Tom**, **Sophia Western**, **Squire Allworthy**, **Blifil** |
| ***Amelia*** | 1751 | |

## Other novelists
| Writer | Work |
|---|---|
| **Tobias Smollett** | ***Roderick Random*** (1748), *Peregrine Pickle*, ***Humphry Clinker*** (1771) — picaresque novels |
| **Laurence Sterne** | ***Tristram Shandy*** (1759–67) — experimental; forerunner of the **stream of consciousness**; *A Sentimental Journey* |
| **Horace Walpole** | ***The Castle of Otranto*** (1764) — **first Gothic novel** |

## Dr. Samuel Johnson (1709–1784) 🔥
- Critic, lexicographer, essayist, poet; the central figure of the **Age of Johnson**. Known as "**Dr. Johnson**" and "the **Great Cham** of literature" (Smollett's phrase).
- His life was written by **James Boswell** — ***The Life of Samuel Johnson* (1791)**, the most famous biography in English.

| Work | Year | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| ***A Dictionary of the English Language*** 🔥 | **1755** | Took about **9 years**; about **40,000+** words; first great English dictionary |
| ***Rasselas*** (*The Prince of Abyssinia*) | **1759** | Philosophical romance; written in a week to pay his mother's funeral costs |
| ***Lives of the Poets*** | **1779–81** | Critical biographies of 52 poets; coined "metaphysical poets" in *Life of Cowley* |
| ***The Vanity of Human Wishes*** | 1749 | Poem, imitation of Juvenal |
| ***London*** | 1738 | Poem |
| *The Rambler* / *The Idler* | 1750–52 / 1758–60 | Periodical essays |
| Edition of **Shakespeare** | 1765 | Famous *Preface to Shakespeare* |

- Famous sayings: "**Patriotism is the last refuge of a scoundrel.**" — "**When a man is tired of London, he is tired of life.**"
- He founded **The Literary Club** (1764) with Reynolds, Burke, Goldsmith and others.

## Oliver Goldsmith (c. 1728–1774) 🔥
- Irish; member of Johnson's circle. He wrote in **every major form**: novel, drama, poem, essay. Johnson's epitaph praised him as one who "left scarcely any style of writing untouched, and touched nothing that he did not adorn."

| Work | Year | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Vicar of Wakefield*** 🔥 | **1766** | **Novel**; **Dr. Charles Primrose**, a kindly vicar, and his family |
| ***She Stoops to Conquer*** 🔥 | **1773** | **Comedy**; **Marlow** mistakes the Hardcastles' house for an inn; **Kate Hardcastle**, **Tony Lumpkin** |
| *The Good-Natured Man* | 1768 | Comedy |
| ***The Deserted Village*** | **1770** | **Poem**: "**Ill fares the land, to hastening ills a prey, / Where wealth accumulates, and men decay**"; the village is **Sweet Auburn** |
| *The Traveller* | 1764 | Poem |
| ***The Citizen of the World*** | 1762 | Essays as letters of a Chinese visitor (Lien Chi Altangi) |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 ***Pamela* (1740)** — Richardson — **epistolary novel**.
- 🔥 **Fielding** = "**Father of the English novel**"; ***Tom Jones* (1749)**.
- ***Joseph Andrews*** began as a parody of *Pamela*.
- 🔥 **Johnson's *Dictionary* (1755)**; his biography by **Boswell (1791)**.
- ***Rasselas*** = Johnson; ***Tristram Shandy*** = Sterne.
- 🔥 Goldsmith: ***The Vicar of Wakefield*** (novel), ***She Stoops to Conquer*** (comedy), ***The Deserted Village*** (poem).
- ***The Castle of Otranto*** = first Gothic novel (Walpole).

**2.** Who is commonly called the "Father of the English novel"?
(a) Henry Fielding (b) Samuel Johnson (c) Laurence Sterne (d) Goldsmith

**3.** Johnson's Dictionary was published in —
(a) 1719 (b) 1791 (c) 1755 (d) 1766

**4.** "She Stoops to Conquer" is a —
(a) novel (b) comedy (c) tragedy (d) poem

**5.** "The Life of Samuel Johnson" was written by —
(a) Goldsmith (b) Boswell (c) Burke (d) Smollett

**6.** Which novel began as a parody of Pamela?
(a) Tom Jones (b) Clarissa (c) Tristram Shandy (d) Joseph Andrews

**7.** "The Deserted Village" was written by —
(a) Oliver Goldsmith (b) Thomas Gray (c) Samuel Johnson (d) Alexander Pope

**8.** "Rasselas" was written by —
(a) Richardson (b) Sterne (c) Fielding (d) Dr. Johnson
