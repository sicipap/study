# Socrates, Plato, Aristotle & famous quotes

> **Why it matters:** The three great Greek philosophers, their teacher–student chain, their books and their famous sayings are among the most frequently asked facts in the ethics section and in general knowledge.

## The chain: teacher → student 🔥
**Socrates → Plato → Aristotle → Alexander the Great**
Socrates taught Plato; Plato taught Aristotle (for about 20 years at the Academy); Aristotle was the tutor of Alexander the Great.

## Socrates (c. 470–399 BC)
- Born in **Athens**. He **wrote nothing**; we know him through **Plato's dialogues** and Xenophon.
- **Socratic method (dialectic):** teaching by asking questions and exposing contradictions.
- Regarded as the **founder of moral philosophy**.
- In **399 BC** he was tried for **impiety and "corrupting the youth"** and sentenced to death by **drinking hemlock** (হেমলক বিষ).
- Sayings: **"Virtue is knowledge"**; **"Know thyself"** (an old Delphic saying he made central); **"The unexamined life is not worth living"** (from Plato's *Apology*); "I know that I know nothing".

## Plato (c. 427–347 BC) 🔥
- Student of Socrates; founded the **Academy** in Athens (**c. 387 BC**), often called the first institution of higher learning in the Western world.
- Main works: ***The Republic*** (his ideal state), ***The Statesman***, ***The Laws***, *Apology*, *Symposium*.
- **Theory of Forms (Ideas):** the real world is the world of perfect Forms; the physical world is its shadow (**allegory of the cave** in *The Republic*).
- **Philosopher-king:** "Until philosophers are kings … cities will never have rest from their evils."
- **Justice** = each class doing its own proper function without interfering in others.
- Three classes of the ideal state, matching three parts of the soul:

| Class | Part of soul | Virtue |
|---|---|---|
| Rulers (philosophers) | Reason | **Wisdom** |
| Guardians / soldiers | Spirit | **Courage** |
| Producers (farmers, artisans) | Appetite | **Temperance** (self-control) |

Justice holds the three in harmony. **Wisdom, courage, temperance and justice** are the **four cardinal virtues**.

## Aristotle (384–322 BC) 🔥
- Born in **Stagira**; studied at Plato's Academy; tutor of **Alexander the Great**; founded the **Lyceum** in Athens (**335 BC**). His followers were called **Peripatetics** (from walking while teaching).
- Called the **father of political science**, and also of logic and biology.
- Main works: ***Politics***, ***Nicomachean Ethics***, *Metaphysics*, *Poetics*, *Rhetoric*.
- **Golden mean**, **eudaimonia** (happiness as the highest good).
- Classified constitutions into six types:

| Rule by | Good form (common interest) | Corrupt form (self-interest) |
|---|---|---|
| One | Monarchy | Tyranny |
| Few | Aristocracy | Oligarchy |
| Many | Polity | Democracy |

- Sayings: **"Man is by nature a political animal"**; **"The law is reason free from passion"**; "We are what we repeatedly do" is a later summary of his view, not his exact words.

## Quick revision
- Socrates → Plato → Aristotle → Alexander.
- Socrates wrote nothing; died by hemlock in 399 BC.
- Plato: Academy, *The Republic*, philosopher-king, theory of Forms, four cardinal virtues.
- Aristotle: Lyceum, *Politics*, *Nicomachean Ethics*, father of political science.
- "Man is a political animal" = Aristotle.
- "Absolute power corrupts absolutely" = Lord Acton.
- "Man is born free …" = Rousseau.
