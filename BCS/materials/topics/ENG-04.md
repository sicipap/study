# Old & Middle English: Beowulf, Chaucer

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask about *Beowulf* (hero, monster, language, period), Chaucer's epithets and *The Canterbury Tales* (number of pilgrims, tales, the Wife of Bath), and other early works.

## Old English period (450–1066)
- Language: **Old English (Anglo-Saxon)**, brought by the Angles, Saxons and Jutes.
- Verse is **alliterative** (repeated initial sounds), with a **caesura** (pause) in the middle of each line; no rhyme.
- Four main manuscripts survive; *Beowulf* is in the **Cotton Vitellius A.xv** (Nowell Codex).

### Beowulf 🔥
| Point | Fact |
|---|---|
| Genre | **Oldest surviving epic in English** / the national epic of the Anglo-Saxons |
| Length | About **3,182 lines** |
| Author | **Unknown (anonymous)** |
| Date | Composed c. **8th century** (some say 7th–10th); manuscript c. 1000 |
| Setting | **Denmark and Sweden (Geatland)** — not England |
| Hero | **Beowulf**, a prince of the **Geats** |
| Danish king helped | **Hrothgar**, whose mead-hall is **Heorot** |
| Monsters | **Grendel**, **Grendel's mother**, and a **dragon** |
| Ending | Beowulf, now old king of the Geats, kills the dragon but **dies**; his young follower **Wiglaf** helps him |

### Other Old English writers
| Writer | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Caedmon** | **First known English poet**; "Caedmon's Hymn"; called the "Father of English song" |
| **Cynewulf** | Signed his poems in runes; *Elene*, *Juliana*, *Christ II* |
| **The Venerable Bede** | ***Ecclesiastical History of the English People*** (written in **Latin**, 731); "Father of English history" |
| **King Alfred the Great** | **Father of English prose**; translated Latin works; began the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* |
| Elegies | *The Wanderer*, *The Seafarer*, *The Wife's Lament* |

## Middle English period (1066–1500)
- After the **Norman Conquest (1066)**, French became the language of the court; English absorbed many French words.
- Key works:
  - ***Sir Gawain and the Green Knight*** — Arthurian romance by the anonymous **Pearl Poet (Gawain Poet)**.
  - ***Piers Plowman*** — allegory by **William Langland**.
  - ***Confessio Amantis*** — **John Gower** ("moral Gower", Chaucer's friend).
  - ***Le Morte d'Arthur*** — **Sir Thomas Malory**, printed by **Caxton in 1485**.
  - **John Wycliffe** — first complete translation of the **Bible into English** (1380s); "Morning star of the Reformation".
  - **Mystery, Miracle and Morality plays**, e.g. ***Everyman***.

## Geoffrey Chaucer (c. 1343–1400) 🔥
### Epithets
- **"Father of English poetry"** — **Dryden** called him this.
- **"Father of English literature"** (common exam answer; some guides also give **"Father of the English language"**).
- **"Well of English undefiled"** — **Spenser** called him this.
- Do not confuse: **"Morning star of the Reformation"** is **John Wycliffe**.
- He was the **first poet buried in Poets' Corner**, Westminster Abbey.

### Works
| Work | Fact |
|---|---|
| ***The Canterbury Tales*** 🔥 | His masterpiece; unfinished; written in the **heroic couplet** (mostly) in the **East Midland (London) dialect** |
| ***Troilus and Criseyde*** | Long love poem in **rhyme royal**; set in Troy |
| ***The Book of the Duchess*** | Elegy for Blanche, wife of John of Gaunt |
| ***The House of Fame***, ***The Parliament of Fowls*** | Dream-vision poems |
| ***The Legend of Good Women*** | Stories of faithful women |
| *Treatise on the Astrolabe* | Prose, scientific |

### The Canterbury Tales 🔥
- **Pilgrims** go from the **Tabard Inn** in **Southwark (London)** to the shrine of **Saint Thomas Becket** at **Canterbury**.
- The **General Prologue** introduces **29 pilgrims** (plus Chaucer himself; the Host **Harry Bailey** is the 31st). Most guides say **"29 pilgrims"**.
- Plan: each pilgrim tells 2 tales going and 2 returning (about 120 tales). Chaucer finished only **24 tales**.
- Famous characters: **the Knight** (first tale), **the Wife of Bath** (married five times), **the Prioress (Madame Eglantine)**, **the Pardoner**, **the Miller**, **the Clerk of Oxford**, **the Summoner**, **the Monk**, **the Friar**, **the Nun's Priest** (Chanticleer the cock).
- Opening line: **"Whan that Aprill with his shoures soote…"**
- Chaucer's Knight is described as **"a verray, parfit gentil knyght"**.

### Chaucer's three periods
| Period | Works |
|---|---|
| **French** | *The Book of the Duchess* |
| **Italian** | *Troilus and Criseyde*, *The House of Fame*, *The Parliament of Fowls* |
| **English** (mature) | ***The Canterbury Tales*** |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 *Beowulf*: oldest English epic, anonymous, hero a **Geat**, monsters **Grendel**, his mother and a **dragon**.
- 🔥 Chaucer: **"Father of English poetry"**.
- *The Canterbury Tales*: **29 pilgrims**, **Tabard Inn**, journey to **Canterbury**.
- **Caedmon** = first English poet; **Alfred** = father of English prose.
- **Bede** wrote his *Ecclesiastical History* in **Latin**.
- **Malory's** *Le Morte d'Arthur* printed by **Caxton (1485)**.
- **Spenser** called Chaucer "**Well of English undefiled**".
