# Drama: Shaw, Wilde, Beckett, Osborne

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask who wrote a play, which play a character belongs to, the subtitle of a play, and labels such as "Theatre of the Absurd", "Angry Young Men" and "comedy of manners".

## George Bernard Shaw (1856–1950), Irish 🔥
- Nobel Prize in Literature **1925**. He also won an **Academy Award (Oscar)** for the screenplay of *Pygmalion* (1938 film). He is often cited as the **first person to win both a Nobel and an Oscar** (Bob Dylan later did both too).
- Member of the socialist **Fabian Society**. Admirer of Ibsen: wrote ***The Quintessence of Ibsenism*** (1891).
- Known for the **drama of ideas / discussion play**, long prefaces, wit. Adjective: **Shavian**.

## Oscar Wilde (1854–1900), Irish 🔥
- Leader of **Aestheticism**: "**Art for art's sake**". Famous for epigrams and the **comedy of manners**.
- Jailed in **1895** for "gross indecency"; in prison he wrote the long letter ***De Profundis***; later the poem ***The Ballad of Reading Gaol*** (1898). He died in Paris in 1900.

| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| ***The Picture of Dorian Gray*** | 1890 (book 1891) | His **only novel**; **Dorian Gray** stays young while his portrait ages; painter **Basil Hallward**, **Lord Henry Wotton** |
| *Lady Windermere's Fan* | 1892 | "I can resist everything except temptation." |
| *Salomé* | 1891 (written in **French**) | One-act tragedy |
| *A Woman of No Importance* | 1893 | |
| *An Ideal Husband* | 1895 | |
| ***The Importance of Being Earnest*** 🔥 | **1895** | Subtitle "**A Trivial Comedy for Serious People**"; **Jack (John) Worthing**, **Algernon Moncrieff**, **Gwendolen Fairfax**, **Cecily Cardew**, **Lady Bracknell**, **Miss Prism**; pun on "Earnest / Ernest"; the "Bunburying" joke |
| Fairy tales | 1888, 1891 | ***The Happy Prince***, *The Selfish Giant*, *The Nightingale and the Rose* |

## Samuel Beckett (1906–1989), Irish 🔥
- Lived in Paris; wrote in **French and English**. Nobel Prize in Literature **1969**.
- Leading dramatist of the **Theatre of the Absurd**. The term was coined by critic **Martin Esslin** (book, 1961). Absurd drama shows the meaninglessness of life, with little plot, circular action and broken language.

| Work | Year | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| ***Waiting for Godot*** 🔥 | French *En attendant Godot* staged **1953**; English 1955 | Subtitle "**a tragicomedy in two acts**"; tramps **Vladimir (Didi)** and **Estragon (Gogo)** wait for Godot, who never comes; **Pozzo** and his slave **Lucky**; a Boy. Opening line: "**Nothing to be done.**" Critic Vivian Mercier: a play in which "nothing happens, twice" |
| ***Endgame*** | 1957 | **Hamm**, **Clov**, **Nagg**, **Nell** (parents in dustbins) |
| *Krapp's Last Tape* | 1958 | Old man and his tape recorder |
| *Happy Days* | 1961 | **Winnie** buried in a mound; **Willie** |
| Novels | 1938–1953 | *Murphy*; trilogy *Molloy*, *Malone Dies*, *The Unnamable* |

Other absurdists: **Eugène Ionesco** (*The Bald Soprano*, *Rhinoceros*), **Jean Genet**, **Harold Pinter** ("comedy of menace": *The Birthday Party*, *The Caretaker*, *The Homecoming*; Nobel 2005), **Edward Albee** (*The Zoo Story*).

## John Osborne (1929–1994), English 🔥
- ***Look Back in Anger*** (**1956**, **Royal Court Theatre**, London) changed post-war British theatre.
- Characters: **Jimmy Porter** (angry, working-class, university-educated), his wife **Alison**, friend **Cliff Lewis**, actress **Helena Charles**, Alison's father **Colonel Redfern**; the couple's "**bears and squirrels**" game.
- It began the "**Angry Young Men**" label and "**kitchen sink drama**" (realistic drama of ordinary working-class homes).
- Other plays: ***The Entertainer*** (1957, Archie Rice), ***Luther*** (1961), *Inadmissible Evidence* (1964).
- Other "Angry Young Men": **Kingsley Amis** (*Lucky Jim*, 1954), **John Wain**, **John Braine** (*Room at the Top*), **Alan Sillitoe**, **Arnold Wesker**.

## Quick revision
- *Arms and the Man*, *Pygmalion*, *Man and Superman*, *Saint Joan*: **Shaw** (Nobel 1925).
- "Chocolate cream soldier" = **Bluntschli**.
- *Pygmalion* → musical **My Fair Lady**; **Eliza Doolittle, Henry Higgins**.
- *The Importance of Being Earnest* (1895): "A Trivial Comedy for Serious People": **Wilde**.
- Wilde's only novel: ***The Picture of Dorian Gray***.
- *Waiting for Godot*: **Vladimir, Estragon, Pozzo, Lucky**; **Theatre of the Absurd** (term: **Martin Esslin**).
- *Look Back in Anger* (1956): **Jimmy Porter**; "**Angry Young Men**", **kitchen sink drama**.

(a) Candida (b) Saint Joan (c) Man and Superman (d) Pygmalion

**3.** "A Trivial Comedy for Serious People" is the subtitle of:
(a) The Importance of Being Earnest (b) An Ideal Husband (c) Lady Windermere's Fan (d) A Woman of No Importance

**4.** Vladimir and Estragon are characters in:
(a) Endgame (b) The Caretaker (c) Waiting for Godot (d) Happy Days

**5.** The term "Theatre of the Absurd" was coined by:
(a) Samuel Beckett (b) Harold Pinter (c) T. S. Eliot (d) Martin Esslin

**6.** Jimmy Porter is the hero of:
(a) Look Back in Anger (b) Lucky Jim (c) The Entertainer (d) Room at the Top

**7.** Oscar Wilde's only novel is:
(a) De Profundis (b) The Happy Prince (c) The Ballad of Reading Gaol (d) The Picture of Dorian Gray

**8.** Who was called the "chocolate cream soldier"?
(a) Sergius (b) Bluntschli (c) Higgins (d) Tanner

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Shaw's anti-romantic comedy of 1894 |
| 2 | d | Flower girl trained by Henry Higgins in *Pygmalion* |
| 3 | a | Wilde's 1895 comedy of manners |
| 4 | c | The two tramps in Beckett's *Waiting for Godot* |
| 5 | d | Martin Esslin's book of 1961 |
| 6 | a | Osborne's 1956 play |
| 7 | d | *Dorian Gray* is his only novel |
| 8 | b | Bluntschli in *Arms and the Man* |
