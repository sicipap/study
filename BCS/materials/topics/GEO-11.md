# Population, urbanization, migration

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask Malthus's theory, demographic terms (TFR, density, sex ratio, dependency ratio), world and Bangladesh population facts from the 2022 census, megacities, and push–pull migration theory. Figures change, so learn the year with each number.

## Key demographic terms 🔥
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Demography (জনমিতি)** | Scientific study of population |
| **Crude Birth Rate (CBR)** | Live births per **1,000** people per year |
| **Crude Death Rate (CDR)** | Deaths per **1,000** people per year |
| **Natural growth rate** | CBR − CDR (migration not counted) |
| 🔥 **Total Fertility Rate (TFR)** | Average number of children a woman would have in her lifetime. **Replacement level ≈ 2.1** |
| **Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)** | Deaths of babies under **1 year** per **1,000 live births** |
| **Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR)** | Maternal deaths per **1,00,000 live births** |
| 🔥 **Population density** | People per **square kilometre** |
| 🔥 **Sex ratio** | Number of **males per 100 females** (in Bangladesh) |
| **Dependency ratio** | Dependants (0–14 and 65+) per 100 working-age people (15–64) |
| 🔥 **Demographic dividend** | Economic boost when the **working-age share** of the population is large — Bangladesh is in this phase now |
| **Census (আদমশুমারি / জনশুমারি)** | Official count of the whole population, usually every 10 years |

## Population theories 🔥
- 🔥 **Thomas Robert Malthus**, *An Essay on the Principle of Population* (**1798**): population grows in a **geometric progression** (1, 2, 4, 8…) but food supply only in an **arithmetic progression** (1, 2, 3, 4…). **Positive checks** (famine, disease, war) and **preventive checks** (late marriage, moral restraint) control population.
- **Demographic Transition Model:** societies move from **high birth and death rates** → falling death rates (population explodes) → falling birth rates → **low birth and death rates** (stable). Linked with **Warren Thompson** and **Frank Notestein**.
- **Optimum population:** the size giving the highest income per person.
- **Population pyramid:** broad base = young, fast-growing (expansive); narrow base = ageing (constrictive).

## World population 🔥
- World population reached **8 billion on 15 November 2022** (UN). It was about **8.2 billion in 2025**.
- 🔥 **India** became the **most populous country in 2023**, passing **China** (UN estimate).
- **11 July = World Population Day** (started by UNDP in 1989, after the "Day of Five Billion", 11 July 1987).
- **UNFPA** (UN Population Fund): started **1969**, HQ **New York**.
- Most densely populated country (excluding city-states such as Monaco and Singapore): **Bangladesh** is among the highest in the world.

## Urbanization (নগরায়ণ) 🔥
- **Urbanization:** the growing share of people living in **towns and cities**.
- **Causes (push–pull):** rural poverty, river erosion, landlessness, disasters (**push**); jobs (garment industry), education, health care, services in cities (**pull**).
- More than **half the world's people** now live in urban areas (the UN estimated **55% in 2018**, rising towards **68% by 2050**).
- 🔥 **Megacity:** a city with **10 million or more** people. **Dhaka** is one of the world's largest and most densely populated megacities.
  - The UN's older ranking (World Urbanization Prospects 2018) put **Tokyo** first. Its **2025** revision, using a new definition of cities, ranked **Jakarta** first and **Dhaka** second, and projects Dhaka to become the largest by about 2050.
- **Primate city:** a city far larger than any other in the country (idea by **Mark Jefferson**, 1939). **Dhaka** is the primate city of Bangladesh.
- **Conurbation:** several cities that grow together into one urban area. **Urban sprawl:** unplanned spread of a city outward. **Counter-urbanization:** people moving out of cities to rural areas.
- **Problems:** slums, traffic congestion, waterlogging, air pollution, housing and water shortages.

> 🔄 Update: city rankings change with each UN revision; check the latest.

## Migration (অভিবাসন) 🔥
- **Migration:** movement of people from one place to another to live.
- **Internal** (within a country, e.g. rural → urban) vs **international** (across borders). **Emigration** = leaving a country; **immigration** = entering a country.
- 🔥 **Laws of migration:** **E. G. Ravenstein** (1885). **Push–pull theory:** **Everett Lee** (1966).
- **Brain drain:** skilled and educated people leaving the country.
- **Remittance:** money sent home by migrants — a major source of foreign exchange for Bangladesh.
- **Climate migrants:** people forced to move by sea-level rise, erosion, cyclones and salinity.
- **Refugee:** a person who flees their country because of persecution or war. **Rohingya:** over **one million** live in camps in **Cox's Bazar**, most arriving after **August 2017**; some have been relocated to **Bhasan Char** (Noakhali).
- **Institutions:** **BMET** (Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training, 1976) handles overseas employment; **IOM** (International Organization for Migration, 1951, HQ **Geneva**); **UNHCR** (UN Refugee Agency, 1950, **Geneva**).
- **18 December = International Migrants Day**.

## Quick revision
- Malthus (**1798**): population **geometric**, food **arithmetic**.
- Replacement-level TFR ≈ **2.1**.
- World population **8 billion on 15 November 2022**; most populous country: **India (2023)**.
- World Population Day: **11 July**.
- Bangladesh Census 2022: **16.51 crore** enumerated; density **~1,119/sq km**; sex ratio **~98**; growth **1.22%**; urban **~31.5%**.
- Megacity = **10 million+**; Dhaka is Bangladesh's **primate city**.
- Laws of migration: **Ravenstein**; push–pull: **Everett Lee**.
- International Migrants Day: **18 December**.
