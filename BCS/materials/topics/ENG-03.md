# Pronoun & its agreement

> **Why it matters:** Exams test the right case (I/me, who/whom), the pronoun after "than/as/between/let", the order "you, he and I", and agreement with "each, everyone, neither".

## Kinds of pronoun
| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Personal** | I, we, you, he, she, it, they (and me, us, him, her, them) |
| **Possessive** | mine, ours, yours, his, hers, theirs |
| **Reflexive / Emphatic** | myself, yourself, himself, itself, ourselves, themselves |
| **Demonstrative** | this, that, these, those, such |
| **Interrogative** | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| **Relative** | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| **Indefinite** | one, none, any, some, somebody, everyone, all, few |
| **Distributive** | **each, either, neither** |
| **Reciprocal** | **each other** (two), **one another** (more than two) |

- **Reflexive:** the action comes back to the subject: "He hurt **himself**."
- **Emphatic:** only for emphasis: "I **myself** saw it."
- "**Hisself**", "**theirselves**", "**ourself**" (in ordinary use) are wrong.

## Case rules 🔥
- **Subject** of a verb → nominative (I, he, she, we, they, who).
- **Object** of a verb or **preposition** → objective (me, him, her, us, them, whom).
  - "**Between you and me**" (not "between you and I").
  - "**Let him and me** go." ("let" takes object pronouns.)
  - "It is **I**." / "It was **he**." (formal: after the verb *be*, nominative).
- **After than / as**, supply the missing verb in your head:
  - "He is taller **than I** (am)."
  - "I love you more **than him**" (= than I love him) ≠ "more **than he**" (= than he loves you).
  - "She is as clever **as he** (is)."
- **Who vs whom:** "**Who** do you think is right?" (subject of *is*). "**Whom** did you meet?" (object of *meet*).

## Order of personal pronouns 🔥
- Good things: **2nd, 3rd, 1st** → "**You, he and I** are friends." (231)
- Admitting fault / blame: **1st, 2nd, 3rd** → "**I, you and he** are to blame." (123)

## Agreement with the antecedent 🔥
A pronoun agrees with its **antecedent** (the noun it replaces) in number, gender and person.

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| **each, every, either, neither, everyone, anyone, no one, somebody** → singular | "**Each** of the boys has done **his** duty." "**Everyone** must do **his** (or his or her) duty." |
| **one** → one's | "**One** should do **one's** duty." (not *his* in strict usage) |
| Two singulars joined by **and** → plural | "**Rahim and Karim** have done **their** work." |
| Two singulars by **or / nor / either…or / neither…or** → singular | "**Either Rahim or Karim** has lost **his** pen." |
| Same person described by two nouns with one article | "**The secretary and treasurer** has resigned **his** post." (one person) |
| Collective noun as one unit → it | "**The jury** gave **its** verdict." |
| Collective noun as members → they | "**The jury** were divided in **their** opinions." |
| **each other** (two) / **one another** (many) | "The two brothers love **each other**." "The students helped **one another**." |

## Relative pronoun points
- **who** for persons, **which** for things/animals, **that** for both.
- Use **that** (not *who/which*) after a **superlative**, **all, same, only, any, nothing, none**: "He is **the best** man **that** I know." "**All that** glitters is not gold."
- Use **whose** for possession: "the boy **whose** father is a doctor".
- Verb after a relative pronoun agrees with its antecedent: "It is **I who am** to blame." "He is **one of the boys who are** clever." (antecedent = boys).

## "None", "such", "same"
- **none** → usually plural in modern use with countables, but exams often prefer **singular** with "none of … is"; both are accepted.
- Avoid using "**such**" or "**same**" as a pronoun in letters: "Please send **them**" (not "send the same").

## Common mistakes
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| Between you and I. | Between you and **me**. |
| Let you and I go. | Let you and **me** go. |
| He is taller than me. (formal writing) | He is taller than **I** (am). |
| I, you and he are friends. | **You, he and I** are friends. |
| Each of the girls have done their work. | Each of the girls **has** done **her** work. |
| Whom do you think is coming? | **Who** do you think is coming? |
| He hurt hisself. | He hurt **himself**. |
| The two sisters love one another. | The two sisters love **each other**. |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 "Between you and **me**"; "Let **him and me** go".
- 🔥 Order: good = **you, he and I**; fault = **I, you and he**.
- **each/every/either/neither** → singular verb and pronoun.
- **each other** = two; **one another** = more than two.
- Use **that** after superlatives and "all".
- "It is **I who am** …" — verb agrees with the antecedent.
- **One** takes **one's**.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** Choose the correct option: "This is a secret between you and ___."
(a) I (b) me (c) myself (d) mine

**2.** Choose the correct order.
(a) I, you and he are friends. (b) He, I and you are friends. (c) I, he and you are friends. (d) You, he and I are friends.

**3.** "Each of the students ___ submitted ___ assignment."
(a) has, his (b) have, their (c) have, his (d) has, their own

**4.** "One should do ___ duty."
(a) his (b) their (c) one's (d) her

**5.** The two brothers love ___.
(a) one another (b) each other (c) themselves (d) theirselves

**6.** "He is the best man ___ I have ever met."
(a) who (b) whom (c) which (d) that

**7.** "It is I who ___ to blame."
(a) is (b) are (c) am (d) be

**8.** "___ do you think will win the match?"
(a) Who (b) Whom (c) Whose (d) Which of

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Object of the preposition "between" |
| 2 | d | Good sense: 2nd, 3rd, 1st person |
| 3 | a | "Each" takes a singular verb and pronoun |
| 4 | c | "One" takes "one's" |
| 5 | b | Two persons: each other |
| 6 | d | "that" after a superlative |
| 7 | c | Verb agrees with the antecedent "I" |
| 8 | a | Subject of "will win", so "who" |
