# Ethics & morality: concept, sources

> **Why it matters:** BCS Preliminary has a separate section on "Ethics, values and good governance" (নৈতিকতা, মূল্যবোধ ও সুশাসন). The opening questions usually ask where the words "ethics" and "morality" come from, what kind of science ethics is, its branches, and the sources of morality.

## Meaning of ethics and morality 🔥
- **Ethics (নৈতিকতা/নীতিবিদ্যা)** comes from the Greek word **"ethos"**, meaning custom, habit or character.
- **Morality (নৈতিকতা/নীতি)** comes from the Latin word **"mores"**, meaning customs or manners.
- Ethics is the **systematic study of right and wrong, good and bad** in human conduct. It is also called **moral philosophy (নীতিদর্শন)**.
- Ethics is a **normative science (আদর্শনিষ্ঠ/আদর্শমূলক বিজ্ঞান)**: it studies **what ought to be**, not just what is. Physics or economics, which describe what is, are **positive (descriptive) sciences**.
- Ethics is a branch of **philosophy**.

## Branches of ethics
| Branch | What it studies |
|---|---|
| **Meta-ethics** | The meaning and nature of moral words ("good", "right") |
| **Normative ethics** | Standards for right and wrong action (e.g. utilitarianism, Kant's duty ethics, virtue ethics) |
| **Applied ethics** | Real-life issues: **business ethics, bioethics, environmental ethics**, professional ethics |
| **Descriptive ethics** | What people actually believe is moral |

## Ethics vs morality
| Ethics | Morality |
|---|---|
| A theoretical, systematic study | Practical, personal belief and behaviour |
| Often set by a system or profession (code of ethics) | Comes from culture, religion and upbringing |
| More universal | Can differ from person to person and society to society |

In everyday Bangla both are often called **নৈতিকতা**; in exams, "ethics" is the study and "morality" is the practice.

## Sources of ethics and morality 🔥
1. **Religion:** the strongest traditional source.
2. **Family:** the **first school of morality**.
3. **Society, customs and culture.**
4. **Conscience (বিবেক):** the **inner source**, often called the "inner voice".
5. **Law:** it supports morality, but it is an **outer (external) source**.
6. **Education and philosophy (reason).**
7. **Literature, role models and institutions.**

## Morality vs law 🔥
| Law | Morality |
|---|---|
| Made and **enforced by the state** | Enforced by conscience and society |
| Breaking it brings **legal punishment** (sanction) | Breaking it brings guilt or social disapproval |
| Deals mainly with **outward actions** | Deals with **both intention and action** |
| Definite and written | Wider, often unwritten |

Law and morality overlap, but not every immoral act is illegal (e.g. ingratitude), and a law can itself be unjust.

## Features and importance of ethics
- It is **voluntary** in practice: a moral act must be done freely and knowingly.
- It judges **voluntary human actions**, not natural events.
- It gives **standards** to judge conduct and builds **character**.
- In public life, ethics is the base of **integrity, accountability and good governance**; that is why Bangladesh adopted the **National Integrity Strategy (2012)**.

## Key thinkers (starter list)
| Thinker | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Socrates** | Regarded as the founder of moral philosophy. **"Virtue is knowledge"**; taught **"Know thyself"** |
| **Aristotle** | Wrote ***Nicomachean Ethics***, the first systematic book on ethics. **Golden mean**: virtue is the middle path between extremes |
| **Jeremy Bentham** | Founded **utilitarianism**: **"the greatest happiness of the greatest number"** |
| **Immanuel Kant** | Duty-based ethics: act only by a rule you would want everyone to follow (**categorical imperative**) |

Note: some books call **Socrates** the "father of ethics/moral philosophy" and others give that title to **Aristotle**, as the author of the first systematic treatise on ethics. If both appear as options, read the question wording carefully ("moral philosophy" → Socrates; "first systematic book on ethics" → Aristotle).

## Quick revision
- Ethics ← Greek "ethos"; morality ← Latin "mores".
- Ethics is a normative science and a branch of philosophy.
- Branches: meta-ethics, normative, applied, descriptive.
- Bioethics and business ethics belong to applied ethics.
- Family = first school of morality; conscience = inner source; law = outer source.
- Law is enforced by the state; morality by conscience and society.
- "Virtue is knowledge" = Socrates; *Nicomachean Ethics* = Aristotle.
