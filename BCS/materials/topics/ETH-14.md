# Emotional intelligence & professional integrity

> **Why it matters:** Emotional intelligence (আবেগীয় বুদ্ধিমত্তা) is now part of the ethics syllabus. Exams ask who coined the term, who popularised it, Goleman's five components, and how integrity, professionalism and whistleblowing work in public service.

## Emotional intelligence (EI) 🔥
- **Definition:** the ability to **recognise, understand, use and manage one's own emotions and those of others**.
- **EQ (emotional quotient)** is the popular name for a measure of EI, compared with IQ.
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Term introduced (scientific model) | **Peter Salovey and John D. Mayer**, **1990** |
| Popularised by | **Daniel Goleman**, book ***Emotional Intelligence: Why It Can Matter More Than IQ*** (**1995**) |
| Background idea | **Howard Gardner**'s theory of **multiple intelligences** (*Frames of Mind*, **1983**), especially **interpersonal** and **intrapersonal** intelligence |

### Goleman's five components 🔥
| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Self-awareness** (আত্মসচেতনতা) | Knowing your own emotions, strengths and weaknesses |
| **2. Self-regulation** (আত্মনিয়ন্ত্রণ) | Controlling impulses and moods; thinking before acting |
| **3. Motivation** (প্রেরণা) | Working for inner reasons, not only money or status |
| **4. Empathy** (সহমর্মিতা) | Understanding other people's feelings and viewpoints |
| **5. Social skills** (সামাজিক দক্ষতা) | Managing relationships, communication, teamwork, handling conflict |

The first three are **personal competence**; empathy and social skills are **social competence**.

### Mayer–Salovey four-branch model
**Perceiving emotions → Using emotions → Understanding emotions → Managing emotions.**

### Empathy vs sympathy
- **Sympathy:** feeling **pity or sorrow for** someone.
- **Empathy:** **putting yourself in another person's place** and understanding their feelings from their point of view. Empathy is the quality valued in public service.

### Why EI matters for a civil servant
- Staying calm under pressure and with angry citizens.
- Fair, humane decisions for the poor and vulnerable.
- Better teamwork, leadership and conflict handling.
- Resisting pressure and temptation (self-regulation supports integrity).

Goleman opens his book with **Aristotle's** challenge: anyone can become angry, but to be angry with the right person, to the right degree, at the right time, for the right purpose and in the right way is not easy.

## Professional integrity (পেশাগত সততা/শুদ্ধাচার) 🔥
- **"Integrity"** comes from the Latin **"integer"**, meaning **whole or complete**: a person of integrity is the same in private and in public, in word and in deed.
- **Professional integrity** means following the **ethical standards of one's profession** consistently, even when no one is watching and even at personal cost.
- **Elements:** honesty, consistency, **moral courage**, keeping promises, fairness, **accountability**, confidentiality, and competence.
- **Professional ethics / code of ethics:** written standards for a profession (e.g. civil service conduct rules, medical ethics, the bar council's rules).
- Bangladesh's **National Integrity Strategy (2012)** defines integrity (শুদ্ধাচার) as behavioural excellence influenced by morality and honesty.

### Threats to integrity
Political pressure, bribery and gifts, conflict of interest, nepotism, fear of transfer, peer pressure, and "everyone does it" thinking.

### Whistleblowing (হুইসেলব্লোয়িং)
- Reporting wrongdoing in an organisation to someone who can act on it.
- Bangladesh: **জনস্বার্থ সংশ্লিষ্ট তথ্য প্রকাশ (সুরক্ষা প্রদান) আইন, ২০১১** (Public Interest Information Disclosure (Provide Protection) Act 2011) protects whistleblowers who disclose in good faith.

### Ethical dilemma
A situation where a person must choose between **two right values** (e.g. loyalty to a superior vs honesty to the public). Tools: the law and rules, the public interest, conscience, the "newspaper test" (would I be comfortable if this appeared in tomorrow's paper?), and advice from seniors or an ethics committee.

## Quick revision
- EI coined by Salovey and Mayer (1990); popularised by Daniel Goleman (1995).
- Goleman's five: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills.
- Gardner (1983): multiple intelligences, including interpersonal and intrapersonal.
- Empathy = understanding from the other's viewpoint; sympathy = feeling pity.
- Integrity ← Latin "integer" (whole).
- Whistleblower protection law: 2011.
- Ethical dilemma = conflict between two right values.

(a) sympathy (b) apathy (c) empathy (d) antipathy

(a) Daniel Goleman (b) Peter Salovey (c) John Rawls (d) Howard Gardner

(a) Motivation (b) Self-regulation (c) Social skills (d) Empathy
