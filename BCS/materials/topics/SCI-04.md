# Heat & temperature

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask heat vs temperature, units, absolute zero, the 4 °C anomaly of water, modes of heat transfer with examples, and why a pressure cooker cooks faster.

## Heat vs temperature 🔥
| Heat (তাপ) | Temperature (তাপমাত্রা) |
|---|---|
| A **form of energy**; total kinetic energy of molecules | **Degree of hotness**; average kinetic energy of molecules |
| Unit **joule (J)**; also calorie (1 cal ≈ 4.2 J) | SI unit **kelvin (K)**; also °C, °F |
| Measured by a **calorimeter** | Measured by a **thermometer** |
| Cause | Effect |

- Heat flows from **higher to lower temperature**, not from more heat to less heat.
- Joule showed that heat is a form of energy (mechanical equivalent of heat, J ≈ 4.2 J/cal).

## Temperature scales and thermometers 🔥
- **C/5 = (F − 32)/9 = (K − 273)/5**.
- **Absolute zero (পরম শূন্য) = 0 K = −273.15 °C (≈ −273 °C) = −459.67 °F**. At absolute zero molecular motion is (theoretically) minimum.
- **Kelvin scale** was given by **Lord Kelvin (William Thomson)**; it has no negative values.
- **−40 °C = −40 °F**. Body temperature **37 °C = 98.4 °F (NCTB) / 98.6 °F = 310 K**.

| Thermometer | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Clinical thermometer** | Range **35–42 °C (95–110 °F)**; has a **kink (constriction)** so mercury doesn't fall back |
| **Mercury thermometer** | Mercury freezes at **−39 °C**, boils at **357 °C**; shiny, uniform expansion, doesn't wet glass. Fahrenheit made it (1714) |
| **Alcohol thermometer** | For **very low temperatures** (alcohol freezes near −115 °C), e.g. polar regions |
| **Pyrometer** | **Very high temperature** (furnaces, Sun) — works by radiation |
| **Thermocouple** | Wide range, uses Seebeck effect |
| Galileo | Made the first thermometer (air thermoscope, c. 1593) |

## Specific heat and latent heat 🔥
- **Specific heat (আপেক্ষিক তাপ)**: heat needed to raise 1 kg by 1 K. Water: **4,200 J/kg·K** (1 cal/g·°C) — **highest among common substances**. So water is used in radiators and hot-water bags, and the sea heats and cools slowly (mild coastal climate).
- **Q = m s Δt** (heat = mass × specific heat × change in temperature).
- **Latent heat (সুপ্ত তাপ)**: heat absorbed/released during change of state **without change of temperature**.
  - Latent heat of **fusion of ice = 3.36 × 10⁵ J/kg (80 cal/g)**.
  - Latent heat of **vaporisation of water = 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg (≈540 cal/g)**. That is why **steam at 100 °C burns more severely than boiling water at 100 °C**.

## Anomalous expansion of water 🔥
- Water has **maximum density (minimum volume) at 4 °C**.
- From 0 °C to 4 °C water **contracts** on heating; above 4 °C it expands.
- Result: in cold countries lakes freeze **from the top**, water at 4 °C stays at the bottom and **fish survive**. Water pipes burst in winter because water expands on freezing. Ice floats (ice is less dense than water, about 0.92 g/cm³).

## Transfer of heat (তাপ সঞ্চালন) 🔥
| Mode | How | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduction (পরিবহন)** | Molecule to molecule, **no bulk movement**; mainly in **solids** | Metal spoon gets hot; cooking pots of metal with wooden/plastic handles. **Silver is the best conductor of heat**, then copper |
| **Convection (পরিচলন)** | **Movement of the heated fluid itself**; liquids and gases | **Sea breeze (day) and land breeze (night)**, boiling water, ventilators placed high, trade winds |
| **Radiation (বিকিরণ)** | **Needs no medium**; travels as electromagnetic (infrared) waves at the speed of light | **Sun's heat reaches Earth**; heat felt near a fire |

- **Thermos (vacuum) flask**, invented by **James Dewar** (1892): vacuum stops conduction and convection; silvered walls stop radiation.
- **Black, rough surfaces absorb and emit heat best**; white/shiny surfaces reflect. So **white clothes are cooler in summer**, dark clothes warmer in winter.
- Bad conductors (insulators): wood, air, wool, glass, plastic. Woollen clothes keep us warm because they **trap air**, a bad conductor.

## Boiling, melting and pressure 🔥
- Water boils at **100 °C at 1 atm**. **Boiling point rises with pressure** → a **pressure cooker** cooks faster (about 120 °C inside). Papin invented it ("Papin's digester").
- At **high altitude** (mountains) pressure is low, so water boils **below 100 °C** and food cooks slowly.
- **Impurities (salt) raise the boiling point** and **lower the freezing point** (salt is spread on icy roads; ice-salt mixture for ice cream).
- **Melting point of ice falls with pressure** → **regelation**; ice skating.
- **Evaporation (বাষ্পীভবন) causes cooling**: sweating, water in an earthen pot (কলসি) stays cool, we feel cold after spirit/acetone on skin. Evaporation happens at any temperature, only at the surface.
- **Dew point (শিশিরাঙ্ক)**: the temperature at which air becomes saturated and water vapour condenses as dew.

## Thermal expansion
- Gaps are left between **railway rails** and in bridges; telephone/electric wires **sag in summer**.
- **Bimetallic strip** (e.g. brass + iron) bends on heating → used in **thermostats**, fire alarms, electric irons.
- **Invar** (iron-nickel alloy) has very low expansion → pendulum clocks, measuring tapes.

## Quick revision
- Heat is energy (J); temperature is degree of hotness (K).
- **Absolute zero = 0 K = −273 °C**; −40 °C = −40 °F.
- Water: **maximum density at 4 °C**; specific heat **4,200 J/kg·K** (highest common).
- **Steam burns more** than boiling water (latent heat of vaporisation).
- **Sun's heat reaches Earth by radiation**; sea/land breeze by **convection**.
- **Pressure cooker**: higher pressure → higher boiling point.
- Clinical thermometer 35–42 °C; alcohol for low, pyrometer for very high temperature.
- Thermos flask: James Dewar.

(a) −273 °C (b) −100 °C (c) 0 °C (d) −459 °C

**4.** Food cooks faster in a pressure cooker because —
(a) heat is lost less (b) the boiling point of water rises (c) the boiling point falls (d) steam is removed

**5.** Which instrument measures very high temperatures, such as in a furnace?
(a) Clinical thermometer (b) Alcohol thermometer (c) Barometer (d) Pyrometer

**6.** Sea breeze and land breeze are examples of —
(a) convection (b) conduction (c) radiation (d) evaporation

**7.** Steam at 100 °C causes more severe burns than water at 100 °C because steam has —
(a) higher temperature (b) more specific heat (c) latent heat of vaporisation (d) lower density

**8.** The SI unit of temperature is —
(a) degree Celsius (b) kelvin (c) joule (d) calorie

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Anomalous expansion: water is densest at 4 °C |
| 2 | c | Radiation needs no medium; space is a vacuum |
| 3 | a | 0 K = −273.15 °C |
| 4 | b | Higher pressure raises boiling point to about 120 °C |
| 5 | d | Pyrometer works by radiation for very high temperature |
| 6 | a | Heated air rises, cooler air moves in: convection |
| 7 | c | Steam gives up extra latent heat (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg) when it condenses |
| 8 | b | Kelvin is the SI base unit of temperature |
