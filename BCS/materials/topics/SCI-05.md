# Sound

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the speed of sound in different media, audible range, ultrasonic/infrasonic uses, echo distance, unit of loudness and the Doppler effect.

## Nature of sound 🔥
- Sound is a form of energy produced by **vibration (কম্পন)**.
- Sound needs a **material medium**; it **cannot travel through a vacuum**. So astronauts on the Moon talk by radio (radio waves are electromagnetic).
- In air (and other fluids) sound travels as a **longitudinal wave (অনুদৈর্ঘ্য তরঙ্গ)** — compressions and rarefactions. In solids it can be both longitudinal and transverse. (Light, by contrast, is a transverse electromagnetic wave.)
- **Wave equation: v = f λ** (speed = frequency × wavelength). Frequency unit: **hertz (Hz)**.

## Speed of sound 🔥🔥
| Medium | Approx. speed |
|---|---|
| **Air at 0 °C** | **332 m/s** (NCTB value; many books say 330–332 m/s) |
| Air at about 20 °C | ≈ 343 m/s |
| **Water** | ≈ **1,450–1,500 m/s** (about 4 times air) |
| **Iron / steel** | ≈ **5,000–5,200 m/s** (about 15 times air) |

- Order: **solid > liquid > gas**. Speed is **zero in vacuum**.
- Speed in air **increases with temperature** (about **0.6 m/s per °C**) and with **humidity** (sound travels faster in moist air, so faster in the rainy season).
- Change of **pressure alone does not change** the speed of sound (at constant temperature).
- **Light is much faster** (3 × 10⁸ m/s), so we **see lightning before hearing thunder**.
- **Supersonic (শব্দোত্তর)**: faster than sound. **Mach number** = speed of object ÷ speed of sound (Mach 1 = speed of sound). A supersonic jet produces a **sonic boom**.

## Frequency ranges 🔥
| Type | Frequency | Examples / uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Infrasonic (শব্দেতর)** | **Below 20 Hz** | Produced by **earthquakes**, volcanoes; heard by **elephants, whales**, dogs sense some |
| **Audible (শ্রাব্যতার সীমা)** | **20 Hz – 20,000 Hz** | Human hearing |
| **Ultrasonic (শব্দোত্তর/অতিস্বনক)** | **Above 20,000 Hz (20 kHz)** | **Bats (echolocation), dolphins**; **SONAR**, **ultrasonography** (seeing a fetus), cleaning delicate parts, detecting flaws in metal, kidney-stone breaking |

- **SONAR = Sound Navigation And Ranging** — uses ultrasound to find sea depth, submarines, fish shoals.
- **Bats** fly in darkness by emitting ultrasound and hearing its echo.
- Dogs can hear up to about 40–45 kHz (dog whistles are ultrasonic to humans).

## Characteristics of sound 🔥
| Characteristic | Depends on |
|---|---|
| **Pitch (তীক্ষ্ণতা)** — shrillness | **Frequency**. Women's and children's voices have **higher pitch** than men's; a mosquito's buzz has higher pitch than a lion's roar |
| **Loudness / intensity (প্রাবল্য/তীব্রতা)** | **Amplitude**; also distance and area of vibrating body |
| **Quality / timbre (স্বরের গুণ)** | **Overtones / harmonics** (waveform); lets us recognise a voice or instrument |

- **Loudness unit: decibel (dB)**; the bel is named after **Alexander Graham Bell**. 0 dB ≈ threshold of hearing; normal talk ≈ 60 dB. Prolonged exposure above about **85 dB** can damage hearing; above ~120 dB is painful.
- Sound above a comfortable level is **noise (শব্দদূষণ)**; the loudness of noise is measured in dB with a sound-level meter.

## Echo and reverberation 🔥
- **Echo (প্রতিধ্বনি)**: reflected sound heard separately. The effect of a sound stays in our ear for about **0.1 s** (persistence of hearing).
- So the reflecting surface must be at least **≈ 16.6 m (about 17 m) away** in air (332 × 0.1 ÷ 2).
- Echo formula: **distance = (speed × time) ÷ 2**.
  - *Example:* echo heard after 2 s, speed 332 m/s → d = 332 × 2 ÷ 2 = **332 m**.
- **Reverberation**: many overlapping reflections in a hall; reduced with curtains, soft boards, carpets.
- Uses of reflection of sound: stethoscope, megaphone, whispering galleries, sound boards, ear trumpets.

## Other effects
- **Doppler effect** (Christian **Doppler**, 1842): apparent change of frequency when source and listener move relative to each other. The horn of an approaching train sounds **higher**, of a receding train **lower**. Also used in police radar and to find the speed of stars (red shift).
- **Resonance (অনুনাদ)**: large vibration when forced frequency = natural frequency. Soldiers **break step while crossing a bridge**; tuning a radio uses resonance.
- **Stethoscope** was invented by **René Laennec (1816)**; it works by multiple reflection of sound.
- Musical instruments: string (violin, sitar), wind (flute), percussion (drum, tabla).

## Quick revision
- Sound **cannot travel in vacuum**; it is a **longitudinal** wave in air.
- Speed in air ≈ **332 m/s at 0 °C**; **solid > liquid > gas**.
- Speed rises with **temperature and humidity**, not with pressure alone.
- **Audible range 20 Hz – 20 kHz**; above = ultrasonic, below = infrasonic.
- Bats and SONAR use **ultrasound**; earthquakes produce **infrasound**.
- **Pitch ↔ frequency; loudness ↔ amplitude; quality ↔ overtones**.
- Echo needs at least **≈17 m**; loudness unit **decibel**.
- Approaching train horn sounds higher: **Doppler effect**.

(a) infrasonic sound (b) radio waves (c) ultrasonic sound (d) infrared light

(a) frequency (b) amplitude (c) speed (d) wavelength only

(a) resonance (b) echo (c) reverberation (d) the Doppler effect

| 3 | c | Bats use ultrasonic echolocation |
