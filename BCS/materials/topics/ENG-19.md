# Conjunction

> **Why it matters:** Exams test correlative pairs (*either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also, no sooner … than, hardly … when, both … and, whether … or*), "lest … should", "though … yet", and "unless/until" misuse.

## What a conjunction does
A **conjunction** joins words, phrases or clauses: "Rahim **and** Karim", "slow **but** sure", "I stayed home **because** it rained."

## Kinds of conjunction
### 1. Coordinating conjunctions
Join two equal parts. Remember **FANBOYS**: **for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so**.

| Type | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cumulative** (adding) | and, as well as, both … and, not only … but also | "He is rich **as well as** generous." |
| **Adversative** (contrast) | but, yet, still, however, whereas, while | "He is poor **but** honest." |
| **Alternative** (choice) | or, either … or, neither … nor, otherwise, else | "Hurry up, **or** you will miss the bus." |
| **Illative** (result) | so, therefore, hence, for | "He was ill, **so** he did not come." |

### 2. Subordinating conjunctions
Join a dependent clause to a main clause.

| Meaning | Conjunctions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Time | when, while, before, after, since, until, till, as soon as | "Wait **till** I come." |
| Reason | because, since, as | "**As** it was late, we left." |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, in case | "**Unless** you work hard, you will fail." |
| Purpose | so that, in order that, **lest** | "Walk fast **lest** you **should** miss the train." |
| Result | so … that, such … that | "He is **so** weak **that** he cannot walk." |
| Concession | though, although, even if | "**Though** he is poor, (**yet**) he is honest." |
| Comparison | than, as … as | "He is taller **than** I." |

### 3. Correlative conjunctions (used in pairs) 🔥
| Pair | Example |
|---|---|
| **either … or** | "**Either** you **or** he must go." |
| **neither … nor** | "He is **neither** rich **nor** poor." |
| **both … and** | "**Both** Rahim **and** Karim were present." |
| **not only … but also** | "He is **not only** clever **but also** hardworking." |
| **whether … or** | "I don't know **whether** he will come **or** not." |
| **though / although … yet** | "**Though** he tried, **yet** he failed." |
| **so … that** | "The box is **so** heavy **that** I cannot lift it." |
| **such … that / such … as** | "He is **such** a fool **that** everyone laughs at him." "**Such** a man **as** he is rare." |
| **as … as / so … as** | "He is **as** tall **as** you." "He is not **so** tall **as** you." |
| **no sooner … than** 🔥 | "**No sooner** had he come **than** she left." |
| **hardly / scarcely … when / before** 🔥 | "**Hardly** had he come **when** she left." |
| **rather … than / would rather … than** | "I would **rather** die **than** beg." |
| **other … than** | "I have no **other** friend **than** you." |

## Rules exams love 🔥
1. **No sooner** takes **than** — never *when* or *then*.
2. **Hardly / scarcely / barely** take **when** or **before** — never *than*.
3. **Lest** is followed by **should** (not *not*): "Work hard **lest** you **should** fail" (not *lest you should not fail* — *lest* already means "so that … not").
4. **Unless** and **until** already contain "not" — don't add another *not*: "**Unless** you **read**, you will fail." "Wait **until** I **return**."
5. **Though** can pair with **yet**, never with *but*: "**Though** he is poor, **but** honest" is wrong.
6. **Both … and** — never *both … as well as*; "both" is not used with negatives.
7. **Parallel structure**: the words after each part of a correlative pair must be of the same kind: "He is **not only** a poet **but also** a painter" (not *not only is a poet but also a painter*).
8. **Neither … nor** (not *neither … or*); **either … or** (not *either … nor*).
9. **Because** gives the reason; don't combine with *so* in one sentence: "**Because** he was ill, he did not come" (not *because … so*).
10. **Since** (time) needs a present perfect main clause: "It **has been** two years **since** he left."
11. **Scarcely / seldom / not only** at the start → inversion: "**Not only did** he come, but he also stayed."
12. **Doubt**: "I doubt **whether/if** he will come" (positive). "I don't doubt **that** he will come" (negative).

## "Than" and "as" with pronouns
"He is older **than I** (am)." "She loves you **as much as I** (do)."

## Common mistakes
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| No sooner had he left when it rained. | No sooner had he left **than** it rained. |
| Hardly had he gone than I came. | Hardly had he gone **when** I came. |
| Though he is poor but he is honest. | Though he is poor, **(yet)** he is honest. |
| Unless you do not work, you will fail. | **Unless you work**, you will fail. |
| Neither Rahim or Karim came. | Neither Rahim **nor** Karim came. |
| Both he as well as his brother came. | Both he **and** his brother came. |
| Because he was ill, so he did not come. | Because he was ill, he did not come. |
| Walk fast lest you should not miss the bus. | Walk fast lest you **should miss** the bus. |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 **No sooner … than**; **Hardly/Scarcely … when**.
- 🔥 **lest … should**; **unless/until** without *not*.
- **Though … yet** (not *but*).
- **Neither … nor**, **either … or**, **both … and**.
- Correlatives need **parallel structure**.
- **FANBOYS** = coordinating conjunctions.
- **so … that / such … that** = result.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** "No sooner had I reached the station ___ the train left."
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) before

**2.** "Scarcely had he finished his speech ___ the audience applauded."
(a) than (b) when (c) then (d) and

**3.** "Work hard ___ you should fail."
(a) lest (b) unless (c) until (d) so that

**4.** "___ he is rich, he is not happy."
(a) Because (b) Unless (c) Although (d) Since

**5.** "He is neither intelligent ___ hardworking."
(a) or (b) and (c) but (d) nor

**6.** Which sentence is correct?
(a) Though he is poor but he is honest. (b) Unless you do not hurry, you will be late. (c) Both he as well as I went. (d) He not only sings but also dances well.

**7.** In "He is poor but honest", "but" is a/an —
(a) cumulative conjunction (b) adversative conjunction (c) illative conjunction (d) correlative conjunction

**8.** "Wait here ___ I come back."
(a) until (b) unless (c) lest (d) whether
