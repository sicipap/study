# Atmosphere layers, rocks, earthquakes

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask the composition of air, which layer has weather/ozone/radio reflection, the coldest layer, rock types with examples (granite, marble), Earth's interior, earthquake waves and the Richter scale.

## Composition of air 🔥
| Gas | Share by volume |
|---|---|
| **Nitrogen (N₂)** | **≈ 78%** |
| **Oxygen (O₂)** | **≈ 21%** |
| **Argon (Ar)** | **≈ 0.93%** (most abundant noble gas in air) |
| **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** | **≈ 0.04%** |
| Others | Neon, helium, methane, krypton, hydrogen, ozone; plus variable **water vapour** and dust |

- Standard atmospheric pressure = **1,013 millibar = 76 cm of mercury**; pressure and density fall with height. About **half the air's mass lies below ≈ 5.5 km**.

## Layers of the atmosphere (বায়ুমণ্ডলের স্তর) 🔥🔥
| Layer (from ground) | Approx. height | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Troposphere (ট্রপোমণ্ডল)** | 0 – **≈ 8 km at poles, ≈ 18 km at equator** (average ≈ 12 km) | **All weather**: clouds, rain, storms; contains most water vapour and dust. Temperature **falls ≈ 6.5 °C per km** (lapse rate). Upper boundary: **tropopause** |
| **Stratosphere (স্ট্রাটোমণ্ডল)** | ≈ 18 – **50 km** | Contains the **ozone layer** (≈ 15–35 km); temperature **rises** with height; calm, no clouds — **jet aircraft** fly in its lower part |
| **Mesosphere (মেসোমণ্ডল)** | ≈ 50 – **80/85 km** | **Coldest layer** (≈ −90 °C at the top); **meteors burn up** here |
| **Thermosphere (তাপমণ্ডল)** | ≈ 80 – 600 km | Very high temperature; contains the **ionosphere**; **aurora** (northern/southern lights) |
| **Exosphere (বহিঃমণ্ডল)** | Above ≈ 600 km | Outermost; thin, merges into space; light gases (H, He) |

- **Ionosphere** (≈ 80–400 km, mainly in the thermosphere) has charged particles that **reflect radio waves** back to Earth, allowing long-distance radio communication. Its layers include the **Kennelly–Heaviside (E) layer** and the **Appleton (F) layer**.
- **Satellites** (including the ISS at ≈ 400 km) orbit in the thermosphere/exosphere.

## Weather quick facts
- Planetary winds: **trade winds**, **westerlies**, polar easterlies. The **Coriolis effect** deflects winds to the right in the Northern Hemisphere.
- **Tropical cyclones** turn **anticlockwise** in the Northern Hemisphere; the calm centre is the **eye**. Names by region: **cyclone** (Bay of Bengal/Indian Ocean), **hurricane** (Atlantic/East Pacific), **typhoon** (West Pacific).
- In Bangladesh's maritime ports, signals run from **1 to 11**; **5–7 = danger**, **8–10 = great danger**, **11 = communication failure**.
- Highest clouds: **cirrus**; thunderstorm cloud: **cumulonimbus**.

## Interior of the Earth 🔥
| Layer | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Crust (ভূত্বক)** | Thinnest; ≈ **5–10 km under oceans**, ≈ **30–70 km under continents**. Upper continental part: **SIAL** (silicon + aluminium); ocean floor/lower part: **SIMA** (silicon + magnesium) |
| **Mantle (গুরুমণ্ডল)** | To ≈ **2,900 km**; largest by volume; source of magma |
| **Outer core** | **Liquid** iron–nickel |
| **Inner core** | **Solid** iron–nickel (**NiFe**), ≈ 5,000–6,000 °C |

- **Mohorovičić (Moho) discontinuity**: between crust and mantle. **Gutenberg discontinuity**: between mantle and core.
- Earth's mean radius ≈ **6,371 km** (equatorial ≈ 6,378 km).

## Rocks (শিলা) 🔥🔥
| Type | How formed | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Igneous (আগ্নেয় শিলা)** — "**primary rock**" | Cooling of **magma/lava** | **Granite** (cooled slowly inside Earth), **basalt** (lava on surface), gabbro, pumice |
| **Sedimentary (পাললিক শিলা)** | Layers of sediment pressed together; may contain **fossils** | **Sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate, coal**, clay |
| **Metamorphic (রূপান্তরিত শিলা)** | Existing rocks changed by **heat and pressure** | **Limestone → marble**, **sandstone → quartzite**, **shale → slate**, **granite → gneiss**, **coal → graphite**, slate → schist |

- **Fossils** and **petroleum/coal** are found in **sedimentary rocks**.
- **Bangladesh** is mostly made of **sedimentary (alluvial) deposits** of the Ganges–Brahmaputra delta. **Hard igneous rock (granite)** is mined at **Maddhapara, Dinajpur**.

## Plate tectonics and earthquakes (ভূমিকম্প) 🔥🔥
- **Alfred Wegener (1912)** proposed **continental drift**; all continents were once one supercontinent, **Pangaea**. **Plate tectonics** (1960s) explains earthquakes and volcanoes at plate boundaries.
- Bangladesh lies near the meeting of the **Indian, Eurasian and Burma plates**; the **north-eastern region (Sylhet, Mymensingh)** is the most earthquake-prone.
- **Focus (hypocentre)**: where the earthquake starts inside the Earth. **Epicentre (উপকেন্দ্র)**: point on the surface **directly above** the focus.

| Seismic wave | Features |
|---|---|
| **P-wave (primary)** | **Fastest**, arrives first; **longitudinal**; passes through solids and liquids |
| **S-wave (secondary)** | **Transverse**; **cannot pass through liquid** → proof that the **outer core is liquid** |
| **L-wave / surface waves** | Slowest; travel along the surface; **most destructive** |

- Recorded by a **seismograph**. **Richter scale (Charles Richter, 1935)** measures **magnitude**; it is **logarithmic** — each whole number = **10 times** the ground-shaking amplitude and ≈ **32 times** the energy. Scientists now use the **moment magnitude scale**. The **Mercalli scale** measures **intensity (damage)**.
- **Tsunami** (Japanese for "harbour wave") is caused mainly by **undersea earthquakes**. **Indian Ocean tsunami: 26 December 2004** (Sumatra, magnitude ≈ 9.1).
- **Pacific "Ring of Fire"** has most of the world's earthquakes and active volcanoes.
- Notable earthquakes: **Great Assam earthquake 1897** (hit this region hard); **Nepal, 25 April 2015 (M 7.8)**; **Türkiye–Syria, 6 February 2023 (M 7.8)**; **Myanmar (Mandalay region), 28 March 2025 (M 7.7)**, felt in Bangladesh.
- Volcanoes: **active, dormant, extinct**. **Vesuvius** buried **Pompeii (79 CE)**; **Krakatoa (1883)** eruption; **Mauna Loa (Hawaii)** is the largest active volcano.

## Quick revision
- Air: **N₂ 78%, O₂ 21%, Ar 0.93%, CO₂ 0.04%**.
- **Troposphere = weather; stratosphere = ozone & jet planes; mesosphere = coldest, meteors burn; thermosphere/ionosphere = radio reflection & aurora.**
- Temperature falls ≈ **6.5 °C/km** in the troposphere.
- **Granite & basalt = igneous; limestone & sandstone = sedimentary; marble & slate = metamorphic.**
- **Limestone → marble; shale → slate; sandstone → quartzite.**
- Core: iron–nickel; **outer core liquid** (S-waves stop).
- **Epicentre** is above the focus; **Richter scale** (logarithmic) measures magnitude.
- Continental drift: **Wegener**; Pangaea.

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Nitrogen ≈ 78% of air |
| 2 | a | Troposphere holds water vapour and clouds |
| 3 | c | Ozone layer lies in the stratosphere (≈15–35 km) |
| 4 | d | Charged particles of the ionosphere reflect radio waves |
| 5 | a | Metamorphism of limestone gives marble |
| 6 | c | Granite forms from slowly cooled magma |
| 7 | b | Epicentre is on the surface above the focus |
| 8 | d | S-waves are transverse and stop at the liquid outer core |
