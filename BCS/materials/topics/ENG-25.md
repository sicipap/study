# Narration

> **Why it matters:** Narration questions ask for the correct indirect form of a statement, question, command or exclamation — testing the reporting verb (asked, ordered, exclaimed), tense back-shift, pronoun change, and time/place words (now → then).

## Direct and indirect speech
- **Direct:** the speaker's exact words inside inverted commas — He said, "**I am ill.**"
- **Indirect:** the words reported in our own words — He said that **he was ill**.
- Parts: **reporting verb** (said) + **reported speech** (the words spoken).

## Rule 1: Tense change 🔥
If the reporting verb is in the **past**, the tense of the reported speech goes **one step back**:

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| present simple (**am/is/write**) | past simple (**was/wrote**) |
| present continuous (**is writing**) | past continuous (**was writing**) |
| present perfect (**has written**) | past perfect (**had written**) |
| past simple (**wrote**) | past perfect (**had written**) |
| past continuous (**was writing**) | past perfect continuous (**had been writing**) |
| **will / shall** | **would / should** |
| **can / may** | **could / might** |
| **must** | **had to** (or **must** unchanged) |

**No tense change when** 🔥:
- The reporting verb is **present or future**: He **says**, "I am ill." → He says that he **is** ill.
- The reported speech is a **universal truth / habitual fact**: The teacher said, "The earth **moves** round the sun." → The teacher said that the earth **moves** round the sun.
- Past perfect, and **could, would, should, might, ought to** stay the same.

## Rule 2: Pronoun change (SON rule)
- **1st person** (I, we, my) → changes to the person of the **Subject** of the reporting verb.
- **2nd person** (you, your) → changes to the person of the **Object** of the reporting verb.
- **3rd person** → **No** change.
- Example: He said to me, "**You** are late." → He told me that **I** was late.

## Rule 3: Words of time and place 🔥
| Direct | Indirect | Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **now** | **then** | **today** | **that day** |
| **here** | **there** | **tonight** | **that night** |
| **this** | **that** | **tomorrow** | **the next day / the following day** |
| **these** | **those** | **yesterday** | **the previous day / the day before** |
| **ago** | **before** | **last night** | **the previous night** |
| **thus** | **so** | **next week** | **the following week** |
| **come** | **go** (when place changes) | **hence** | **thence** |

## Rule 4: By type of sentence
### Assertive (statement)
**said → said**, **said to → told**; connector **that**.
- He said to me, "I will help you." → He **told** me **that** he **would** help me.

### Interrogative (question) 🔥
Reporting verb → **asked / enquired / wanted to know**; question becomes a **statement form** (no question mark); no "that".
- **Yes/No question** → **if / whether**: He said to me, "**Are** you ill?" → He asked me **if I was** ill.
- **Wh-question** → the **wh-word** is kept: She said to him, "**Where do** you live?" → She asked him **where he lived**.
- Don't write "He asked me where **did I live**".

### Imperative (command, request, advice)
Reporting verb → **ordered / commanded / told** (command), **requested** (please, kindly), **advised** (advice), **forbade** (don't); verb → **to + V1**.
- He said to me, "**Go** home." → He **ordered me to go** home.
- She said to him, "**Please** help me." → She **requested him to help** her.
- The teacher said, "**Don't** make a noise." → The teacher **forbade** us **to make** a noise. / … told us **not to make** a noise.
- **Let's** (proposal) → **proposed / suggested that … should**: He said, "**Let us** go out." → He **proposed that they should** go out.

### Optative (wish, prayer)
**wished / prayed** + that + **might**:
- She said to me, "**May** you be happy!" → She **wished that I might** be happy. — "**May Allah bless you**" → He **prayed that** Allah **might bless** me.

### Exclamatory
**exclaimed with joy / sorrow / surprise / wonder**; turn into a statement:
- He said, "**Hurrah!** We have won." → He **exclaimed with joy that** they had won.
- She said, "**Alas!** I am ruined." → She **exclaimed with sorrow that** she was ruined.
- He said, "**What a** fine day it is!" → He **exclaimed that** it was **a very fine** day.

### Special words
- "**Yes**"/"**No**" → **replied in the affirmative / negative**.
- "**Good morning**" → **wished** (someone) **good morning**; "**Thank you**" → **thanked**; "**Sir**" → **respectfully**.

## Common mistakes
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| He said me that he was ill. | He **told** me that he was ill. |
| He asked me that where I lived. | He asked me **where I lived**. |
| He asked me where did I live. | He asked me where **I lived**. |
| She told me that she will come tomorrow. | She told me that she **would** come **the next day**. |
| He ordered me that go. | He ordered me **to go**. |
| The teacher said the sun rose in the east. | … the sun **rises** in the east. |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 Past reporting verb → **one tense back**; **will → would, can → could**.
- 🔥 **Universal truth** → no tense change.
- 🔥 **now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, ago → before**.
- Question → **asked + if/whether** or **wh-word**, statement word order.
- Command → **ordered/requested/advised + to + V1**; "Don't" → **forbade / told … not to**.
- **Let's** → **proposed/suggested that … should**.
- Exclamation → **exclaimed with joy/sorrow**.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** He said to me, "I am busy now." Indirect —
(a) He said to me that he is busy now. (b) He told me that he was busy then. (c) He told me that I was busy then. (d) He told me that he had been busy now.

**2.** She said to me, "Do you like tea?" Indirect —
(a) She asked me if I liked tea. (b) She asked me do I like tea. (c) She told me that I liked tea. (d) She asked me that did I like tea.

**3.** The teacher said, "The sun rises in the east." Indirect —
(a) The teacher said that the sun rose in the east. (b) The teacher said that the sun had risen in the east. (c) The teacher said that the sun would rise in the east. (d) The teacher said that the sun rises in the east.

**4.** He said to me, "Please lend me your pen." Indirect —
(a) He ordered me to lend him my pen. (b) He requested me to lend him my pen. (c) He told me please lend him my pen. (d) He asked me that lend him my pen.

**5.** In indirect speech, "tomorrow" becomes —
(a) the next day (b) that day (c) the previous day (d) today

**6.** He said, "Let us play football." Indirect —
(a) He told us to play football. (b) He ordered that we play football. (c) He said let us play football. (d) He proposed that they should play football.

**7.** She said, "Alas! My father is dead." Indirect —
(a) She said alas that her father is dead. (b) She exclaimed with joy that her father was dead. (c) She exclaimed with sorrow that her father was dead. (d) She told that her father had dead.

**8.** He said to me, "Where are you going?" Indirect —
(a) He asked me where I was going. (b) He asked me where was I going. (c) He told me where I am going. (d) He asked me that where I was going.
