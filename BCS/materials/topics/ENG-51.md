> **Why it matters:** "The meaning of *de facto* / *sine die* / *fait accompli* is…" is a favourite of BCS, bank and judicial-service papers. Legal and diplomatic Latin and French phrases are also common in newspapers.

## Pairs that are often confused
| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **De facto / de jure** | in fact / by law |
| **Sine die / sine qua non** | adjourned without a date / essential condition |
| **In camera / in toto** | in private / completely |
| **A priori / a posteriori** | before experience / from experience |

## Quick revision
- **De facto** = in fact; **de jure** = by law.
- **Sine die** = indefinitely (adjournment); **sub judice** = under trial.
- **Prima facie** = at first sight; **bona fide** = genuine.
- **Ex officio** = by virtue of office; **in camera** = in private.
- **Status quo** = existing state; **per capita** = per head.
- **Fait accompli** = accomplished fact; **faux pas** = social blunder.
- **Coup d'état** = sudden seizure of power; **laissez-faire** = non-interference.
- **Bête noire** = object of dislike; **carte blanche** = full freedom.
