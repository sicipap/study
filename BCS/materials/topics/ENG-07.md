# Verb: finite/non-finite (gerund, participle, infinitive)

> **Why it matters:** Exams ask you to spot a gerund vs a present participle, choose "to + verb" or "verb + ing" after words like *look forward to, avoid, let, make*, and fix dangling participles.

## Finite and non-finite verbs
- A **finite verb** changes with the **subject's person and number and with tense**: "He **goes**." "They **went**." Every complete sentence needs a finite verb.
- A **non-finite verb** does **not** change with the subject or tense: **infinitive, gerund, participle**. "He likes **to swim**." "They like **to swim**."

| Non-finite | Form | Works as |
|---|---|---|
| **Infinitive** | (to) + base verb | noun, adjective, adverb |
| **Gerund** | verb + ing | **noun** |
| **Present participle** | verb + ing | **adjective** (or in continuous tenses) |
| **Past participle** | verb + ed/en | adjective, perfect tenses, passive |

Other verb types: **transitive** (takes an object: "He **wrote** a letter"), **intransitive** (no object: "The baby **sleeps**"), **auxiliary** (be, have, do) and **modal** (can, may, must, shall, will, should, would, might, ought to).

## Infinitive
- **Uses:** subject ("**To err** is human"), object ("I want **to go**"), purpose ("He came **to see** me"), after adjectives ("glad **to meet** you").
- **Bare infinitive (without "to")** 🔥 after:
  - modals: can, could, may, might, must, shall, will, should, would — "You **must go**."
  - **let, make, see, hear, watch, feel, bid, help** (active): "Let him **go**." "He made me **laugh**." "I saw him **cross** the road."
  - **had better, would rather, would sooner, need not, dare not**: "You **had better leave**."
  - But in the **passive**, "to" comes back: "He **was made to work**."
- **Split infinitive** ("to boldly go") is avoided in formal writing: "to go boldly".
- Verbs followed by the infinitive: **want, decide, hope, agree, refuse, promise, plan, wish, expect, afford, pretend, learn**.

## Gerund 🔥
A gerund is **verb + ing used as a noun**.
- Subject: "**Swimming** is good exercise."
- Object: "I enjoy **reading**."
- Object of a preposition: "He is fond of **singing**."
- **A preposition is always followed by a gerund**, never by a bare verb.

### "to" as a preposition + gerund 🔥
**look forward to, with a view to, be used to, be accustomed to, object to, confess to, in addition to, prefer … to, devoted to, given to**:
- "I **look forward to hearing** from you." (not *to hear*)
- "He came **with a view to getting** a job."
- "She **is used to working** late." (compare "He **used to work** late" = past habit).

### Verbs followed by a gerund
**avoid, enjoy, finish, mind, stop, deny, admit, practise, risk, miss, postpone, consider, can't help, it is no use, it is worth, busy**:
- "Would you **mind opening** the window?" — "I **can't help laughing**." — "The book is **worth reading**." — "**It is no use crying** over spilt milk."

### Gerund vs infinitive changes meaning
| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| He **stopped smoking**. | He gave up smoking. |
| He **stopped to smoke**. | He stopped (walking/work) in order to smoke. |
| I **remember posting** the letter. | I posted it and I remember it. |
| **Remember to post** the letter. | Don't forget to post it. |

### Possessive before a gerund
"I dislike **his** coming late" (not *him coming*). "Excuse **my** being late."

## Participle
- **Present participle** (verb + ing) as adjective: "a **running** train", "**Hearing** the noise, he ran out."
- **Past participle**: "a **broken** chair", "**Written** in haste, the letter had many mistakes."
- **Perfect participle**: "**Having finished** the work, he went home."

### Gerund or participle? 🔥
Ask: does the -ing word name an action (noun) or describe a noun (adjective)?
- "a **walking** stick" = a stick for walking → **gerund** (purpose).
- "a **walking** man" = a man who is walking → **participle**.
- "a **sleeping** baby" → participle; "a **sleeping** car/bag" → gerund.

### Dangling (unrelated) participle 🔥
The participle must refer to the **subject** of the main clause.
- Wrong: "**Walking** along the road, **a snake** bit him." (The snake was not walking.)
- Right: "**Walking** along the road, **he** was bitten by a snake."
- Wrong: "Being a rainy day, I stayed home." Right: "**It being** a rainy day, I stayed home." (**absolute** phrase with its own subject).

## Common mistakes
| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| I look forward to meet you. | I look forward to **meeting** you. |
| He made me to cry. | He made me **cry**. |
| You had better to go now. | You had better **go** now. |
| He is used to get up early. | He is used to **getting** up early. |
| I avoid to eat junk food. | I avoid **eating** junk food. |
| He insisted to go. | He insisted **on going**. |
| Let him to speak. | Let him **speak**. |

## Quick revision
- 🔥 Finite verb changes with subject/tense; **infinitive, gerund, participle** do not.
- 🔥 **look forward to / with a view to / be used to + -ing**.
- **let, make, see, hear, had better, would rather** + bare infinitive.
- Passive of make: "He **was made to** work."
- **Gerund = noun**, **participle = adjective**.
- **avoid, enjoy, mind, finish, can't help, worth** + gerund.
- A participle must agree with the subject — avoid the dangling participle.

## Practice MCQ
**1.** "I look forward to ___ from you."
(a) hear (b) heard (c) hearing (d) be heard

**2.** In "Swimming is good for health", "swimming" is a —
(a) present participle (b) gerund (c) infinitive (d) finite verb

**3.** "You had better ___ a doctor."
(a) see (b) to see (c) seeing (d) seen

**4.** Which sentence contains a finite verb?
(a) To be honest (b) Having finished the work (c) Walking in the rain (d) He writes daily

**5.** "He made me ___ the work again."
(a) to do (b) doing (c) do (d) done

**6.** In "a walking stick", "walking" is a —
(a) participle (b) infinitive (c) adverb (d) gerund

**7.** "Would you mind ___ the door?"
(a) closing (b) to close (c) close (d) closed

**8.** Which sentence is correct?
(a) He came with a view to get a job. (b) Walking along the road, a snake bit him. (c) He is accustomed to working hard. (d) I avoid to go there.

## Answer key
| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | "to" is a preposition in look forward to |
| 2 | b | -ing word used as the subject, a noun |
| 3 | a | had better + bare infinitive |
| 4 | d | "writes" changes with the subject |
| 5 | c | make + bare infinitive in the active |
| 6 | d | stick for walking: purpose, so gerund |
| 7 | a | mind + gerund |
| 8 | c | accustomed to + gerund |
