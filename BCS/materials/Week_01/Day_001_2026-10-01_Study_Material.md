## Hour 1 — Bangladesh Affairs: Ancient Bengal (প্রাচীন বাংলা)

### 1.1 The name "Bangla / Bengal"
- The word **"বঙ্গ" (Vanga)** is first mentioned in the **ঐতরেয় আরণ্যক (Aitareya Aranyaka)**.
- **আবুল ফজল**, in **আইন-ই-আকবরী**, explained "Bangal" as **বঙ্গ + আল** (আল = embankment/dyke).
- Sultan **শামসুদ্দিন ইলিয়াস শাহ** united the whole region in the 14th century and took the title **"শাহ-ই-বাঙ্গালাহ"**. From then on, the whole region was called **"বাঙ্গালাহ"**.

### 1.2 Ancient janapadas (প্রাচীন জনপদ) 🔥
In ancient times Bengal was not one country. It was divided into many small regions called **জনপদ**.

| Janapada | Present-day area | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **পুণ্ড্র (Pundra)** | Bogura, Rajshahi, Dinajpur (North Bengal) | **Oldest janapada.** Capital **পুণ্ড্রনগর = মহাস্থানগড়** (Bogura) |
| **বরেন্দ্র (Varendra)** | Bogura, Rajshahi, Dinajpur | Part of Pundra; between the Ganga and the Karatoya |
| **বঙ্গ (Vanga)** | Dhaka, Faridpur, Bikrampur area | The name "Bangladesh" comes from this |
| **গৌড় (Gauda)** | Murshidabad, Malda, Chapainawabganj | Shashanka's kingdom |
| **সমতট (Samatata)** | **Cumilla & Noakhali** | Mentioned in Samudragupta's Allahabad inscription |
| **হরিকেল (Harikela)** | Eastern Bengal (Sylhet – Chattogram) | |
| **চন্দ্রদ্বীপ (Chandradwip)** | **Barishal** | Old name of Barishal |
| **রাঢ় (Radha)** | West of the Bhagirathi (West Bengal) | |
| **তাম্রলিপ্ত (Tamralipta)** | Midnapore (West Bengal) | Famous ancient seaport |
| **শ্রীহট্ট (Srihatta)** | Sylhet | |

### 1.3 Important archaeological sites
- **মহাস্থানগড় (Bogura):** on the bank of the **করতোয়া** river. It is the **oldest city-site** in Bangladesh. The **Mahasthan Brahmi inscription** (Maurya period) found here is the oldest inscription found in Bangladesh.
- **উয়ারী-বটেশ্বর (Narsingdi):** a fort-city about **2,500 years old**. Jahangirnagar University has excavated it since 2000. It shows ancient trade links.

### 1.4 Gangaridai and Alexander
- **Alexander the Great** invaded India in **326 BCE**.
- Greek writers mention a powerful kingdom called **গঙ্গারিডই (Gangaridai)** in the Ganges delta. Its strength was one reason Alexander did not advance further east.

### 1.5 Maurya Empire (মৌর্য সাম্রাজ্য)
- Founder: **চন্দ্রগুপ্ত মৌর্য** (c. 321 BCE). Capital: **পাটলিপুত্র**.
- His adviser **কৌটিল্য (চাণক্য)** wrote **"অর্থশাস্ত্র"**.
- Greek ambassador **মেগাস্থিনিস** wrote **"ইন্ডিকা"**.
- **সম্রাট অশোক** accepted **Buddhism** after the **Kalinga War (261 BCE)**.
- North Bengal (Pundra) was part of the Maurya Empire. The Mahasthan Brahmi inscription is the proof.

### 1.6 Gupta Empire (গুপ্ত সাম্রাজ্য)
- Founder: **শ্রীগুপ্ত**. **প্রথম চন্দ্রগুপ্ত** started the Gupta era in **320 CE**.
- **সমুদ্রগুপ্ত** is called the **"Napoleon of India"**. His **Allahabad pillar inscription** (written by Harisena) names **সমতট** as a frontier kingdom.
- **দ্বিতীয় চন্দ্রগুপ্ত (বিক্রমাদিত্য):** Chinese traveller **ফা-হিয়েন** visited India in his reign.
- The Gupta period is called the **"Golden Age"** of ancient India.

### 1.7 Shashanka (শশাঙ্ক) 🔥
- The **first independent, sovereign king of Bengal (বাংলার প্রথম স্বাধীন সার্বভৌম রাজা)**.
- Kingdom: **গৌড়**. Capital: **কর্ণসুবর্ণ** (Murshidabad, West Bengal).
- His main rival was **হর্ষবর্ধন** of Thaneswar/Kanauj.
- Chinese traveller **হিউয়েন সাং** visited Bengal in the 7th century.

### 1.8 Matsyanyaya (মাৎস্যন্যায়)
- After Shashanka's death, Bengal fell into anarchy for about **100 years (mid-7th to mid-8th century)**.
- **মাৎস্যন্যায়** means "the rule of fish": big fish eat small fish, the strong oppress the weak.
- It ended when the leaders chose **গোপাল** as king (c. 750 CE). Gopala founded the **পাল বংশ**, which you will study on Saturday.

**Memory hook:** *পুণ্ড্র প্রাচীন → মৌর্য → গুপ্ত → শশাঙ্ক (প্রথম স্বাধীন) → মাৎস্যন্যায় → গোপাল (পাল)*

## Hour 2 — International Affairs: Core Concepts 🔥

### 2.1 State (রাষ্ট্র)
A state has **four elements**:
1. **Population** (জনসমষ্টি)
2. **Territory** (নির্দিষ্ট ভূখণ্ড)
3. **Government** (সরকার)
4. **Sovereignty** (সার্বভৌমত্ব). **This is the most important element.** It separates a state from other organizations.

- The **Montevideo Convention (1933)** set the legal criteria for statehood: permanent population, defined territory, government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states.
- **Nation (জাতি)** is a people united by language, culture, history and a common feeling. **State** is a political-legal unit. A nation can exist without a state. The Kurds and the Palestinians are examples.

### 2.2 Sovereignty (সার্বভৌমত্ব)
- It means supreme power, both **internal** (over everyone inside the state) and **external** (freedom from outside control).
- **Jean Bodin (জ্যাঁ বোদাঁ)** is the **father of the modern theory of sovereignty**. His book is *Six Books of the Commonwealth* (1576).
- **Thomas Hobbes** wrote ***Leviathan*** (1651): an absolute sovereign is needed to escape the "state of nature".
- **John Austin** gave the **legal (monistic) theory** of sovereignty.
- **Rousseau** gave the idea of **popular sovereignty** (*The Social Contract*, 1762).
- The **Treaty of Westphalia (1648)** ended the Thirty Years' War and started the **modern sovereign nation-state system**.

### 2.3 Geopolitics (ভূ-রাজনীতি)
It studies how geography (location, resources, seas, borders) shapes politics and power.

| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Rudolf Kjellén** (Sweden) | **First used the term "geopolitics"** (1899) |
| **Friedrich Ratzel** (Germany) | "Lebensraum" (living space); state as an organism |
| **Alfred Thayer Mahan** (USA) | **Sea-power theory**, *The Influence of Sea Power upon History* (1890) |
| **Halford Mackinder** (UK) | **Heartland theory** (1904): "Who rules the Heartland commands the World-Island…" |
| **Nicholas Spykman** (USA) | **Rimland theory**: control of the coastal rim of Eurasia is the key |

### 2.4 Balance of power (ক্ষমতার ভারসাম্য)
- Power is spread so that **no single state can dominate** the others. If one state grows too strong, others unite against it.
- Classic example: the **Congress of Vienna (1815)** and the "Concert of Europe" after Napoleon.
- World systems: **unipolar** (one superpower), **bipolar** (two, as in the Cold War), **multipolar** (many).

### 2.5 Key thinkers and terms
| Term / book | Person |
|---|---|
| ***The Prince*** (power politics, "end justifies the means") | **Niccolò Machiavelli** |
| ***Arthashastra*** | **Kautilya** |
| ***Politics Among Nations*** (1948), **classical realism** | **Hans J. Morgenthau** |
| **Neo-realism**, *Theory of International Politics* (1979) | **Kenneth Waltz** |
| **Soft power** (attraction through culture and values) | **Joseph Nye** |
| **"Iron Curtain"** speech (1946) | **Winston Churchill** |
| **Détente** | Easing of US–USSR tension in the 1970s |
| **Buffer state** | A small state between two rival powers (e.g. Nepal, Bhutan) |

## Hour 3 — Computer & ICT: History & Generations of Computers 🔥

### 3.1 Early calculating devices
| Device | Inventor / year |
|---|---|
| **Abacus** | Earliest calculating device (ancient Mesopotamia/China) |
| **Napier's Bones** | John Napier, 1617 |
| **Slide rule** | William Oughtred, 1622 |
| **Pascaline**, the **first mechanical calculator** (addition/subtraction) | **Blaise Pascal, 1642** |
| Stepped Reckoner (could multiply) | Gottfried Leibniz, 1673 |
| Punched-card loom | Joseph Marie Jacquard, 1801 |

### 3.2 Pioneers 🔥
- **Charles Babbage** is the **father of the computer**. He designed the **Difference Engine** (1822) and the **Analytical Engine** (1830s). The Analytical Engine had a "mill" (like a CPU) and a "store" (like memory).
- **Ada Lovelace** is the **first computer programmer**. She wrote an algorithm for the Analytical Engine.
- **Herman Hollerith** built a punched-card tabulating machine for the **1890 US census**. His company later became **IBM**.
- **Alan Turing** proposed the **Turing Machine** (1936). He is called the father of theoretical computer science and AI.
- **John von Neumann** gave the **stored-program concept**: program and data are kept in the same memory.
- **Howard Aiken** built the **Mark I** (1944), an electromechanical computer, at Harvard with IBM.
- **ENIAC** (1946) was the first general-purpose electronic computer. It was built by **J. Presper Eckert & John Mauchly** and used about **18,000 vacuum tubes**.
- **UNIVAC I** (1951) was the **first commercial computer**.

### 3.3 Generations of computers 🔥🔥 (most asked)
| Generation | Period | Main technology | Features / examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | 1946–1958 | **Vacuum tube** | Huge, very hot, machine language. ENIAC, EDVAC, UNIVAC I, IBM 650 |
| **2nd** | 1959–1964 | **Transistor** (invented 1947, Bell Labs) | Smaller, faster; assembly & high-level languages (**FORTRAN, COBOL**); magnetic core memory. IBM 1401, **IBM 1620** |
| **3rd** | 1965–1971 | **Integrated Circuit (IC)** (Jack Kilby & Robert Noyce) | Operating systems, keyboard, monitor. IBM System/360 |
| **4th** | 1971–present | **Microprocessor**, VLSI | Personal computers. **First microprocessor: Intel 4004 (1971)**. IBM PC (1981) |
| **5th** | Present & future | **AI**, ULSI, parallel processing | Natural-language processing, robotics, quantum computing |

**Memory hook:** *Tube → Transistor → IC → Microprocessor → AI*

### 3.4 Computers in Bangladesh
- The **first computer in Bangladesh** was an **IBM 1620** (a 2nd-generation computer). It was installed in **1964** at the **Atomic Energy Centre, Dhaka**.
- **Bijoy** Bangla keyboard: **Mustafa Jabbar** (1988). **Avro** keyboard: **Mehdi Hasan Khan** (2003).

## Hour 4 — Ethics & Governance: Ethics & Morality: Concept and Sources 🔥

### 4.1 Meaning
- **Ethics (নৈতিকতা/নীতিবিদ্যা)** comes from the Greek word **"ethos"**, meaning custom, habit or character.
- **Morality (নৈতিকতা/নীতি)** comes from the Latin word **"mores"**, meaning customs or manners.
- Ethics is the **systematic study of right and wrong, good and bad** in human conduct.
- Ethics is a **normative science (আদর্শনিষ্ঠ বিজ্ঞান)**. It studies **what ought to be**, not just what is.
- Ethics is a branch of **philosophy**.

### 4.2 Branches of ethics
| Branch | What it studies |
|---|---|
| **Meta-ethics** | The meaning and nature of moral words ("good", "right") |
| **Normative ethics** | Standards for right and wrong action (e.g. utilitarianism, Kant's duty ethics) |
| **Applied ethics** | Real-life issues: **business ethics, bioethics, environmental ethics**, professional ethics |
| **Descriptive ethics** | What people actually believe is moral |

### 4.3 Ethics vs morality
| Ethics | Morality |
|---|---|
| A theoretical, systematic study | Practical, personal belief and behaviour |
| Often set by a system or profession (code of ethics) | Comes from culture, religion and upbringing |
| More universal | Can differ from person to person and society to society |

### 4.4 Sources of ethics and morality 🔥
1. **Religion:** the strongest traditional source.
2. **Family:** the first school of morality.
3. **Society, customs and culture.**
4. **Conscience (বিবেক):** the **inner source**, often called the "inner voice".
5. **Law:** it supports morality, but it is an outer source.
6. **Education and philosophy (reason).**
7. **Literature, role models and institutions.**

### 4.5 Morality vs law
| Law | Morality |
|---|---|
| Made and **enforced by the state** | Enforced by conscience and society |
| Breaking it brings **legal punishment** (sanction) | Breaking it brings guilt or social disapproval |
| Deals mainly with **outward actions** | Deals with **both intention and action** |

### 4.6 Key thinkers (starter list, more on Thursdays ahead)
- **Socrates** is regarded as the founder of moral philosophy. He said **"Virtue is knowledge"** and taught **"Know thyself"**.
- **Aristotle** wrote ***Nicomachean Ethics***, the first systematic book on ethics. His "golden mean" says virtue is the middle path between extremes.
- **Jeremy Bentham** founded **utilitarianism**: **"the greatest happiness of the greatest number"**.
- **Immanuel Kant** founded duty-based ethics: act only by a rule you would want everyone to follow (**categorical imperative**).
